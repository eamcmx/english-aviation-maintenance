How to use Marking notes

Every answer below carries a short WHY so you can explain the point, not just tick the box. Accept the alternatives shown after a slash (/). Spelling of the specialist terms should be exact (for example release to service, sign-off, minimum equipment list). In the grammar answers the key is the pattern has/have been + past participle, not memorised sentences.

WritingModel answer · annotated

A strong response in Simplified Technical English (STE): short sentences, one idea each, approved words, and the unit's terms. Award marks for accurate terms, the present perfect passive, a legal deferral (MEL reference + time limit), and a clear airworthiness statement that does not name the worker.

Certificate of release to service statement (about 60–80 words)

The following work has been carried out to approved data. The nose-wheel tyre has been replaced on work order 5120. All task cards have been closed. The independent inspection has been completed and signed off. One item has been deferred under the MEL: the reading light at seat 12C is inoperative, category C, 10 days. A placard has been fitted. On this basis, the aircraft is airworthy and is released to service.
Why it scores: every sentence is short and holds one idea (STE). The completed work is stated in the present perfect passive — has been replaced, have been closed, has been completed, has been deferred, has been fitted — and the worker is never named. The deferral is legal because it gives the MEL, the category and the time limit, and the text closes with the airworthiness statement and "released to service".
Sentence-level checks to reward: ✓ has been / have been + participle throughout · ✓ under 18 words per sentence, one idea each · ✓ the deferral names the MEL and a time limit · ✓ ends with "airworthy" and "released to service", worker not named.
Band 5 (very good): passive throughout, STE clean, legal deferral, clear release Band 3 (adequate): meaning clear, 2–3 passive/term slips Band 1 (weak): active voice, missing MEL basis, long sentences, missing terms
