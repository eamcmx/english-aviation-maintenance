Requesting parts from stores

Request a part by number, quantity and reason · confirm interchangeability and certification · handle a backorder.

0–06 min
Outcomes & plan

By the end you can…

  • Request a part by part number, quantity and reason, precisely.
  • Confirm the interchangeability and certification status of a component.
  • Handle a shortage by negotiating an alternative or a timescale.

Today's plan

🎧 Three stores calls → 📖 vocabulary → 🔧 could/would → ✏️ exercises → 🗣 role-play a backorder → ✍️ a request note.

Grammar spine: Could / Would you + base verb.

The scenario

An AOG on the A320 line

A hydraulic filter has failed on test. The aircraft cannot depart. You telephone stores — you must give the part number, the quantity and the reason, and ask politely. Then the part turns out to be on backorder. Now you must find a safe alternative and agree a time.

Key idea: a stores request is teamwork, not an order. That is why English uses could and would.

06–24 min · Listening
Dialogue 1

Request by number, quantity, reason

ACould you confirm the stock for me, please?

BCould you provide the exact part number first?

AThe part number is 2-6-1-1-4-5-0-1.

BHow many units do you require?

AA quantity of two — one to fit, one spare.

Listen for

  • the part number, read digit by digit
  • the quantity and the reason
  • the papers: serviceable tag + EASA Form 1
  • the requisition number R-7742
Dialogue 2

Interchangeability & certification

ACould you confirm the interchangeability?

BLet me open the IPC for the cross-reference.

BConfirmed, provided that the mod status matches.

ACould you confirm the certification status?

BValid — it carries a current EASA Form 1.

The chain of proof

IPC cross-reference → mod status matches → valid Form 1 → batch number B-228 → shelf life good. Only then is the part safe to fit.

Dialogue 3 · headline

Handling a backorder

BI am sorry, but that valve is on backorder.

AThe aircraft is AOG — could you offer an alternative?

BThere is an alternative, and interchangeability is confirmed.

BCould you raise a new requisition?

Negotiation moves

  1. Name the pressure — AOG.
  2. Ask for a way out — an alternative.
  3. Check it is safe — interchangeability + Form 1.
  4. Agree a fast time.

36–50 min · Grammar
Grammar focus

Polite requests: could / would

Could / Would + you + base verb ( + please )

  • Could you check the stock? — no to
  • Would you send it to the line? — modal first
  • Could you possibly confirm it? — softer still
Latvian & Russian speakers

Three predictable mistakes

✗ Could you to check the stock?✓ Could you check the stock?No to after a modal — base verb.
✗ Could you confirms the Form 1?✓ Could you confirm the Form 1?No -s after a modal.
✗ You could send me two, please?✓ Could you send me two, please?Modal first — not statement order.

Before you fit an alternative

Is it safe? All three must be green

  • Mod status matches on aircraft and part
  • Valid Form 1 (certification status)
  • Serviceable tag present on the box

Interchangeability is confirmed in the IPC cross-reference — then, and only then, fit it.

Negotiating time

How long is too long?

  • Today / 1 day — acceptable, fit it.
  • 2–3 days — OK only with a safe alternative.
  • 10 days — far too long for an AOG.

Language: Ten days is too long. · Could you offer an alternative? · as soon as possible

Hearing the figures

Part-number read-back

On the phone, one wrong digit is the wrong part. Practise until the read-back is automatic.

Generate a number, say it as words (two-six-one…), then type the digits.

Role-play · four cards

On the phone to stores

Card 1 — Technician

Request a filter: part number, quantity, reason. Ask with could/would.

Card 2 — Technician

Check an alternative: interchangeability, Form 1, batch, shelf life.

Card 3 — Technician (AOG)

Part on backorder — ask for an alternative or a shorter time.

Card 4 — Stores

Stock is zero — give the news politely, offer a way out.

82–90 min · Writing & wrap
Writing · Simplified Technical English

A parts request note

Write 80–110 words to request a filter for an AOG A320. Give the part number, quantity and reason, then ask stores to check stock and send it.

  • Short sentences, simple words, one idea per line.
  • Every request with could or would.
  • Modal + base verb — no to, no -s.

STE habits

One instruction per line · active voice · same term every time · a number is a figure · ask, do not order.
