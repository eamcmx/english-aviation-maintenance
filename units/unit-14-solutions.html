E6Error-spotting — find and fix the mistakes

#Corrected clauseWhy
1The AD must be done by Friday.No to after must; passive is must + be + done.
2The inspection must be done before the deadline.Passive obligation needs be.
3We had to comply last month.must has no past form; use had to for past obligation.
4You must not fly after the deadline.Word order — the negative is must not, not "not must".
5The operator shall report compliance.No to after shall; use the base verb.
6… so we need not repeat it. (or: do not have to)Duty ended → no obligation. must not would wrongly ban the repeat.
7The pin must be replaced within two years.Passive needs the past participle replaced, not replace.
8Must we plan the work today?Question form of must; no to before plan.

WRITEModel writing answer — annotated

Task: a 90–120 word briefing note to planning about AD 2026-0147 — action, effective date, compliance deadline, grounding risk; use must and shall; Simplified Technical English.

Subject: AD 2026-0147 — compliance plan needed, A320 fleet To: Planning Department A new airworthiness directive, AD 2026-0147, is now in force. The effective date is 22 August 2026. The AD is mandatory. It orders a recurring inspection of the main landing gear pivot pin. Two aircraft are affected: YL-BAK and YL-BAM. The first inspection must be done within 500 flight cycles. For YL-BAK, this falls on 05 September 2026. Please book a slot before that date. We must not miss the compliance deadline. Non-compliance will ground the aircraft. After the check, the technician shall make a logbook entry. Thank you. K. Berzins, Base Engineer
Why this answer scores well
  • Task coverage: states the action (recurring inspection of the pivot pin), the effective date (22 Aug), the compliance deadline (500 FC / 05 Sep for YL-BAK) and the grounding risk — all four required points.
  • Grammar focus: uses must be done (passive obligation), must not miss (prohibition) and shall make (written-legal). Correct, varied, and none with a stray to.
  • Simplified Technical English: one idea per line; short sentences (all ≤ 18 words); the active voice where natural; the same name every time — "the main landing gear pivot pin", never "it" or "the part".
  • Register: a real memo shape — subject line, addressee, clear action request ("Please book a slot"), sign-off. A planner can act on it in seconds.
  • Common learner slips to mark down: "must to be done" (extra to); "must done" (missing be); "must not repeat" where "need not" is meant; a vague verb ("the pin should be looked at") that hides the obligation.
