Reporting inspection findings: borescope and visual.

A borescope inspection of an engine turbine finds a crack. You describe the finding to your lead, its type, size, location and severity, then you compare it against the manual's serviceable limits and recommend accept, repair or refer. All of it in clear spoken B2 English, using comparatives to judge each finding against the limit.

ScenarioReport a borescope finding to the lead VocabularyInspection findings, 22 terms GrammarComparatives for limits (longer than allowed) Class length90 minutes · balanced skills

By the end of this unit you can …

  • Can describe a defect's type, size, location and severity precisely.
  • Can compare a finding against the manual's serviceable limits.
  • Can recommend accept, repair or refer, with justification.

01The situation

It is afternoon in the maintenance hangar at Riga. An Airbus A320, registration YL-BBX, has arrived for a scheduled inspection. The work order requires a borescope inspection of the number-two engine. Because you cannot dismantle the engine, you examine the internal components with the borescope camera instead.

You are the technician performing the inspection. On the display you notice a crack on a turbine blade, so you immediately have a reportable finding. First you describe the finding precisely to your lead: its type, its size, its location and its severity. Afterwards you both consult the maintenance manual and compare each finding against the published serviceable limits. Finally you recommend an appropriate action: accept it, repair it, or refer it to engineering. You must always justify the decision clearly.

In plain words: four ideas before you start

Borescope, a small camera on a flexible probe that looks inside an engine. Finding, anything you notice in an inspection that may be a defect. Serviceable limit, the largest damage the manual allows for a part to stay in use. Comparatives, the grammar you use to compare a finding with the limit: longer than, deeper than, smaller than. Every hard word is glossed in the vocabulary bank.

Dialogue 1: Describe the borescope finding

A = Jānis, technician · B = Elīna, lead technician

Transcript

JānisElīna, I have finished the borescope inspection of engine 2, and I have one finding.

ElīnaGood work, Jānis. Give me the type of defect first, and then the location.

JānisIt is a crack. The crack is on the leading edge of a turbine blade.

ElīnaWhich stage is it, and which blade number? Be precise, because the report must be exact.

JānisIt is stage 1, blade number 12. The crack starts near the blade tip.

ElīnaUnderstood. Now give me the dimension of the crack. How long is it on the screen?

JānisThe borescope measuring tool reads it at about 4 mm along the edge.

Elīna4 mm on the leading edge. And the severity, does the crack look deep to you?

JānisThe crack looks shallow, although the line is very clear on the screen.

ElīnaIs there any other defect near it, such as pitting or light erosion?

JānisYes. There is light erosion on the same blade, close to the tip.

ElīnaThat is a good report. Type, location, dimension and severity, that is exactly what I need.

JānisI also saw a small dent on a fan blade during the visual inspection.

ElīnaWe record every finding, although some are minor. Note the dent as well.

JānisThe dent is small, so I will give you the exact dimension in a moment.

ElīnaPerfect. Next, we compare each finding against the serviceable limits in the manual.

Dialogue 2: Compare the finding against the serviceable limits

A = Jānis, technician · B = Elīna, lead technician

Transcript

ElīnaOpen the maintenance manual at chapter 72, and find the damage limits table.

JānisI have the table here. It lists the serviceable limit for each type of defect.

ElīnaStart with the crack. What is the limit for a crack on the leading edge?

JānisThe maintenance manual allows 3 mm for a crack on the leading edge.

ElīnaAnd our crack is 4 mm, so the crack is longer than the limit.

JānisYes. 4 mm is longer than 3, so the crack is out of limits.

ElīnaCorrect. A crack that is longer than allowed is not serviceable, so we cannot leave it.

JānisNow the dent. The limit for a fan-blade dent is 2 mm.

ElīnaOur dent is 1.5 mm. Is that dimension within limits?

Jānis1.5 is shallower than 2, so the dent is within limits.

ElīnaGood. The dent is smaller than the limit, so we can accept it as it is.

JānisThe crack is the real problem, because it is bigger than the manual allows.

ElīnaExactly. Always compare the measured dimension against the serviceable limit before you decide.

JānisShould I write out of limits next to the crack on the report?

ElīnaYes. Write it clearly, so the next action is obvious to the whole team.

Dialogue 3: Recommend accept, repair or refer

A = Jānis, technician · B = Elīna, lead technician

Transcript

ElīnaNow the visual inspection findings on the wing access panel. What did you see there?

JānisI found corrosion on the panel, near the fastener holes on the lower edge.

ElīnaIs it light corrosion, or is there deep pitting in the metal surface?

JānisIt is light. The pitting is shallow, and the affected area is quite small.

ElīnaGood. For light corrosion we can blend the surface, as long as we stay within limits.

JānisSo we repair it by blending, and then we protect the surface with primer again.

ElīnaCorrect. A blend repair is acceptable, provided the final depth stays within limits.

JānisThere is also a scratch on the same panel, and a very small dent beside it.

ElīnaHow deep is the scratch, compared with the limit in the maintenance manual?

JānisThe scratch is 0.1 mm, and the limit is 0.2.

ElīnaSo the scratch is shallower than the limit. We accept it, then, and record it.

JānisAgreed. But the turbine crack is out of limits, so we cannot simply accept that one.

ElīnaFor the crack we refer the finding to engineering, because they decide the repair.

JānisSo the plan is: accept the scratch, blend the corrosion, and refer the crack.

ElīnaExactly. Accept, repair or refer, and always write the reason for each decision.

JānisUnderstood. I will write the recommendation and the reason next to every finding.

04Grammar in context, comparatives for limits

You evaluate every finding against a limit, so you continually compare two measurements: the finding and the limit. English expresses this comparison with the comparative. For a short adjective, add -er and afterwards than: long → longer than. For a longer adjective, use more and afterwards than: severe → more severe than. Listen carefully to how Jānis and Elīna apply it.

Elīna: "Our crack is 4 mm, so the crack is longer than the limit."

Jānis: "1.5 is shallower than 2, so the dent is within limits."

Elīna: "The dent is smaller than the limit, so we can accept it."

Jānis: "The crack is bigger than the manual allows."

The underlying pattern is always identical: comparative + than + the limit. Whenever the finding is bigger than the limit, it is consequently out of limits. Whenever the finding is smaller than the limit, it is consequently within limits. Alternatively, you can say the finding is not as deep as the limit, which similarly means it remains within limits.

AdjectiveComparativeExample at work
longlonger thanThe crack is longer than the limit, so it is out of limits.
deepdeeper thanThe pit is deeper than allowed, so we cannot accept it.
smallsmaller thanThe dent is smaller than the limit, so it is within limits.
severemore severe thanThis finding is more severe than the last one.

Two particular points deserve your attention. Firstly, always use than after the comparative, never as, from or then. Secondly, never combine more with an -er word: write longer than, never more longer than. These two mistakes are unfortunately the most frequent at your level.

Three mistakes to avoid (Latvian & Russian speakers)

Why it happensWrongRight
You add "more" to a short adjective that already has "-er", so you double the comparative.The crack is more longer than the limit.The crack is longer than the limit.
Your first language uses a word like "as" or "from" to compare, so you drop "than".The dent is deeper as the limit.The dent is deeper than the limit.
"Than" and "then" sound the same, so you write the time word by mistake.The pit is deeper then allowed.The pit is deeper than allowed.

E7 · Reading 2 Genre: Maintenance manual, damage limits (AMM)

Read the limits page, then answer

MAINTENANCE MANUAL, DAMAGE LIMITS (ATA 72-00)

This chapter gives the serviceable limits for turbine and fan blades. Compare every finding against the limit before you decide.

Cracks: a crack on the leading edge is acceptable up to 3 mm. A crack that is longer than 3 mm is out of limits, and you must refer it to engineering.

Dents: a dent on a fan blade is acceptable up to 2 mm deep. A dent that is deeper than 2 mm is out of limits.

Scratches: a scratch is acceptable up to 0.2 mm deep. A scratch that is shallower than 0.2 mm is within limits, so you may accept it.

Corrosion: light corrosion may be removed by blending, provided the final depth stays within the blend limit. Deep pitting is out of limits.

Note: always record the dimension of each finding. Never estimate the size by feel, because a small error can change the decision. If a finding is out of limits, do not accept it. Refer it, so engineering can decide the repair.

07Writing, a borescope finding report

Write a short finding report for the technical records. Use Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100): one idea per sentence, short sentences, and the active voice. When you compare the finding with the limit, use a comparative with than. End with a clear recommendation and a reason.

Task

A borescope inspection of a combustion chamber finds a crack. The crack is on the leading edge of a liner panel. Its dimension is 5 mm. The serviceable limit in the maintenance manual is 4 mm. During the same check, a visual inspection finds light corrosion on a nearby bracket; the corrosion is within the blend limit. Write the report. Cover: the tool used; the type, location, dimension and severity of each finding; the comparison against the serviceable limit (use a comparative); and your recommendation (accept, repair or refer) with a reason. Write 100–130 words.

Show a model answer

BORESCOPE INSPECTION REPORT, COMBUSTION CHAMBER

Inspection: borescope inspection of the combustion chamber.

Finding 1: a crack on the leading edge of a liner panel. Dimension: 5 mm. Severity: clear and open.

Assessment: the serviceable limit is 4 mm. The crack is longer than the limit, so it is out of limits.

Recommendation: refer the crack to engineering, because it is out of limits.

Finding 2: light corrosion on a nearby bracket, found by visual inspection. The pitting is shallow.

Assessment: the corrosion is within the blend limit.

Recommendation: repair it by blending, then protect the surface. Accept it after the blend.

Status: engine not released until engineering answers on Finding 1.
