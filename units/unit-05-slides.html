§1 · Listening · Dialogue 1 · the board overview

Report the finished aircraft

A: Aircraft one is the A320 on stand twelve, and I have completed the left engine oil change.

B: Did you run the leak check afterwards, or is it still outstanding?

A: I ran the leak check at fifteen hundred, and it was dry, so the task is closed.

Gist first: How many aircraft? Which one is finished?

Listen for the move: A reports, B asks status.

Connectors to catch: becausealthoughas soon as — the B2 glue inside short sentences.

§1 · Listening · Dialogue 2 · the deferred defect

One open defect — but the aircraft still flies

A: I applied the MEL and fitted a placard, so the item is carried over to you.

B: How long is the aircraft allowed to stay deferred like this?

A: The MEL gives us a category C, so we have ten days to rectify it.

Gist first: Can it still fly? Why?

  • deferred = put off to fix later, with authority
  • outstanding = simply not done yet

A deferred defect stays open until somebody actually fixes it — you never sign it off to look tidy.

§1 · Listening · Dialogue 3 · mid-task avionics

Report work in progress — and the next step

A: Task card thirty-four forty-one oh-five, and I have finished steps one to four already.

B: So steps five and six are still outstanding, if I understand you correctly?

A: Correct — I simply ran out of shift time. Follow the AMM diagram and troubleshoot the wiring loom, then run the BITE test again.

Gist first: What is finished? What is left?

Catch the figures: task card 34-41-05 · connector checked at 16:30.

The borescope is not needed — the radar fault is purely electrical.

§3 · Interactive · rAF · the tense timeline

Present perfect vs past simple — where does it sit?

Present perfect = done and it matters now. Past simple = a finished detail with a clear past time.

Signals: already, just, yet, so far → perfect · at 15:00, yesterday, then → past simple.

§3 · Grammar · the three L1 traps

Latvian & Russian: fix these three by ear

✗ I already signed the card.  →  ✓ I have already signed the card.
'already' needs the present perfect.

✗ I have closed the defect at 14:00.  →  ✓ I closed the defect at 14:00.
a clear past time needs the past simple.

✗ Did you finish the borescope yet?  →  ✓ Have you finished the borescope yet?
'yet' asks about now — don't drop 'have'.

§6 · Writing · Simplified Technical English

Write the handover note (90–120 words)

STE rules: short sentences · one idea per line · simple approved words · active voice · one word for one thing.

Call a placard a placard every time — no synonyms.

Cover all three:

  • A1 — completed & serviceable
  • A2 — deferred defect on the MEL (not safety-critical)
  • A3 — mid-task, outstanding steps, next action

have completed… (done, relevant) · ran the check at 15:00 (finished time).
