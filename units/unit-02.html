01The situation

You are working in a pair on a Part-145 line. One mechanic reads the task card aloud, while the other performs the work and confirms each step out loud.

The task card is the legal record of the maintenance job, so every step must be completed in the correct order. Before anyone signs, the work must be complete, correct, and verified against the reference. Although the sequence looks simple, a missing article or a misread instruction can produce a serious error. Whenever a step is unclear, you stop immediately and query it, because you never sign off work that you cannot personally guarantee. In this unit you practise the walkthrough itself: read an instruction, perform it, confirm completion, and challenge anything that appears inconsistent with the documentation.

Reading a task card is therefore a language skill as much as a technical one. You interpret the sequence, decode the warning conventions, and confirm each action in precise, unambiguous English. Because the certifying signature carries legal weight, the vocabulary in this unit is not decoration; it is the exact language that keeps an aircraft airworthy. Learn it thoroughly, and rehearse each instruction aloud until the imperative forms feel automatic under pressure.

Four conditions before you sign off

  • Every step of the card is complete, and the steps were performed in order.
  • The work matches the reference, so the AMM value and the procedure agree.
  • Any defect you found is recorded in the tech log, and it is not signed off.
  • Your entry is legible, dated, and stamped with your personal authorisation.

02Listen: three walkthroughs

Listen first, and do not read the transcript yet. Afterwards, listen a second time and open the transcript to verify what you understood.

Dialogue 1: At the bench, reading the card together

A reads the task card, while B performs the work and confirms each step.
Transcript

ARight, task card 32-41-05 is ready. Before we begin the procedure, confirm that you are prepared.

BPrepared. I will confirm every step aloud, so you read each instruction and I verify it.

AStep one: open the work order and check the reference, which is AMM 32-41-05.

BThe work order is open, and the reference matches the AMM exactly. Step one is complete.

AStep two: remove the access panel carefully, because eight fasteners secure it to the structure.

BThe access panel is removed, and all eight fasteners are collected safely in the tray.

AObserve the WARNING here: do not touch the connector while electrical power remains applied.

BUnderstood. The power is isolated and tagged, so the connector is safe to handle now.

AStep three: inspect the bracket for cracks, and examine the weld carefully under magnification.

BOne question, though. The instruction says inspect, but which inspection lamp should I select?

AUse the borescope lamp, because the AMM note specifies a high-intensity light source.

BUnderstood. Inspecting the bracket now, there are no cracks, and the weld appears sound.

AStep four: torque the two bolts to 25 newton metres using the calibrated torque wrench.

BBoth bolts are torqued correctly to 25. The torque wrench clicked twice, confirming the value.

AStep five: fit new lockwire on the bolts, and never reuse the original wire.

BNew lockwire is fitted, and the old wire is discarded. Now we can proceed to sign off.

The three conventions at a glance

A WARNING protects people, a CAUTION protects the equipment, and a NOTE simply helps you work correctly. If you ever confuse them, apply the strictest reading and consult your supervisor. On a task card the boxes always appear immediately before the step they govern, so read ahead before you act, and never treat a WARNING as optional advice.

Dialogue 2: WARNING, CAUTION or NOTE?

A trainee asks how to interpret the warning boxes. Together they also discover a defect.
Transcript

ACould you help me read this task card? I am uncertain about the warning symbols.

BCertainly. Examine the boxes carefully, each one is a WARNING, a CAUTION, or a NOTE.

AWhat is the difference between them? On the printed page they appear quite similar.

BA WARNING indicates a risk to people, so if you ignore it, someone could be injured.

AAnd the CAUTION box? The card displays a CAUTION immediately before step six.

BA CAUTION indicates a risk to the component, so ignoring it can damage expensive equipment.

ASo the NOTE is less serious? There is a NOTE about the required torque value.

BCorrect. A NOTE provides helpful information only, and it is never a safety instruction.

AUnderstood. The CAUTION says do not overtighten the fitting, so I will proceed slowly.

BExactly. Use the torque wrench and stop precisely at the value; never estimate the torque.

AWhile I inspect the hydraulic line, I notice a small crack. Is that a defect?

BYes, that is a defect, so do not sign off the step until we record it.

AWhere should I document the defect, in the work order, or in the tech log?

BRaise it in the tech log; afterwards we open a separate task card for the repair.

AUnderstood. I am recording the defect in the tech log, and my handwriting must be legible.

BPrecisely. Unless the entry is legible, another mechanic cannot read it, so please print clearly.

Dialogue 3: Line station, installing an avionics unit

Two avionics technicians install a control unit and verify the conditions before sign off.
Transcript

ANew task card for the avionics bay: today we install the replacement control unit.

BGood. The AMM is open on the tablet, and the reference is AMM 34-21-00.

AStep one: remove the old unit. First disconnect the two electrical plugs, then the bracket.

BBoth plugs are disconnected and the old unit is out; I am placing it on the mat.

AObserve the CAUTION here: do not bend the connector pins during installation.

BNoted. I will align the unit carefully, because the pins are fragile although they look robust.

AStep two: install the new unit. Seat it fully, then fit the four fasteners.

BThe new unit is installed and all four fasteners are hand-tight, so it is ready for torque.

AStep three: torque the fasteners to 8 newton metres, following the specified cross pattern.

BTorqued to 8 in the cross pattern, and the torque wrench reading confirms the value.

AObserve the NOTE: a functional test is required before we close the access panel.

BUnderstood. Running the built-in test now, the screen reports no defect, so everything is serviceable.

AGood result. Refit the access panel, fit all eight fasteners, and verify the seal is seated.

BThe access panel is refitted and the seal is seated, so the work order is nearly complete.

AFinal step: sign off each task on the card, then write the date and stamp it.

BSigned off and stamped. My entry is legible, and I am updating the tech log accordingly.

04Grammar in context: the imperative

A task card commands one discrete action at a time, so it uses the imperative. The verb comes first, and the subject disappears entirely. This is the grammar of every procedural step.

Look at these lines from the dialogues, because each one begins with the base verb:

"Remove the access panel." · "Torque the two bolts to 25 newton metres." · "Do not reuse the original wire."

The documentation never says "you should remove" or "you must torque"; instead it issues a direct instruction. For a negative step, it uses do not followed by the base verb. To indicate the correct order, it adds a signpost such as first, then, or next, or simply a step number. Notice that the imperative sounds efficient rather than rude, because the situation is professional and the priority is clarity.

The form

PurposeFormExample from a card
Positive commandbase verb + objectRemove the access panel.
Negative commanddo not + base verbDo not reuse the lockwire.
Show the orderfirst / then / next + commandFirst disconnect the plugs, then the bracket.
Condition before commandif / when + clause, commandIf the play is excessive, record a defect.

Three mistakes to avoid

Latvian and Russian have no articles, and they build the negative instruction differently. Consequently these three errors are extremely common among speakers of both languages. Study the correction carefully.

WrongRightWhy
Remove access panel first.Remove the access panel first.English requires the definite article the.
Not touch the connector.Do not touch the connector.A negative instruction needs the auxiliary do not.
You should to inspect the seal.Inspect the seal.A procedural step uses the bare verb, without a subject.

One further habit matters for aviation English. The imperative pairs naturally with Simplified Technical English, the controlled writing standard used across maintenance documentation. Both prefer one instruction per sentence, an approved verb, and no unnecessary words. When you write your own steps later in this unit, keep every line short and direct, exactly as a genuine task card would present it.

06Speaking: role-play

Work with a partner and use the useful phrases provided. Read a step, confirm it, and query anything unclear before sign off.

Swap roles after each card, so that both of you practise reading the instruction and confirming the work. Speak slowly and clearly, exactly as you would in a noisy hangar where a misheard number can cause a real defect.

Card 1: The bench walkthrough

Situation: You are at the bench with a task card for a panel job.

Your role: You read the task card aloud, one step at a time.

Your goal: Read each step clearly, and wait for your partner to confirm before you continue.

Useful phrases: "Step one: remove the access panel." · "Is that step complete?" · "Confirm before we continue."

Card 2: The confirming mechanic

Situation: Your partner reads the steps aloud while you perform the work.

Your role: You confirm each completed step, or you query it immediately.

Your goal: Confirm the steps you understand, and ask about any instruction that is unclear.

Useful phrases: "Step two is complete." · "Wait, one question." · "Which reference should I use?"

Card 3: A defect before sign off

Situation: You inspect a bracket and discover a small crack near the weld.

Your role: You report the defect to the certifying mechanic.

Your goal: Describe the defect precisely, and explain why you cannot sign off the step.

Useful phrases: "I found a defect." · "There is a small crack near the weld." · "I cannot sign off this step."

Card 4: Reading the warning boxes

Situation: A new trainee is uncertain about the WARNING, CAUTION and NOTE boxes.

Your role: You are the experienced mechanic, and you explain the difference.

Your goal: Explain each box in simple words, and give one clear example of each.

Useful phrases: "A WARNING is a risk to people." · "A CAUTION is a risk to the part." · "A NOTE is advice only."

07Writing: a tech-log defect entry

Write a short tech-log entry for a defect you have discovered. Use Simplified Technical English: short sentences, one instruction each, the base verb, and clear approved words.

Task: During Step 3 of task card 27-51-11 you discover a 4 mm crack on the flap track. Write a tech-log defect entry of four to six lines. State clearly what you found, where you found it, and what must happen before anyone signs off.

Remember your reader. Another mechanic, or an inspector, must understand your entry months later without asking you anything. So write plainly, state only the facts, and never hide a defect to save time or to close a job faster.

Model answer
Task card 27-51-11, Step 3. Reference AMM 27-51-11.
Defect found on the flap track. There is a 4 mm crack near the weld.
The crack exceeds the limit. Do not sign off this step.
Raise a repair task card. Inspect the track again after the repair.
Entry made by: [name and stamp]. Keep this entry legible.

Self-check: tick before you submit

  • I used the base verb for each command, with the where needed.
  • I named the task card, the step, and the reference.
  • My entry is legible, and it states why I did not sign off.
