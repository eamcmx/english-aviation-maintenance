The dispatch decision under pressure

A defect appears near departure. Combine the MEL, an airworthiness directive and the defect into one compliant decision — then relay it from voice to voice and record the deferral.

Listening · Vocabulary · Grammar (reported speech) · Speaking · Writing  ·  90 minutes

By the end · how the class runs

Three outcomes for the simulation

1 · Decide. Combine the minimum equipment list, the airworthiness directive and the defect into one dispatch decision.

2 · Justify. Explain the decision clearly to operations and the crew.

3 · Document. Record the deferral and the procedures applied.

Four ideas first: MEL AD repair category reported speech

Every hard word is glossed on first use and lives in the vocabulary bank. Instructions to learners stay short; the professional density is in the dialogues.

§1 · the situation

Stand nine, and the clock is running

Evening at Riga. An A320, reg YL-BBX, on stand nine. The crew wrote up a defect: the centre-tank fuel indication reads zero — inoperative.

You are the certifying engineer. You cannot decide alone. The decision passes from voice to voice.

Three sources to combine:

🛠 the defect (fuel indication INOP)

📘 the MEL (may we defer it?)

📋 an open AD on the fuel pumps (are we in compliance?)

§1 · voice to voice

The decision is relayed — so we report speech

Engineer ↔ planner  →  engineer → captain  →  engineer → next shift

Every arrow is a moment of reported speech. "The planner said the item was deferred." "They told me the inspection had been done." "She asked whether the AD was valid." Listen for the relay, not only the decision.

§2 · Interactive · the turnaround clock

Dispatch happens against a running clock

15:00 to departure

Teacher prop — start it and let it run through the listening.

Fifteen minutes to push-back. The decision must be compliant and quick.

Under five minutes the clock turns red — the moment when a clear relayed message matters most.

§2 · Listening · Dialogue 1

Engineer & planner — combine the facts

ANNA The centre-tank fuel indication read zero. It is inoperative.
MARK Have you checked the minimum equipment list?
ANNA Yes. It is a repair category C item.
MARK The records office said the inspection had been done on Monday.
MARK The MEL adds a restriction. The centre tank must stay empty.

Listen for:

  • repair category & interval
  • the proviso (confirm fuel how?)
  • the AD compliance status
  • the restriction the MEL adds

First play: gist. Second play: detail. Transcript hidden until after.

§2 · Listening · Dialogues 2 & 3

Justify to the captain, then document

Dialogue 2 — justify.

ANNA The planner said we could defer it under the MEL.
ANNA They said the inspection had been done, and we were in compliance.
CAPTAIN I am happy to accept the aircraft with that restriction.

Dialogue 3 — document.

MARK The deferral reference is delta-nine-one-one.
ANNA I will note the aircraft is in compliance, with the threshold recorded.
ANNA I am signing the certificate of release to service now.

Every relayed line is reported speech — the grammar focus in action.

§2 · Interactive · the dispatch decision

All three gates must pass to dispatch

Decision

Set the switches to match Dialogue 1.

A defect alone is not the decision. You combine three sources and only then say GO or NO-GO.

Flip the AD switch off: the decision becomes no-go — and someone must relay that.

§4 · Grammar · reported speech

The backshift — step one tense back

"The item is deferred." → The planner said the item was deferred.

"We can defer it." → …said we could defer it.

"The tank must stay empty." → Mark told me it had to stay empty.

Backshift map

is → was
can → could
must → had to
will → would
has done → had done

§4 · Interactive · backshift stepper

Watch the tense step back

Reported form

Pick a line and press "Report it".

The reporting verb is past (said, told), so the reported verb steps back one tense.

Cold-call after a few: "Report this — The slot is held."

§4 · Grammar · say/tell & questions

Two rules, and the errors to expect

say vs tell

✗ He said me the tank was empty.

✓ He told me the tank was empty.

tell + person · say + no person

reported questions

✗ She asked can we dispatch.

✓ She asked whether we could dispatch.

whether/if + statement order, no "do/can"

Also watch: dropped articles (accept the aircraft) and time shift (tomorrow → the next day). See the key, §2.

§5 · Interactive · confirm the fuel

Meet the proviso — drip-stick check

Load sheet: 7.2 t on the wings
drip: 0.0 t

Status

Set the drip-stick reading to match the load sheet.

The proviso says confirm the fuel by hand. The record must say how it was confirmed.

"I confirmed it with the drip sticks. The figure matches the load sheet."

§6 · Speaking · role-plays

Four cards — relay the decision aloud

Card 1 engineer + planner combine the facts (hydraulic pump + AD)

Card 2 justify the deferral to the captain

Card 3 relay the captain's questions ("She asked whether…")

Card 4 shift handover — the whole decision

Swap roles after each card. Assess with the B2 rubric: range · accuracy · fluency · interaction · coherence.

§7 · Writing · STE dispatch record

Write the deferral — in Simplified Technical English

A left brake-temperature indication is inoperative (MEL cat C, 10-day interval). AD in compliance, next threshold 60 hours. Restriction: lower max taxi speed.

State: defect · MEL ref · AD compliance · restriction · placard · sign-off. 90–120 words.

STE habits

  • short sentences (< ~20 words)
  • one instruction per sentence
  • active voice, approved words
  • no long strings of nouns
§8 · wrap-up

Hand it over — the whole decision, relayed

"I deferred the item under the MEL. The records office said the inspection had been done. The captain said she was happy to accept it. I signed the release. The reference is delta-nine-one-one."

Decide · justify · document — the shape of every real dispatch.
