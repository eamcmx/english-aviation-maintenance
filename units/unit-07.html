Requesting parts from stores

On the line and in the hangar, most jobs stop until the correct component arrives, so you must telephone stores, request a part politely and precisely, and keep the job moving whenever the part is unavailable.

Unit7 · Requesting parts from stores FunctionRequest, confirm, and negotiate a part GrammarPolite requests with could / would AI stageConsultant · conversation partner

By the end of this unit you can …

  • Can request a part by part number, quantity and reason, precisely.
  • Can confirm the interchangeability and certification status of a component.
  • Can handle a shortage by negotiating an alternative or a timescale.

00The scenario

You are a technician on the A320 line, and a filter has just failed on test, so the aircraft cannot depart until you replace the component. You telephone stores immediately, because you must provide the part number, the quantity and the reason, and you must request politely. Then the situation deteriorates, because the component you require is on backorder. Now you must confirm a safe alternative, verify its documentation, and negotiate a timescale that you can both accept.

Stores requests are cooperative, not commanding, because the colleague in stores is assisting you and is not receiving your orders. For that reason you request with could and would, so the enquiry resembles teamwork rather than pressure. Listen to each recording first, and examine the transcript afterwards, because effective listeners recognise the polite forms before they see them written down.

03Grammar in context — polite requests with could / would

Stores requests are cooperative, not commanding, because you are asking a colleague for assistance and not issuing an instruction. In English, could and would perform this softening work, so the request resembles teamwork. Examine these three lines from the dialogues, and notice the identical simple shape in each one.

“Could you check the stock for me, please?” — D1
“Would you send them to the A320 line, please?” — D1
“Could you offer an alternative?” — D3

The pattern is simple, although it is easy to get wrong. After could or would, use the base verb, and do not add to or -s. Position the subject after the modal, because that word order produces a question. Add please or possibly whenever you want to sound even more polite.

FunctionStructureExample
Ask someone to do somethingCould / Would + you + base verbCould you check the stock?
Ask to receive somethingCould I have + nounCould I have two filters, please?
Ask about ability or timeWould you be able to + base verbWould you be able to send it today?
Soften even moreCould you possibly + base verbCould you possibly confirm the Form 1?

Three common mistakes (Latvian and Russian speakers)

✗ Could you to check the stock?
✓ Could you check the stock?
Why: there is no to after could, so you use the base verb, although Latvian and Russian frequently add the infinitive here.
✗ Check the stock. (as a request to a colleague)
✓ Could you check the stock, please?
Why: a bare order sounds impolite in English, so because a stores request is cooperative, you soften it with could or would.
✗ You could send me two, please?
✓ Could you send me two, please?
Why: for a request you position the modal first, so it becomes Could + you, and you avoid statement word order with a question mark.

08Reference — Simplified Technical English for stores

Stores requests are read fast, often on a noisy line or a busy hangar floor, so you write them in Simplified Technical English. You use short sentences, one instruction per line, and simple words. You keep the specialist terms of this unit, because they are precise, but you cut every word that does not carry meaning. The worked examples below show the shape of a clear request, and how to rewrite a wordy line into a clean one.

The five habits that keep a request clear

Write one idea in each sentence, and keep each sentence short. Use the active voice, so the reader knows who does what. Use a term the same way every time; do not call it a filter on one line and a unit on the next. Give a number when you mean a number, so a quantity is a figure, not a phrase. Ask with could or would, because a bare order sounds rude and slows the answer.

WORKED 1 — the shape of a stores call
1  Say who you are and where.   "This is Anna, avionics bay."
2  Give the part number.        "Part number 5-9-2-0-1-1."
3  Give the quantity.           "Quantity: one."
4  Give the reason.             "The relay is unserviceable."
5  Ask politely.                "Could you check the stock, please?"

         to have a look and see if there are any of these in stock.
AFTER:   Could you check the stock, please?
