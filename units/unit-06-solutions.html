#CorrectionWhy
1must checkNo "to" after must. Modal + base verb.
2should not"not" goes after the modal.
3should checkNo third-person "-s" after a modal.
4held"yesterday" needs the past simple, not a modal form.
5must not skip"must not" + base verb; no "to".

Model writing answer — annotated

A toolbox-talk record, 106 words, in Simplified Technical English. Full marks: correct genre, the human factor and its consequence named, and preventive measures with correct modals.

TOOLBOX TALK RECORD — DISTRACTION — 8 AUGUST 2026
Topic: a near-miss on the late shift.
Event: a technician took a phone call during a flap inspection. He was distracted. He left a spanner near a control cable. A colleague found it during a cross-check.
Human factor: distraction. Complacency also played a part, because the task felt routine. Low awareness can start an error chain, and the likely consequence is a trapped or damaged control cable.
Preventive measures: You must switch off your phone near an open panel. You must not take a call with your hands inside the aircraft. We should double-check every torque and cross-check each safety-critical task. We had better repeat this talk weekly.
Recorded by: team lead. Action: file an incident report.
Genre / STE. Labelled fields (Topic, Event, Human factor, Preventive measures) and short one-idea sentences — the Simplified Technical English habit the task asks for.
Human factor + consequence. "distraction" is named, complacency is added with a reason, and the likely consequence ("a trapped or damaged control cable") is stated — this is the outcome to reward.
Modals. must / must not for firm rules, should for advice, had better for a strong recommendation — each followed by the base verb, no "to", no "-s".
Length. 106 words, inside the 90–120 band. Mark down a record that omits the consequence, uses long sentences, or misuses a modal (e.g. "must to switch off").
