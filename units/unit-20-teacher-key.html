BLatvian & Russian interference — the errors and why

The class's first languages are Latvian and Russian. Both build cause-and-result joins differently from English, and both lack articles. The five patterns below are the marking priorities for the written trail and for E6.

LV + RU 1 · The doubled connector

✗ Because the seal was worn, so we replaced it. → ✓ Because the seal was worn, we replaced it.

Why it happens: Latvian pairs the subordinator with a resumptive adverb — tā kā… tāpēc / jo… tādēļ — and Russian does the same with потому что… поэтому / так как… то. The paired connector is correct in the L1, so learners transfer both halves into English. English allows only one linking word per join. Fix drill: "One join, one word — delete the second."

LV + RU 2 · Comma-splice with "therefore / consequently"

✗ The valve leaked therefore we removed it. → ✓ The valve leaked. Therefore, we removed it. (or "…leaked, so we removed it.")

Why it happens: In Russian and Latvian a conjunctive adverb (поэтому / tāpēc) can join two clauses after a comma, so learners treat English therefore/consequently as a comma conjunction. In English these are sentence adverbs: they need a full stop or semicolon before them, or you swap in the true conjunction so. Fix drill: "Full stop before therefore, or use so."

LV + RU 3 · "because of" + clause

✗ delayed because of the seal was worn → ✓ delayed because the seal was worn

Why it happens: Neither L1 marks the preposition/conjunction split the way English does (Russian из-за and Latvian dēļ attach to a noun, but learners over-generalise the "cause = because of" mapping). English: because of + noun (because of the wear); because + clause (because it was worn). Fix drill: "See a verb after it? Use because, not because of."

LV + RU 4 · Missing articles

✗ Found defect on seal. We fitted new seal. → ✓ Found the defect on the seal. We fitted a new seal.

Why it happens: Latvian and Russian have no articles, so a/the are simply absent from the L1 system and get dropped under time pressure — exactly the pressure of writing a fast record. Teach the record habit: a known, specific item → the; one new, non-specific item → a. Fix drill: mark every noun in a draft line and check it has an article or a good reason not to.

LV + RU 5 · Vague words instead of the approved term

✗ The part is okay. Second check was done. → ✓ The part is serviceable. The dual inspection was done.

Why it happens: Learners reach for a general L1-equivalent word (okay / normal / in order) because the precise English term is newer than the everyday one. STE and the audit both need the approved term. Fix drill: keep the vocabulary bank open while writing; swap each vague word for its bank term.

CExtension activities

  • Broken-trail hunt (fast finishers). Give a pair the "Audit finding QA-2026-018" scenario from the cross-reference widget on paper. They write the two-line finding and the corrective action in STE, then swap and mark each other against the L1 fixes above.
  • Genre swap. Take today's defect and re-cast one record in a different genre — a MEL deferral, a Service Bulletin note, or a BITE-driven avionics job (see quiz Reading). Students decide which document comes next and why, using a linking word.
  • Auditor gauntlet. One student defends a completed trail while three "auditors" each fire one question about a different link (cross-reference, reference number, sign-off block). Time-box to two minutes; grade coherence and interaction on the rubric.
  • STE tightening. Hand out a wordy, correct-but-unclear trail summary. Pairs cut it to short one-idea sentences, keep every reference number, and add one linking word per join. Compare cuts on the board.
  • Solo learners. Point them to the three AI-partner prompts in §5 (mentor, mock auditor, peer reviewer) and the chat buddy; then the writing self-check list in §6.

DCEFR B2 speaking rubric — role-plays

Use for the mock-auditor and peer-review role-plays (§5). Five criteria, four bands. "On target" is a solid B2 performance; "Strong" reaches into B2+/C1; "Developing" is B1 slipping below the outcome; "Below" needs re-teaching.

CriterionStrong (B2+)On target (B2)Developing (B1)Below
Range Uses the full bank precisely (traceability, cross-reference, dual inspection) and varies connectors. Uses most bank terms correctly; a good spread of linking words. Relies on a few terms; repeats one or two connectors ("and", "so"). Everyday words replace the specialist terms; almost no connectors.
Accuracy Connectors, articles and terms nearly always correct; self-corrects the doubled connector. Generally accurate; occasional article slip or one-per-join lapse that does not block meaning. Frequent L1 errors (double connector, "because of" + clause, dropped articles) that begin to blur meaning. Errors are constant and obscure the reasoning.
Fluency Speaks at a natural pace; short, confident sentences; pauses only to think, not to search. Keeps going with short sentences; some pausing but the trail stays clear. Halting; long pauses to find a term; sentences trail off. Cannot sustain the exchange without heavy prompting.
Interaction Answers the auditor's exact question, checks understanding, and pushes back politely when a link is queried. Responds to each question on topic; takes and gives turns smoothly. Answers a different question than the one asked; needs the partner to lead. Little genuine exchange; ignores the partner's questions.
Coherence Defends the trail as a chain of reasons; every claim joined by the right connector; nothing orphaned. Presents the records in a logical order with mostly correct linking words. Order is loose; links are asserted, not shown; the auditor must fill gaps. A list of facts with no visible reasoning between them.

Quick score: one band per criterion, then read across. Feedback tip — name the single highest-value fix first (usually a coherence or accuracy point), because it lifts the defence fastest.
