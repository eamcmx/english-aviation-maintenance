Everything the teacher needs to run Unit 17 as a 90-minute class and to assess it fairly. Grammar focus: the active imperative per Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100).

2 · Latvian & Russian interference — why each error happens

Both first languages push learners toward the same three mistakes on instructions. Name the cause, not just the fix, so students understand the correction.

Error (typical output)Why it happens (L1 cause)Correction to teach
"The connector must be disconnected." (passive for a command)Latvian and Russian written procedures and signs favour impersonal/passive forms (Latvian reflexive-passive; Russian passive and impersonal constructions). Learners transfer that register into English instructions.STE forbids the passive for a step. Recast to the active imperative: Disconnect the connector. Name the doer's action, verb first.
"It is necessary to torque the bolt." (empty introduction)Direct calque of Latvian ir nepieciešams… and Russian необходимо…, both natural ways to state a requirement. It feels polite and correct to the learner.Delete the introduction; start with the verb: Torque the bolt. The requirement is already carried by the imperative.
"To fit the blanking cap." (infinitive as a command)Russian uses the bare infinitive for written commands and prohibitions (e.g. Не курить = "no smoking"). Learners map that onto English "to + verb".English commands use the bare imperative: Fit the blanking cap. No "to".
"Fit blanking cap." (missing article)Neither Latvian nor Russian has articles, so "the/a" is routinely dropped.Keep the article: Fit the blanking cap. Drill "the/a" explicitly in the collocation round.
"Removing the bolt." (-ing for the command)Overgeneralisation of the English "-ing" form, reinforced by aspect habits from the L1 verb system.Use the plain verb for the step: Remove the bolt.
Pronunciation: "torque" → /tɔːrk/Learners read the spelling and add a vowel or read "qu" as /kw/; "torque" is a single syllable ending /k/.Model /tɔːk/ (BrE) or /tɔːrk/, one syllable. Drill "torque the bolt", "torque wrench", "torque seal".

3 · Extension activities (for strong groups / early finishers)

  • Information-gap IPC. Student A has the figure with item numbers hidden; Student B has the part list. They must talk (not show) to agree item 40 = 761782C, checking effectivity for YL-BBX. Trains the identify language under a real constraint.
  • Genre transfer. Give a two-line tech-log entry ("ENG 2 GEN caution"); students write the matching task-card steps as active imperatives, then swap and peer-mark against the STE self-check.
  • Avionics variant. Re-run role-play 2–3 for a different LRU change (e.g. a control-display unit or a BITE-fail unit): same coordination and torque/tag/test language, new component noun. Keeps both mechanics and avionics represented.
  • Handover relay. Chain of three students: each hears the previous handover once and passes it on. Tests retention of the spoken figures (figure, item, torque value, serviceable/unserviceable).
  • Dictogloss. Read Dialogue 2 at natural speed twice; pairs reconstruct the removal sequence in correct STE imperatives. Compare with the transcript.
  • AI partner. Set one conversation-partner prompt from the speaking section as speaking homework; the page tutor stays in role at B2 and recasts errors gently.

4 · CEFR B2 speaking rubric — role-plays

Use for the four role-play cards. Score each pair (or each speaker) 1–5 per dimension. A B2 pass = mostly band 4 with no dimension below 3.

Dimension5 — strong B2/C14 — solid B23 — approaching B22 — below B2
RangeUses the unit's specialist terms and collocations flexibly (torque the mounting bolt, fit fresh lockwire, tagged unserviceable) with natural connectors.Uses most specialist terms accurately; connectors present (because, unless, as soon as).Core terms used but repetitive; few connectors; leans on general words.Frequent gaps; misses key terms; relies on gesture or L1.
AccuracyInstructions are consistently active imperatives; articles and verb forms correct; errors rare and self-repaired.Mostly correct imperatives; occasional slip (passive, dropped article) that does not block meaning.Some correct imperatives but recurrent passive/"it is necessary to"; several article slips.Instructions often passive or infinitive; errors block meaning.
FluencyEven pace, appropriate to a live task; hesitations natural, not searching for basic words.Generally smooth; short pauses to plan; keeps the task moving.Noticeable hesitation; pace slows the coordination.Halting; long pauses; partner has to prompt often.
InteractionClear turn-taking; warns before moves; confirms each step; checks partner understands ("Connector is off?").Confirms most steps; warns before major moves; responds to partner.Some confirmation; misses warnings; occasional overlap or dead air.Little confirmation; steps not coordinated; talks over or ignores partner.
CoherenceSequence fully logical (identify → remove → tag → install → torque → lock/seal → test → sign); signposted.Sequence logical with minor gaps; mostly signposted (First…, Now…, Before we close…).Sequence broadly right but steps out of order or unlinked.Sequence unclear; steps missing or jumbled.

Quick feedback frame: name two things the pair did well (e.g. "clear warnings", "correct torque language") and one fix (e.g. "recast 'must be disconnected' → 'Disconnect the connector'"). Tie the fix to the interference table above.

Listening L1–L8

#AnswerRationale
L124-11-01Rob: "Open figure twenty-four eleven zero-one." Distractors reorder the digit groups to test careful listening.
L240Numerical. "The generator is item number forty." 45 (the clamp) is the near-miss.
L3FalseConnector is disconnected first; the mounting bolt is removed last because the unit is heavy once it is out.
L4a blanking capFitted on the open plug to keep dirt out. Distractors are the other unit parts (bonding lead, warning tag, torque seal).
L534Numerical. "It is thirty-four newton metres." 43 tests digit reversal, a common listening slip.
L6So the next shift can see the bolt was torqued.The torque seal is visible evidence. The longer distractor (blanking-cap function) guards against length bias.
L7TrueHandover: "The functional test was good, so the generator is serviceable. The task card is signed."
L81Numerical. "We changed the IDG on engine one." Bay two is the distractor context.

Reading R1–R5

#AnswerRationale
R1Within 600 flight hours.SB extract: compliance within 600 flight hours from receipt; 761782C stays approved until then. Distractors swap the metric (calendar days) or the trigger (immediately / next check).
R2TrueMEL permits dispatch with one IDG inoperative provided it is disconnected and placarded; Category C, rectify in 10 days.
R3Removed and replaced the IDG.Tech-log action line records a part change, then torque/lockwire/seal/test. "Reset only" and "MEL deferral" are the traps.
R4TrueAMM note: do not exceed 34 N·m; if exceeded, replace the bolt (over-torque damages the fastener).
R5When the install and the test are both complete.Sign-off rule is explicit; signing early or before the work is wrong.

Use of English UE1–UE6

#AnswerRationale
UE1DisconnectPassive → active imperative; drop "must be", verb first.
UE2FitDelete "It is necessary to"; start with the verb.
UE3Torque"is to be torqued" → "Torque"; value stays with the object.
UE4torque wrenchBank term: the calibrated tool set to the value and used until it clicks. "a torque wrench" also accepted.
UE5effectivityBank term: the IPC data showing which aircraft a part applies to. "the effectivity" accepted.
UE6Run / Carry outBoth accepted; verb-first imperative for "must be carried out".

Vocabulary V1–V4 (matching)

#Correct pairs
V1lockwire = wire that stops a fastener turning loose · blanking cap = closes an open pipe or plug · bonding lead = earths the case to the airframe · torque seal = paint mark showing a bolt was torqued · effectivity = which aircraft a part applies to
V2serviceable = fit for use · unserviceable = not fit, must be repaired · item number = identifies a part in a figure · part number = the maker's unique reference · task card = the printed list of steps
V3check → the effectivity · torque → the mounting bolt · fit → a blanking cap · run → a functional test · sign → the task card
