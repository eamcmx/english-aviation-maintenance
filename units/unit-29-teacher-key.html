First-language interference — Latvian & Russian

These learners share features that shape their English errors here: no articles, no present perfect tense, and a passive built differently from English. Anticipate the errors below and recast them gently.

1 · Dropping "been" in the passive

All checks have completed.

All checks have been completed.

Why it happens: the English passive stacks two auxiliaries (have + been) before the participle. Latvian ("visas pārbaudes ir pabeigtas") and Russian ("все проверки завершены") build the passive with a single participle and often no separate "been"-equivalent, so learners map it onto one auxiliary and drop been. Drill: the passive is never complete without been.

2 · Base verb instead of the past participle

The aircraft has been release to service.

The aircraft has been released to service.

Why it happens: the -ed participle is unstressed and its final consonant is easy to lose, especially for Latvian speakers who shorten endings and Russian speakers who devoice final consonants. Learners hear and produce the bare stem. Drill the pair aloud: release → released, defer → deferred, sign → signed, and note the irregular set → set.

3 · Present simple passive for a finished job ("are" for "have been")

All the tasks are completed.

All the tasks have been completed.

Why it happens: neither Latvian nor Russian has a present perfect. Learners reach for the plain present ("uzdevumi ir pabeigti" / "задачи выполнены") and translate it as are completed. In a release we want the present perfect passive, which links a finished action to its present result. Contrast the two on the board: are completed (a state) vs have been completed (a completed action that matters now).

4 · Bonus: dropped articles & word stress

Neither L1 has articles, so watch for Pump is no-go item → The pump is a no-go item. Drill "specific known thing → the; one of many → a" on the unit nouns (the MEL, a placard, the technical log). For pronunciation, drill the letter names C-R-S / M-E-L / A-M-M, the silent "g" in sign-off, and the stress in airworthy and release to service.

Extension activities (fast finishers / a second session)

  • Passive relay. In pairs, one student says an active line ("I closed all the task cards"); the partner recasts it the release way ("All the task cards have been completed / closed"). Swap and speed up.
  • Basis for the release. Give a mixed pile of facts (some complete, some open); students sort what supports a release and what blocks it, then say the basis in three passive sentences.
  • MEL judgement. Hand out five defects; students decide which may be deferred and which are no-go, and justify each in one sentence with the MEL as the authority.
  • STE clean-up. Give a wordy, active-voice release note; students cut it to Simplified Technical English — one idea per sentence, present perfect passive, under 18 words, no named worker.
  • Voicemail back. Students record a 30-second shift-handover voicemail (work done, one open item, what is left before release), then swap and transcribe a partner's, checking the numbers and the passive forms.

CEFR B2 speaking rubric — for the four role-plays

Score each learner 1–5 on the five criteria. B2 sits at bands 3–4. Use it to give one focus per role-play, not five corrections.

BandRangeAccuracyFluencyInteractionCoherence
5 · above B2 Uses the unit terms and flexible phrasing to hand over and justify a release with ease. High control; present perfect passive and articles almost always correct; slips are self-repaired. Smooth, natural pace; hesitation only to plan content, not to find words. Leads the handover, checks understanding, and confirms each point without prompting. Clear work-done → open-item → checks → basis structure with natural connectors.
4 · strong B2 Good range of unit vocabulary; some reformulation to justify the deferral clearly. Generally accurate; occasional dropped "been" or article that does not block meaning. Mostly fluent; a few pauses but keeps going. Responds well, asks and answers, reaches the goal (state work, confirm checks, release). Logical order; uses "so", "because", "unless", "as soon as" appropriately.
3 · B2 threshold Enough vocabulary for the task; leans on fixed phrases from the cards. Errors present (dropped "been", "are" for "have been") but meaning is clear. Some slow patches; occasional restart, still communicative. Takes part and reaches the goal with a little support from the partner. Order mostly clear; connectors simple but correct.
2 · below B2 Limited vocabulary; frequent gaps that stop the handover. Frequent errors that sometimes block meaning; no self-repair. Frequent long pauses; effortful. Mostly reactive; relies on the partner to drive. Ideas listed rather than linked; jumps around.
1 · well below Very few words; cannot sustain the role. Errors in almost every utterance; meaning often lost. Halting; long silences. Cannot keep the exchange going. No clear structure.

Target evidence for a B2 pass: the learner states the completed work in the passive (has been passed, have been closed), names the one open item, confirms the independent inspection and documentation, and justifies the deferral under the MEL before declaring the aircraft airworthy.
