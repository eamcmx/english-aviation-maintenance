2 · Latvian / Russian interference — why each error happens

These are the predictable errors for a Latvian- and Russian-speaking B2 group on this grammar and vocabulary. Each note explains the transfer, so it can be named at the board, not just corrected.

2.1 Future tense inside the time clause

When the load will finish, verify the configuration. When the load finishes, verify the configuration.

Both Latvian and Russian mark the future in both clauses: Latvian Kad ielāde būs pabeigta…, Russian Когда загрузка закончится (perfective future)…. The speaker therefore expects a future form after when. English blocks it: the time clause stays in the present. Fix on the board: underline the two verbs and show only the main clause may look to the future.

2.2 Dropped article the

Once configuration is verified, close job. Once the configuration is verified, close the job.

Neither Latvian nor Russian has articles, so learners under-produce the, especially before a known, specific noun (this configuration, this job). Fix: ask "which configuration?" — the answer "the one we just loaded" cues the definite article. Drill with the bank nouns: the dataloader, the tech log, the load media.

2.3 Dropped passive be

Once loaded the software, run BITE. Once the software is loaded, run BITE.

Russian and Latvian form short passives/participles without an auxiliary (загружено, ielādēts), so the English is/are is felt to be unnecessary. Learners also invert to verb-first. Fix: insist on subject + is + past participle; contrast the active once you load the software with the passive once the software is loaded.

2.4 Two smaller transfers to watch

  • Third-person -s and singular agreement: the checksum are good → the checksum is good; Slavic/Latvian agreement does not cue the English -s.
  • Pronunciation of the references: stress and the "th" in three, and the vowel in load vs loud; number strings (622-1140-001) should be grouped and read back, as the dialogues model.

3 · Extension activities

For fast finishers or a second lesson on the same unit.

  • Barrier information gap. Student A holds a task card, Student B a configuration report with two planted mismatches (wrong revision, wrong mod). They must cross-check by voice only and find the two faults. Rich once/when and read-back practice.
  • Live BITE role-play. One student "is" the unit and reads out a BITE line (PASS/FAIL); the other decides go/no-go and justifies with a time-clause sentence.
  • STE rewrite. Give a wordy manufacturer paragraph; students rewrite it into Simplified Technical English (short, active, one idea) and count words saved.
  • Number dictation race. Teacher dictates part numbers, load numbers and checksums at speed; students write and read back. Trains the exact listening the quiz numerical items test.
  • Shift-handover writing. Using Track 4 as a model, students write a six-line handover for a half-finished load, then record it as a voicemail.
  • AI partner debrief. After the conversation partner, students report three recasts the AI gave them and the rule behind each.

4 · CEFR B2 speaking rubric — role-plays

Use while monitoring §6. Five strands, three bands. A confident B2 performance sits in "At B2"; note where a learner is "Approaching" for targeted feedback.

StrandBelow B2 (B1)At B2 (target)Above B2 (C1)
RangeRelies on a few memorised words; gaps in the bank terms force vague wording.Uses the unit's specialist terms and collocations accurately (data load, cross-check, power cycle); can vary phrasing.Precise, flexible vocabulary; comfortable paraphrase when a term is missing.
AccuracyFrequent slips in the time-clause tense, articles and passive; errors block meaning.Good control of once/when + present, articles and the passive; errors rare and self-repaired.High accuracy even in longer turns; errors are minor and slips only.
FluencyHalting; long pauses while searching for references.Speaks at a steady pace; hesitation natural, not disruptive; reads number strings smoothly.Smooth, effortless delivery; pace supports the safety-critical read-back.
InteractionWaits to be prompted; rarely confirms or reads back.Confirms each reference aloud ("Do you confirm?"), reacts to the partner, manages the go/no-go exchange.Leads the exchange, checks understanding, repairs the partner's errors tactfully.
CoherenceSteps out of order; connectors missing.Sequences the procedure logically with once, when, as soon as, then, because, unless.Signposts clearly; the whole procedure is easy to follow end to end.
Quick score: rate each strand 1 (Below) / 2 (At) / 3 (Above). 10–12 = a solid B2 pass; 8–9 = pass with a named target; below 8 = re-do one card after feedback. Feed back on one strand only, or the correction load overwhelms.
