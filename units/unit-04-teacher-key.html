2 · First-language interference — Latvian & Russian

These three errors are predictable for Latvian- and Russian-speaking learners. Each note explains why the error happens, so the correction sticks.

A · "At first" for step one

✗ At first, we jack the aircraft.   ✓ First, we jack the aircraft.

Why it happens: Latvian sākumā and Russian сначала cover both "first (step one)" and "at first (in the beginning, but later it changed)". Learners map them to the more literal-looking "at first". In English, "at first" carries a contrast — it implies the situation later changed — so it is wrong for a first instruction. Fix: reserve "at first" for stories ("At first it worked, then it failed"); use "First," for step one. Target of E3.1, E4.1, E6 line 1.

B · Dropping -ing after before / after

✗ After remove the panel, log the torque.   ✓ After removing the panel, log the torque.

Why it happens: Neither Latvian nor Russian has a gerund (an -ing noun form of the verb). Both languages use a subordinate clause or a different construction, so learners transfer the bare verb after the preposition. Fix: drill the fixed pattern before / after + verb-ing as a chunk. Contrast with "First we remove the panel" (base form after a subject) so they see the -ing is triggered by the preposition. Target of E4.2, E4.3, E6 lines 3–4.

C · Dropping the article "the"

✗ First jack aircraft, then remove wheel.   ✓ First, jack the aircraft; then remove the wheel.

Why it happens: Latvian and Russian have no articles at all; definiteness is carried by word order, case or context. Learners therefore omit "the" and "a" because there is no L1 slot for them. This is the most persistent error and needs constant, low-stakes correction rather than one explanation. Fix: over-correct today — every base-check noun is a specific, known object ("the aircraft", "the panel", "the work order"), so "the" is almost always required. Target of E4.4, E6 lines 2 and 7. A useful extra: subject-verb agreement ("any defect goes", "we sign") also slips because the L1s conjugate differently — see E6 lines 6 and 8.

3 · Extension activities

  • Sequence dictation (fast, 5 min). Read the six safety steps in a scrambled order; students write them back in the correct order using sequencers ("First… then… as soon as…"). Self-check against the jacking widget.
  • Genre swap (10 min). Give pairs the tech-log defect entry (E7) and ask them to turn it into a spoken handover voicemail, like Dialogue 4 — what is done, what is open, what to do first. Record on a phone and play two.
  • STE rewrite (10 min). Hand out a deliberately wordy, ambiguous defect note. Pairs rewrite it in Simplified Technical English: short sentences, one instruction each, approved words. Compare against the STE self-check list.
  • New-genre reading (10 min). Bring in a real MEL, AD or SB extract (the quiz reading texts are a starting point). Students find: who issues it, is it mandatory, and by when. Builds the documentation-reading outcome.
  • Clarification role-play plus (8 min). The supervisor gives an ambiguous instruction on purpose; the mechanic must ask one precise clarification question before acting. Rotate so everyone asks and answers.
  • Vocabulary quick-fire (5 min). Teacher says a collocation verb ("raise…", "record…", "set up…"); students call the noun ("…a work order", "…the torque", "…a safety cordon").

4 · CEFR B2 speaking rubric — role-plays

Use during role-play cards 1–4. Observe each pair for one card; note one strength and one fix. Five bands map to the CEFR B2 descriptors.

Criterion4 · Strong B23 · Solid B22 · Approaching B21 · Below B2
Range
vocabulary & structures		 Uses the hangar-workflow terms and several sequencers accurately and flexibly; some subordination (although, unless, as soon as). Uses most bank terms and common sequencers correctly; limited but sufficient range. Relies on a few basic terms; repeats "first / then"; gaps force simpler wording. Very limited vocabulary; frequent searching; the task cannot be fully expressed.
Accuracy
grammar control		 Good control; the L1 errors (at first / -ing / articles) are rare and self-repaired. Generally accurate; some L1 slips that do not block meaning. Frequent L1 errors (missing "the", "at first", bare verb after before/after) that sometimes obscure meaning. Errors are constant and meaning is often lost.
Fluency
flow & pace		 Speaks at a natural, even pace; pauses are for thought, not for language. Keeps going with only occasional hesitation; pace is workable. Noticeable hesitation and false starts; pace slows the exchange. Halting; long pauses stop the exchange.
Interaction
turn-taking & clarification		 Initiates, responds and, in Card 4, asks a precise clarification question; manages the partner well. Takes turns smoothly and asks a clarification question when prompted. Responds but rarely initiates; clarification is vague ("what?"). Cannot sustain the exchange without help.
Coherence
ordering the plan		 Presents the steps in a clearly ordered, logical plan; sequencers link every step. Plan is ordered and mostly linked; the sequence is easy to follow. Some ordering, but links are missing so the plan jumps. Ideas are listed with no order; the plan is hard to follow.
Quick conversion: 16–20 = strong pass at B2; 11–15 = at B2; 6–10 = approaching, give targeted feedback; 5 or below = revisit the vocabulary and sequencer work before re-attempting.
