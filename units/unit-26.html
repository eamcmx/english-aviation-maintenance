Unit 26 of 32 · Handling ground damage and the damage report

🕐 90-minute class🎧 listening · 🗣 speaking · ✍️ writing🔑 structural damage, dent, stringer, allowable damage limit

By the end of this unit you can …

  • Describe ground-damage location and extent using station and zone references.
  • Compare damage against the SRM's allowable-limits language.
  • Recommend a repair route or referral, justifying it.

The situation

It is a busy morning in Hangar 2, and the ramp is crowded with ground vehicles. A belt loader is driven away from a parked aircraft, and unfortunately it clips the fuselage. This is ground damage, a very common problem during aircraft handling. A mechanic discovers a small dent in the metal skin, low on the left side of the aircraft.

Nobody is injured, although the situation is still serious, so the aircraft is immediately placed on hold. The team must now complete three professional tasks in the correct order. First, they describe the exact location of the dent using zone and station references. Next, they measure the damage carefully and compare it against the Structural Repair Manual.

Finally, they must make a clear decision and justify it. Sometimes the damage stays within the allowed limits, so a simple repair is possible. However, when the dent exceeds the limit, the mechanic writes a referral, because only engineering can approve a special repair scheme. Every measurement and decision goes into the paperwork.

Accurate reporting protects everyone on the ramp and inside the hangar. An incorrect location wastes valuable time, because the wrong technician inspects the wrong panel. A missing measurement forces engineering to request the information again, which delays the aircraft. Therefore, a careful and complete report is genuinely part of the maintenance itself, not extra paperwork.

Notice how the speakers always describe the damage by zone and by station. A vague description, such as 'lower left', is never enough for a professional damage report. The precise location decides which allowable damage limit applies, so accuracy really matters here.

Dialogue 1

Reporting the ground-handling dent

Transcript
MECHIvan, I must report ground damage on aircraft nine immediately, because I discovered it two minutes ago.
LEADUnderstood, please stay calm. Is anyone injured, and is the aircraft still in a safe condition?
MECHNobody is injured, although a belt loader struck the fuselage while it was being driven away.
LEADSo this is ground damage caused by the belt loader. Where exactly is the dent located?
MECHThere is a visible dent in the skin, on the lower left side of the fuselage.
LEADI need the precise location by zone and station, and not simply the description 'lower left'.
MECHIt is positioned in zone 127, near station 480, directly below the forward cargo door.
LEADGood. Is the dent on a flat panel, or is it over a stringer or a frame?
MECHIt sits on flat skin, although one stringer runs very close, adjacent to the damaged area.
LEADNoted carefully. A dent near a stringer still needs a thorough inspection, not a quick glance.
MECHI understand completely. The dent appears smooth, but I have not measured the dent depth yet.
LEADFirst, take a clear photograph, and mark the zone and station numbers directly on the image.
MECHI have marked it already, and I recorded the exact time in the tech log.
LEADWell done. Did the belt loader also leave any scratch or gouge on the surface?
MECHThere is a light scratch adjacent to the dent, although I can see no crack.
LEADOkay, please do not touch it further. I will bring the SRM and a depth gauge.
Dialogue 2

Measuring the dent against the SRM

Transcript
MECHI have the depth gauge ready, so let us carefully measure the dent depth together now.
INSPGood. Position the gauge across the dent, and read the deepest point very slowly.
MECHThe dent depth is approximately 1.4 millimetres at the centre of the damage.
INSPRecord that number now. Next, measure the length and the width of the dent as well.
MECHIt is 40 millimetres long and 30 millimetres wide, roughly oval in overall shape.
INSPLet us open the SRM and locate the allowable damage limit for this particular panel.
MECHThe SRM is open here. I am checking zone 127 and the exact skin thickness.
INSPThe allowable damage limit states that the depth must remain under 2 millimetres.
MECHSo our measured dent depth is 1.4 millimetres, which means it is within limits.
INSPYes, the depth is within limits, although you must inspect the stringer condition too.
MECHThe stringer appears perfectly straight, and I can see no crack and no corrosion.
INSPGood, because corrosion near a dent would completely change the whole repair scheme.
MECHCan we simply blend out the small surface scratch beside the dent?
INSPWe can blend out the scratch, unless it is deeper than the manual actually allows.
MECHUnderstood. I will record the measurements, and you can confirm they are within limits.
INSPAgreed. As long as everything stays within limits, this becomes a straightforward repair scheme.
Dialogue 3

Repair or referral

Transcript
MECHIvan, unfortunately one more dent on the upper skin is clearly not within limits.
ENGShow me the exact numbers. How far does it exceed the allowable damage limit?
MECHThe dent depth is 2.6 millimetres, although the allowable limit here is only 2 millimetres.
ENGSo this dent, which exceeds the allowable damage limit, needs more than a simple blend out.
MECHYes. It also sits directly over a frame, in zone 300, near station 720.
ENGThe dent that sits over a frame is exactly the case we must never rush.
MECHDo we actually have a repair scheme in the SRM for this exact position?
ENGThe SRM provides a repair scheme for flat skin, but not for this frame area.
MECHSo the damage, which the SRM does not cover, needs a referral to engineering.
ENGCorrect. We raise a referral, because engineering must design a special repair scheme.
MECHI will describe the ground damage precisely by zone, station, depth, and overall shape.
ENGAdd clear photographs, and state plainly that the dent exceeds the allowable damage limit.
MECHShould I recommend a definite repair route, or should I only report the facts?
ENGReport the facts first, then recommend a referral, and always give a clear reason.
MECHMy reason is straightforward: the damage is over a frame and clearly out of limits.
ENGThat is a strong justification. Send the referral, and mark the aircraft on hold immediately.

Grammar in context: relative clauses to specify

A professional damage report must always pin down exactly which damage exceeds the limits. Relative clauses do this important job, because they add the key detail directly inside the sentence. Look carefully at these lines, which come from today's dialogues.

"So this dent, which exceeds the allowable damage limit, needs more than a simple blend out."
"The dent that sits over a frame is exactly the case we must never rush."
"So the damage, which the SRM does not cover, needs a referral to engineering."

The clause with commas adds extra information about one known dent. The clause without commas identifies which particular dent among several you actually mean. In a report, that difference decides whether the reader understands your exact meaning.

Relative clauses give your report real precision, which matters when several dents appear on one aircraft. Without them, a colleague cannot always tell which particular dent you consider dangerous. With them, your sentence identifies the exact damage and its condition immediately. This precision separates a professional report from a confusing note.

Relative wordUse it forCommas?Example sentence
thatthings, when you pick one outno commasThe dent that sits over a frame is serious.
whichthings, when you add extra detailuse commasThe dent, which exceeds the limit, needs a referral.
whopeopledepends on meaningThe mechanic who found the dent wrote the report.
wherea place or a locationdepends on meaningThis is the zone where the loader hit the skin.

Common mistakes (and the fix)

These errors are common for Latvian and Russian speakers, because both languages often use one word for people and for things. Study each correction carefully.

The dent, that exceeds the limit, needs a repair.
The dent that exceeds the limit needs a repair.
"That" identifies one particular dent, so you use no commas around it.
The mechanic which found the damage wrote a report.
The mechanic who found the damage wrote a report.
Use "who" for people; Latvian and Russian frequently use a single word for both.
The dent, which it exceeds the limit, needs a referral.
The dent, which exceeds the limit, needs a referral.
Do not repeat the subject with "it" after the relative word "which".

Speaking: role-plays

Work with a partner and take one card each. Use the useful phrases, and try to stay calm and professional. When you finish, swap roles and practise the conversation a second time.

Card 1: Report the dent

Situation: A belt loader has struck the fuselage, and you have just discovered a dent.
Your role: the mechanic who found the ground damage.
Your goal: report the damage clearly, and give the location by zone and station.
Useful phrases: "I must report ground damage." · "There is a dent in the skin." · "It is in zone …, near station …." · "Nobody is injured."

Card 2: Measure against the SRM

Situation: You and the inspector measure the dent together, against the manual.
Your role: the inspector holding the SRM.
Your goal: get the dent depth, then decide whether it stays within limits.
Useful phrases: "Use the depth gauge." · "The allowable damage limit is …." · "That is within limits." · "Check for any crack or corrosion."

Card 3: Repair or referral

Situation: A second dent is positioned over a frame, and it is out of limits.
Your role: the mechanic speaking to the structures engineer.
Your goal: recommend a repair scheme or a referral, and justify your decision.
Useful phrases: "This dent exceeds the limit." · "The SRM does not cover this." · "I recommend a referral, because …." · "The dent is over a frame."

Card 4: Hand over to the next shift

Situation: Your shift is ending, so you telephone the next mechanic.
Your role: the day mechanic who is handing over the job.
Your goal: explain the damage, the exact location, and the current status (on hold).
Useful phrases: "The aircraft is on hold." · "The dent is in zone …, station …." · "We raised a referral." · "Do not fit the panel yet."

Writing: the ground damage report

Now you will write a short Ground Damage Report for the dent in Dialogue 3. A damage report must be clear enough for the next shift to understand it immediately, without any confusion.

Use Simplified Technical English (STE): write short sentences, keep one idea in each line, choose the active voice, and use only common words plus this unit's specialist terms. Include what happened, the damage type, the location by zone and station, the measurements, the SRM check, your result (within limits or out of limits), and your recommendation with a reason. Write between 80 and 120 words.

Simplified Technical English exists precisely because maintenance readers come from many different countries. Short sentences and approved vocabulary reduce misunderstandings, especially during a stressful situation on the ramp. When you write one clear instruction per line, the next mechanic follows it without hesitation. Consequently, your report becomes faster, safer, and considerably more reliable.

Show a model answer
GROUND DAMAGE REPORT 1. What happened: A tug struck the upper fuselage during towing. 2. Damage: one dent in the skin. No crack. No corrosion. 3. Location: zone 300, station 720, over a frame. 4. Measurements: depth 2.6 mm, length 35 mm, width 25 mm. 5. SRM check: the allowable damage limit over a frame is 1.0 mm. 6. Result: the dent depth is 2.6 mm. The dent is out of limits. 7. Recommendation: raise a referral to engineering. Engineering must design a repair scheme. 8. Action: the aircraft is on hold. Do not fit the panel. Reported by: [your name]. Date: 08 AUG 2026.
