E1 Gap-fill — put the right term in the gap

  1. turnaroundThe short time on the ground between arrival and the next departure.
  2. ground timeThe total time the aircraft stays on the ground for the turnaround.
  3. handoverYou give a handover when you pass the job and information to the next technician.
  4. defectA fault on the aircraft that must be recorded and then resolved.
  5. dispatchTo release and send the aircraft out for its flight.
  6. tech logThe aircraft logbook where each defect and action is registered.
  7. slotThe fixed time window in which the aircraft may depart.
  8. walkaroundThe visual check performed walking around the whole aircraft.
  9. pushbackWhen the tug reverses the aircraft off the stand.
  10. delayA departure later than the scheduled time.

E2 Matching — match the term to its meaning

  1. MEL → the list of what may be inoperative for dispatchMinimum equipment list — it says what is allowed to be unserviceable and still fly.
  2. deferred defect → a defect you may carry now and fix laterThe MEL permits it to be carried; full rectification comes at a later check.
  3. no-go item → an item that must work or the aircraft cannot flyIf it is unserviceable, dispatch is prohibited until it is rectified.
  4. rectify → to fix a defect fullyRectify = repair completely, so it is no longer a fault.
  5. troubleshoot → to find the cause of a fault step by stepA systematic search for the cause, e.g. with a BITE test.
  6. serviceable → fit to use and legal to fit or to flyA serviceable part has a tag and a valid certificate.
  7. release to service → the sign-off that the aircraft is fit to flyThe certification signature confirming the aircraft is airworthy again.
  8. task card → the job document that lists one task and its stepsOpened for a job so the procedure and steps are clear.
  9. AOG → aircraft on ground; a missing part stops departureA required component is missing, so the aircraft cannot depart.
  10. BITE → the built-in self-test that shows fault codesBuilt-in test equipment — the automated self-test with fault readouts.

E3 Dialogue completion — complete the handover

  1. turnaroundThe aircraft has landed but the short ground interval is tight.
  2. ground timeAsking how much time is available before pushback.
  3. handoverPassing the job to the incoming technician.
  4. walkaroundWhere a defect would be found — the visual check.
  5. defectA fault on the engine cowl, recorded in the log.
  6. task cardThe job document opened for the work.
  7. MELCheck the MEL before deferring the defect. (Accept upper or lower case.)
  8. dispatchYou cannot send the aircraft out with an open no-go item.
  9. pushbackFit the part before the aircraft is pushed back off the stand.

E4 Grammar transformation — fix the time clause

Rule: in a time clause with before / after / as soon as / once / until / when, the verb is in the present tense. Will and to are removed from the time clause; will may stay in the main clause.

  1. Fit the panel before the aircraft departs.Present after before: will depart → departs.
  2. After I finish the walkaround, I will call you.Present after after; will stays in the main clause only.
  3. As soon as the clip arrives, we will fit it.Present after as soon as: will arrive → arrives.
  4. Before you sign the log, check the task card.A clause after before, verb in present: will sign → sign.
  5. We will dispatch after we close the tech log.Present after after: the second will is removed.
  6. Once the BITE test passes, we release the aircraft.Present after once: will pass → passes.
  7. We cannot dispatch until the warning clears.Present after until: will clear → clears.
  8. Check the MEL before you defer the defect.No infinitive after before: before to defer → before you defer.
  9. When the tug arrives, we push back.Present after when: will arrive → arrives.

E6 Error-spotting — find and fix the eight errors

  1. Fit the panel before the aircraft departs.Present tense after before.
  2. After I finish the walkaround, I sign the log.Present tense after after.
  3. Check the MEL before you defer the defect.A clause, not the infinitive to defer.
  4. As soon as the clip arrives, we fit it.Present tense after as soon as.
  5. We cannot dispatch until the warning clears.Present tense after until.
  6. Once the BITE test passes, we release the aircraft.Present tense after once.
  7. When the tug arrives, we push back.Present tense after when.
  8. Before you sign, close the task card.Present tense after before.

E7 Reading 2 — the MEL dispatch message

  1. 35 minutes"ground time 35 minutes" in the header. Accept "thirty-five minutes".
  2. the cowl hinge"one deferred defect is open on the cowl hinge".
  3. C-4402"the clip, part number C-4402". Accept C4402 / 4402.
  4. No"available in stores, so there is no AOG".
  5. the panel sensor"the panel sensor must be serviceable before dispatch".
  6. a BITE test"run a BITE test as soon as you reach the aircraft".
  7. twenty ten (20:10)"the slot closes at twenty ten".

E8 Listening review — questions on Dialogue 3

  1. the panel warning"the panel warning remains illuminated in the flight deck".
  2. a BITE test"I will run a BITE test before we disturb any wiring."
  3. an open circuit"The BITE shows an open circuit."
  4. the sensor plug / connector"I will reseat the connector immediately." Accept plug or connector.
  5. the release to service"Before I sign the release to service…"
  6. they lose the slot if there is a delay"we forfeit the slot if we cause a delay". Accept "to keep the slot".

W Writing — annotated model tech log entry

A strong 80–110 word answer in Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100): short sentences, one instruction each, present tense in every time clause, actions in order. Model below, with the reason each choice earns marks in the right column.

TECH LOG ENTRY — A320 — TURNAROUND STAND 12
Defect: right cowl hinge cracked. Found on the walkaround before boarding.
States the defect and where it was found. Short sentence, one idea.
Decision: the MEL allows the defect. Carry it as a deferred defect.
Gives the MEL decision clearly — the required "decide" step.
Action: fit the temporary clip, part number C-4402. The clip is serviceable.
Names the action and the part; confirms it is serviceable.
Action: run a BITE test before you touch the wiring. Reseat the sensor plug.
Time clause: "before you touch" — present tense, correct order.
Result: the panel warning clears after the reset. Close the panel with new fasteners.
Records the result; one instruction per sentence (STE habit).
Sequence: complete the tech log before pushback. After you close the task card, sign the release to service.
Two time clauses: "before pushback" and "after you close" — both present, actions in order.
Note: rectify the hinge fully at the next base check.
Closes the deferral loop — the future rectification is stated.
Mark bandWhat earns it
ContentDefect, MEL decision, action/part, result, and the sign-off sequence are all present.
Grammar (focus)Every time clause uses the present tense — no will, no to after before/after.
STE styleShort sentences (under 20 words), one instruction each, plain approved words, clear order.
RegisterReads like a real tech log entry, not a narrative — labelled lines, imperative verbs.
Common student slips to watch for: "before you will touch the wiring" (should be touch); "after you will close the card" (should be close); long chained sentences with three actions (split them). Full band descriptors are in the teacher key.
