The situation

Aircraft YL-BBX — behind, on the critical path

  • Release slot: 22:00 tonight. The team is ~2 hours behind.
  • The blocker: the avionics mod — wiring done, but the BITE test failed.
  • The ask: two extra mechanics to clear the test by 18:00.
  • The risk: miss the slot → the aircraft goes AOG.

Every negotiation follows one pattern: state the status → propose → justify the trade-off → agree a deadline.

Ask the room
  • What does protect the slot mean?
  • Which job can safely wait, and why?
  • Who signs the aircraft back to service?

Collect answers on the board; you will confirm the exact terms in the vocabulary block.

Warm-up · pairs · 2 min

Before the meeting starts

  • Think of a time a job ran late. What was the one step that held everything up?
  • Your team is short of people for two jobs today. How do you decide which job comes first?
  • What three facts must be in a good handover to the next shift?

Report one sentence to the class. Keep it short and factual — meeting register.

Listening 1 · Dialogue 1 · ~16 turns

The morning status update

LEADThe release slot is 22:00 tonight. We are approximately two hours behind.
PLANNERExplain exactly which component is blocking the whole task.
LEADThe wiring is complete, but the BITE test failed. I need additional manpower.
PLANNERI am going to reassign two mechanics from Bay 3 onto the modification.
Listen for
  • How far behind? (two hours)
  • The blocker? (failed BITE test)
  • The request? (two mechanics)
  • Which job waits, and why? (Bay 3 cabin — has a buffer)

Play twice with books closed, once with the transcript open.

Listening 2 · Dialogue 2 · ~16 turns

Trading priorities and resources

AThe cable set requires four hours to rig. That is the genuine bottleneck.
BUnless we commence by 18:00, we cannot protect the slot.
AWhich activity carries greater risk if we rush it?
BThe rigging. Rushing a control cable is never acceptable, so it keeps priority.
The negotiation move

State a position → give a reason with because / so → reach agreement.

“The rigging keeps priority, because rushing a control cable is never safe.”

Ask the room for two more “because” reasons. Write them up.

Vocabulary · production planning

The words that run the meeting

Six terms carry the whole negotiation. Teach these live; the other fourteen are drilled in E1–E3.

  • slot — the fixed ready-and-return time
  • critical path — the chain that sets the finish
  • bottleneck — the one slow step
  • lead time — the wait before a task is ready
  • buffer — spare time for problems
  • milestone — a key checkpoint

Grammar in context · future forms

Choose the form by meaning

MeaningFormExample
Promise / decide nowwill + baseWe will finish by 18:00.
Plan decided beforegoing toI am going to brief the inspector.
Fixed arrangement + timepresent continuousWe are starting at 18:15.
Timetablepresent simpleThe slot is at 22:00.
Grammar · the key contrast

Decided now, decided before, or fixed?

will — you decide as you speak: “The test failed — I'll get two more mechanics.”

going to — you decided earlier: “I'm going to brief the inspector.”

present continuous — a fixed arrangement with a time: “We're starting at 18:15.”

Form drill: will + base verb (no to) · am/is/are going to + base · am/is/are + -ing.

First-language interference · LV / RU

Three errors to catch and recast

I do it tomorrow.
I will do it tomorrow.
LV/RU often use a present form for a future promise. English needs will.
We will to finish it.
We will finish it.
No to after will — use the bare verb.
Tomorrow I will meet the planner at 9:00.
Tomorrow I am meeting the planner at 9:00.
An arranged plan takes the present continuous, not will.

Listening for figures · E8

Catch the numbers and times

Replay Dialogue 3. Understanding spoken times and quantities is its own skill — and the quiz tests it.

  • Test done by? 18:00 — “eighteen hundred”
  • Rigging starts? 18:15
  • Transfer by? 21:30
Say each time two ways

18:00 = “eighteen hundred” = “six p.m.”
21:30 = “twenty-one thirty” = “half past nine”

Model it, then have the class read the times back.

Negotiation model · resource board

Move people — justify the trade-off

Set the board, then say the trade-off out loud:

“I am moving two mechanics onto the mod. The Bay 3 job can wait, because it has a buffer until Friday.”

Every setting must come with a reason. That reason is what the role-play rewards.

Risk · slippage vs buffer

When does slippage become AOG?

Slide the slippage up and watch the buffer disappear.

  • Small slip → report calmly with a plan.
  • Slip threatens the slot → escalate.

“If the rigging slips, I will escalate. As soon as the test passes, we start the rigging.”

Writing · Simplified Technical English

Write the shift-handover note

Task

6–8 short lines for the night shift. Include: the slot, the mod status, the rigging plan, the spares, and one future-form commitment.

Model line: “We will transfer the aircraft by 21:30.”

STE habits
  • One idea per line. Short sentences.
  • Active voice. Facts, not opinion.
  • Name the blocker — or write “no blocker”.

Start three lines in class; finish for homework.

Wrap-up

Three moves you now own

Present a status  ·  justify a priority  ·  agree who does what by when

Grammar in one line: choose the future form by meaning — will to promise, going to for a decided plan, present continuous for a fixed arrangement.

Exit ticket

Say one commitment about your own next shift, using a future form.
