Certifying release to service.

Hand an aircraft over for release. State the completed work, confirm the checks, and justify the certificate to certifying staff, in the plain, impersonal English a release is written in.

By the end of this unit you can …

  • Can state precisely what work was done and what remains open.
  • Can explain the basis for a certificate of release to service.
  • Can confirm all documentation and independent checks are complete.

01The situation

It is the end of a shift in the hangar. You have finished the work, and now you must hand the aircraft over. The certifying staff will not sign until you are clear. You must say what was done, what is still open, and why the aircraft is safe to fly. In this unit you practise that handover, so the release to service is quick and correct.

A release to service is a promise in writing. Because you are declaring the aircraft airworthy, the language must be exact, and nothing important can be omitted.

Listen to the three dialogues first, before you read the words. Try to catch the completed work and the open items, although this is difficult at first. Then open the transcript and read along. After that, build the specialist vocabulary, study the grammar, and complete the eight exercises. Finish with the speaking cards and the writing task, unless your teacher directs you differently. Throughout, notice how the certifying staff describe finished work impersonally, because a release records what has been done, not who did it.

02Model dialogues

Listen first with the transcript closed. Then open it and read along.

Dialogue 1: Handing over a landing-gear job

A = Jānis, mechanic (B1)  ·  B = Elīna, certifying staff

Transcript

JĀNISMorning, Elīna. I have finished the work order on the main landing gear.

ELĪNAGood. Talk me through it before I look at the release to service.

JĀNISThe task card told me to change the shock strut seal. I followed the aircraft maintenance manual for every step.

ELĪNAWas the old seal unserviceable, or was it just a routine change?

JĀNISIt was unserviceable. It was leaking fluid, so the rectification was needed.

ELĪNAFine. What is the scope of work on this pack, exactly?

JĀNISOne seal change, one leak check, and a functional test of the gear.

ELĪNAAnd the new seal, is it serviceable, with full traceability?

JĀNISYes. It is serviceable. The certificate gives full traceability back to the maker.

ELĪNAGood. Has the functional test been passed?

JĀNISIt has passed. The gear moved freely, and there was no leak after the test.

ELĪNAIs any item still open that I should know about?

JĀNISOne small thing. The gear-bay light is dead, so I have logged it.

ELĪNAThen that becomes a deferred defect. We carry it under the rules.

JĀNISI have already written everything in the technical log.

ELĪNAPerfect. If the paperwork is complete, I can call it airworthy and release it.

Dialogue 2: An independent inspection on a control run

A = Roberts, avionics technician (B2)  ·  B = Anna, certifying staff

Transcript

ROBERTSAnna, the aileron control run is repaired. Can you do the independent inspection?

ANNAYes. This is a control-cable job, so it needs a duplicate inspection by two people.

ROBERTSI know. I am the first person, and you are the certifying staff for the second check.

ANNACorrect. Show me the task card and the approved data you used.

ROBERTSHere. I worked from the wiring diagram and the approved data in the manual.

ANNAGood. Has the cable tension been set to the figure in the book?

ROBERTSYes. I used a calibrated tensiometer, and I have recorded the reading.

ANNALet me check the turnbuckle and the locking wire myself.

ROBERTSPlease do. The independent inspection is there to catch my mistakes.

ANNAThe lock wire looks correct. The connector has full traceability, which is good.

ROBERTSDo you want a functional test before the sign-off?

ANNAYes. Move the control, and I will watch the surface and the sense.

ROBERTSThe aileron moves the right way. Up on this side, down on the other.

ANNAGood sense, correct rigging. The functional test has been passed.

ROBERTSThen can you do the sign-off for the duplicate inspection?

ANNAYes. Both signatures go on the task card, and the record is complete.

Dialogue 3: Justifying the certificate of release to service

A = Elīna, certifying engineer  ·  B = Māris, shift supervisor

Transcript

ELĪNAMāris, I am ready to raise the certificate of release to service for the A320.

MĀRISGood. Before you sign, tell me the basis for the release to service.

ELĪNAAll task cards are closed. All the independent checks are complete.

MĀRISAnd the one defect from last night, how are we handling it?

ELĪNAThe left reading light is dead. I have made it a deferred defect.

MĀRISIs that allowed? Check the minimum equipment list first.

ELĪNAI did. The minimum equipment list lets us defer one reading light.

MĀRISGood. So the aircraft is still serviceable for the flight?

ELĪNAYes. It is serviceable and airworthy with that item deferred.

MĀRISWhere has the deferred defect been written down?

ELĪNAIt is in the technical log, with the MEL reference and a time limit.

MĀRISAnd can every certifying staff on the next shift see it?

ELĪNAYes. The entry is clear, and the placard is on the light switch.

MĀRISThen the basis is solid. Completed work, closed cards, one legal deferral.

ELĪNASo I can do the sign-off and issue the certificate now?

MĀRISYes. Sign it. The aircraft is airworthy and released to service.

04Grammar in context, present perfect passive

A release says what has been done. It does not say who did it. So we use the present perfect passive.

Look at these lines from the dialogues. The action is finished, and it still matters now. We do not name the person. The work is the subject, not the worker.

From Dialogue 1: “Has the functional test been passed?”, “It has passed.”
From Dialogue 2: “Has the cable tension been set to the figure in the book?”
From Dialogue 3: “Where has the deferred defect been written down?”

Notice why this structure dominates a release. The organisation, not the individual, carries the responsibility. Therefore the certifying staff describe the maintenance impersonally. The documentation records the outcome, although the mechanic stays identifiable through the signature. Because the participle expresses a finished result, the reader immediately understands the current condition.

The form is simple. Take have or has, then been, then the past participle (the third form of the verb). The subject is the equipment, not the person.

Subjecthave / hasbeenpast participleFull sentence
All checkshavebeencompletedAll checks have been completed.
The sealhasbeenreplacedThe seal has been replaced.
The defecthasbeendeferredThe defect has been deferred.
Both signatureshavebeenrecordedBoth signatures have been recorded.

Three common mistakes (Latvian & Russian speakers)

✗ All checks have completed. ✓ All checks have been completed. Do not drop been. Latvian and Russian have no such helper, so it is easy to lose. The passive always needs been.
✗ The aircraft has been release to service. ✓ The aircraft has been released to service. Use the past participle, not the base verb. Add the -ed: release → released.
✗ All the tasks are completed. ✓ All the tasks have been completed. The present simple passive is not the same. For a finished job that matters now, use have been, not are. Russian and Latvian have no present perfect, so this slip is common.

07Writing task

Write a certificate of release to service statement in Simplified Technical English.

A professional release avoids ambiguity completely. Consequently, every statement confirms a completed action, a documented reference, or an approved deferral. Because approving staff rely entirely on your wording, imprecise language is unacceptable. The certificate becomes permanent evidence, therefore accuracy is essential.

Task. An A320 has had a nose-wheel tyre change on work order 5120. All task cards are closed. The independent check is done. One item is deferred: the cabin reading light at seat 12C is inoperative, deferred under the MEL for 10 days. Write the CRS statement (about 60–80 words). Use the present perfect passive. Keep sentences short. Do not name the worker.

Simplified Technical English: write it clean

One idea per sentence. Use short, common words. Use the same word for the same thing. Keep each sentence under 20 words. Write in the passive: has been done, not we did.

Show a model answer
Model answer The following work has been carried out to approved data. The nose-wheel tyre has been replaced on work order 5120. All task cards have been closed. The independent inspection has been completed and signed off. One item has been deferred under the MEL: the reading light at seat 12C is inoperative, category C, 10 days. A placard has been fitted. On this basis, the aircraft is airworthy and is released to service.

Self-check

  • Did I use the present perfect passive (has been / have been + participle)?
  • Did I keep every sentence short, with one idea each?
  • Did I state the completed work, the open item, and the MEL basis?
  • Did I avoid naming the worker, and end with “released to service”?
