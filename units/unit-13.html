Querying a Service Bulletin with the OEM

A service bulletin arrives, but the effectivity is unclear. You must ask the maker the right question, understand the answer, and confirm whether your aircraft is affected. This unit gives you the language to do it well.

CourseEnglish for Aviation Maintenance Unit13, Service Bulletins FocusIndirect questions for precision Class length90 minutes (self-study ready)

By the end of this unit you can …

  • Can summarise an SB's applicability and required action accurately.
  • Can formulate precise questions to the OEM about ambiguous instructions.
  • Can confirm understanding of the OEM's answer and the next step.

00The situation

You work on the line at a Part-145 station in Riga. This morning a service bulletin lands in the mailbox. It asks for a bracket change on the pitot system.

The problem is the effectivity. The wording is not clear. You are not sure if your aircraft, serial number 5127, is on the list. You must not open the task until you know. So you call the OEM support desk. You ask a clear question, you listen well, and you confirm the next step before you plan any work.

Why does this matter so much? A wrong reading of a bulletin can cost hours of work. It can send parts you do not need. Worse, it can leave a real fault open on the aircraft. A precise question saves time and keeps the aircraft safe. This is the skill of the unit: ask well, listen well, and confirm before you act.

A good query call has four short steps. First, name the bulletin and its revision. Second, give your serial number. Third, ask your precise questions. Fourth, repeat the answer back and agree the next step. Keep each step short and clear.

In this unit you listen first, then read. Play each dialogue. Then open the transcript and check your answers.

01Model dialogues

Three short calls to the OEM. Each one performs the same job from a different angle: ask, confirm, and agree the next step.

Dialogue 1: "Is my aircraft affected?"

A line technician calls the OEM about an unclear effectivity.

Transcript
A · TechnicianGood morning. This is Rihards from Baltic Line Maintenance. I have a question about a service bulletin.
B · OEM supportGood morning. I can help. Which service bulletin are you looking at?
A · TechnicianIt is SB 34-2189, revision 2. Could you tell me which effectivity applies?
B · OEM supportOf course. The effectivity lists the affected aircraft. Do you have your serial number ready?
A · TechnicianYes. Our aircraft serial number is 5127. The registration is YL-BAK.
B · OEM supportThank you. One moment while I open the applicability table.
A · TechnicianWhile you check, could you confirm whether this bulletin is mandatory or recommended?
B · OEM supportThis one is recommended, not mandatory. It is not linked to an airworthiness directive.
A · TechnicianGood to know. The wording of the applicability was not clear to me.
B · OEM supportI understand. An old part number in the text made it confusing.
A · TechnicianThank you. I did not want to open the task without a clear answer.
B · OEM supportThat is the right call. Serial 5127 is inside the effectivity. Your aircraft is affected.
A · TechnicianUnderstood. So we should plan the modification. Is revision 2 the latest revision?
B · OEM supportYes, revision 2 is current. I will send the applicability in writing for your records.
A · TechnicianThat would help a lot. Could you also copy our engineering mailbox?
B · OEM supportCertainly. I will copy your engineering team. Please expect the email within the hour.

Dialogue 2: "Mandatory, and is there a deadline?"

A base engineer checks compliance and a superseded revision.

Transcript
A · EngineerHello, this is Anna, base engineer at Riga. I am calling about SB 27-0451.
B · OEM supportHello Anna. I have the file open. What would you like to clarify?
A · EngineerI need a clarification on compliance. Is this bulletin linked to an airworthiness directive?
B · OEM supportYes, it is. The airworthiness directive makes the modification mandatory for affected aircraft.
A · EngineerI see. Then compliance is not optional. Could you tell me which revision is current?
B · OEM supportRevision 3 is current. Revision 2 is superseded, so please do not use it.
A · EngineerThank you. Our old paperwork still shows revision 2. That is why I asked.
B · OEM supportGood that you checked. A superseded revision can send you down the wrong path.
A · EngineerUnderstood. Could you confirm whether our fleet is affected by this modification?
B · OEM supportI can, once I have each serial number. The effectivity is split by batch.
A · EngineerI will read them out. The first serial number is 4402.
B · OEM supportSerial 4402 is affected. The modification is mandatory for that aircraft.
A · EngineerAnd is there a deadline for compliance? I want to plan the work early.
B · OEM supportYes. Compliance is due within twelve months of the airworthiness directive date.
A · EngineerThat helps. Could you send me the applicability list and the revision status?
B · OEM supportOf course. I will email the OEM applicability sheet and the compliance dates today.

Dialogue 3: "Let me confirm the next step"

The technician confirms the accomplishment instructions and how to record the work.

Transcript
A · TechnicianHi, this is Rihards again. Thank you for confirming the applicability yesterday.
B · OEM supportYou are welcome. How can I help you today?
A · TechnicianI have read the accomplishment instructions. I want to confirm the next step.
B · OEM supportPlease go ahead. Tell me how you understand the accomplishment instructions.
A · TechnicianFirst we remove the bracket. Then we install the new part number. Is that right?
B · OEM supportThat is correct. After the modification, you inspect the area before you close the panel.
A · TechnicianUnderstood. Do these accomplishment instructions require any rework of the wiring?
B · OEM supportNo rework of the wiring. The modification only changes the bracket and the fasteners.
A · TechnicianGood. What is the lead time for the modification kit? We need to order it.
B · OEM supportThe lead time is about three weeks. I would advise you to order it soon.
A · TechnicianWe will order today. After we finish, how do we record the embodiment?
B · OEM supportRecord the embodiment with a logbook entry. Quote the service bulletin and the revision.
A · TechnicianSo the logbook entry must show the service bulletin number and the revision. Correct?
B · OEM supportCorrect. The logbook entry proves compliance if an inspector asks later.
A · TechnicianThat is clear now. Could you confirm the next step in one sentence?
B · OEM supportYes. Order the kit, embody the modification, then make the logbook entry.

03Grammar in context, indirect questions

On the phone with the OEM you want to sound polite and precise. Indirect questions do both. They are softer, and they carry the exact detail you need.

Look at these lines from the dialogues. Each one is an indirect question:

  • "Could you tell me which effectivity applies?" (Dialogue 1)
  • "Could you confirm whether this bulletin is mandatory?" (Dialogue 1)
  • "Could you tell me which revision is current?" (Dialogue 2)
  • "Do you know how you record the embodiment?" (from Dialogue 3)

The key rule is word order. After the opener, the clause uses normal statement order: subject, then verb. You do not use do or does, and you do not swap the subject and verb. For a yes/no question, add whether or if.

Why use indirect questions on the phone? They sound polite and calm. They also make you slow down and give the exact detail. On a busy line, a soft, clear question gets a better answer. It shows the OEM that you know the paperwork well.

Good openers are easy to reuse. Try these: "Could you tell me …", "Could you confirm …", "May I ask …", "Do you know …", and "Could you clarify …". Pick one, then add the clause in statement order. With practice, the pattern becomes automatic.

Direct questionIndirect (polite) question
Which effectivity applies?Could you tell me which effectivity applies?
Is the bulletin mandatory?Could you clarify whether the bulletin is mandatory?
What is the current revision?Do you know what the current revision is?
Does the AD apply to us?May I ask whether the AD applies to us?

Three common mistakes (Latvian / Russian speakers)

✗ Could you tell me which effectivity does apply?
✓ Could you tell me which effectivity applies? Latvian and Russian have no do/does helper. Do not add it, and do not keep it here.
✗ Could you tell me what is the current revision?
✓ Could you tell me what the current revision is? Keep statement order: subject before verb. Do not swap them as in a direct question.
✗ I would like to know is my aircraft affected.
✓ I would like to know whether my aircraft is affected. A yes/no question needs whether or if. Russian and Latvian often drop it.

More useful phrases

Keep these ready for any query call. Say them out loud twice.

  • Opening: "This is [name] from [station]. I have a question about a service bulletin."
  • Give detail: "Our aircraft serial number is 5127. The bulletin is SB 34-2189."
  • Ask precisely: "Could you tell me whether our aircraft is affected?"
  • Check the level: "May I ask whether the action is mandatory or recommended?"
  • Check the paper: "Could you tell me which revision is current?"
  • Confirm back: "So the next step is to order the kit. Is that correct?"
  • Ask in writing: "Could you send me the applicability sheet for our records?"
  • Close well: "Thank you for the clarification. That is very clear now."

07Key points to remember

A short review before you finish. Read each line and check that you can do it.

  • Always check the applicability and effectivity before you plan any work.
  • Give your serial number early. It is the fastest way to a clear answer.
  • Find out whether the action is mandatory or recommended.
  • A modification is mandatory when an airworthiness directive requires it.
  • Always use the current revision. A superseded revision leads you the wrong way.
  • Ask about the lead time for parts, so you can order the kit in good time.
  • Confirm whether the task needs any rework before you start.
  • Record the embodiment with a logbook entry that quotes the bulletin and revision.
  • Use indirect questions to sound polite and to get the exact detail you need.
  • When in doubt, ask the OEM for a clarification. Never guess.

These habits keep your work safe and your paperwork clean. They also build trust with the OEM. A technician who asks clear questions gets clear answers. That is the goal of this whole unit.
