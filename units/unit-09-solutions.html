WRITINGModel answer — a fault-isolation tech log entry (annotated)

80–110 words, Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100): one instruction per sentence, short sentences (under 20 words), present tense in every if-clause. The annotations show why the model works.

TECH LOG ENTRY — RIGHT FLAP SLOW — FAULT ISOLATION

Symptom: the right flap moves slowly on one side. The left flap is normal.

Test 1: check the circuit breaker on panel P6. The breaker is not tripped.

Test 2: inspect the connector at the actuator. The connector is clean and tight.

Test 3: run a continuity check on the wire. The reading is good.

Result: the wiring is not the root cause. The actuator is probably weak.

Next step: send the actuator for a bench test. If it fails, replace it.

Note: the fault is still open. Do not defer without the MEL.

Why it works — 1 (structure): the entry follows the fault-isolation sequence — symptom, tests, result, next step, note. A reader finds each part at once.
Why it works — 2 (STE): one idea per sentence, every sentence under twenty words, and plain approved words (check, inspect, run, send). No long chains of clauses.
Why it works — 3 (grammar focus): the next step uses the first conditional correctly — "If it fails, replace it." Present tense in the if-clause, an order in the result. No will.
Why it works — 4 (register + safety): it names the root cause honestly, keeps the fault open, and blocks an illegal defer ("Do not defer without the MEL"). That is the professional habit the unit trains.

Word count of the model: about 95 words — inside the 80–110 target. A learner answer that reaches the same four features earns full marks, even with different wording.
