§1 · lead-in

One job, five stages — in order

1 · Documentation — check effectivity, raise the work order, open the task cards.

2 · Wiring — isolate power, change the connector, test continuity.

3 · Software load — load the new part number from the laptop.

4 · Functional test — run the test and the BITE; judge any fault.

5 · Sign-off — record the result, update the status, sign the release.

The whole job is configuration control.

Every change is traced on paper. The aircraft moves from pre-mod to post-mod, and nothing is left unrecorded.

§1 · the job on one card

Reference numbers used today

Aircraft: MSN 4471  ·  Service bulletin: 24-51 (optional)

Task cards: 24-51-A (wiring) · 24-51-B (software)

New connector: part number 622-1140-002

Software: part number 285-A051

Continuity limit: below one ohm

Gain −3 against a limit of −5

Inside the limit → an accepted deviation, recorded on the work order. Below −5 → reject the aircraft. This one decision drives the sign-off.

§2 · listening · Dialogue 1

Reading the paperwork

AToday we embody a new service bulletin on the number-two radio.
BIs this service bulletin mandatory, or is it optional?
AIt is optional, because there is no airworthiness directive behind it.
BLet me check the effectivity, so that it truly applies to our aircraft.

Listen twice, then check

  • Which stage is this?
  • Mandatory or optional? Why?
  • What serial number is on the effectivity list?
  • How many task cards, and which comes first?

Notice: B reads back and confirms every reference.

§2 · listening · Dialogue 2

Making the change

AUnlock the connector, then pull it straight, never sideways.
BShould I test continuity now, before I mate and lock the connector?
APut the meter on both ends, and confirm the reading is almost zero.
BContinuity is good on every pin, because there is no break.

Listen twice, then check

  • What two stages finish here?
  • What do you test before mating the connector?
  • How often is the new wire tied?
  • What does the loader tool ask for?

Safety order: isolate → change → test → mate and lock.

§2 · listening · Dialogue 3

Test, deviation and sign-off

BIt reads antenna gain low, so this looks like a genuine snag to me.
BIt reads minus three, although the limit is minus five.
ASo we are still inside the limit. This counts as a deviation, but an accepted one.
BThe entry is written, and you have signed it. We have embodied the modification.

Listen twice, then check

  • Does the aircraft pass?
  • What colour is the BITE message? What does it say?
  • Reading vs limit — inside or outside?
  • Where is the deviation recorded?

Target line: "We have embodied the modification."

§2 · listening · Track 4

Shift-handover voicemail

RThis message is a handover for the night team.

  • What is the deviation?

§3 · fast drill

Say the whole partner

effectivity → check the effectivity · continuity → test continuity · deviation → record a deviation

Then the present-perfect bridge: "We have embodied the modification · recorded the deviation · loaded the software."

§4 · grammar in context

Present perfect for the result now

"We have embodied the modification."

"I have recorded the deviation."

Subjectauxparticiple
I / you / we / theyhaveembodied
he / she / ithasloaded
questionHave / Has+ subj + participle

Result now vs timed step

We have embodied it. — done, result stands now.

We finished it at 14:00. — a timed step, so past simple.

A finished-time word (yesterday, at 14:00) forces the past simple. Run E4 and E6.

§4 · anticipate the error

Three traps for Latvian & Russian speakers

1 · Dropped auxiliary

We already embodied it.

We have already embodied it.

No present perfect in L1 → keep have.

2 · Present perfect + finished time

We have finished it yesterday.

We finished it yesterday.

Time word → past simple.

3 · Wrong participle

The mechanic has wrote it.

The mechanic has written it.

Participle, not past simple.

Quick flags

Fit connector. → Fit the connector. (articles in speech)

Make the test. → Run the test.

§ interactive · wiring

Continuity meter — pass or fail?

Below one ohm is an unbroken path — mate the connector. One ohm or more means a break or a bad pin — do not mate.

Read the result aloud in a full sentence, with the reason clause: "…because there is no break along the wire."

§ interactive · functional test

Antenna gain — accept the deviation or reject?

Limit is −5. Inside the limit → an accepted deviation, recorded on the work order. Outside → reject the aircraft.

Say the decision as a sentence with so: "Minus three is inside the limit, so we record a deviation."

§ interactive · the whole job

Five stages — report each as done

At each stage, one learner reports it in the present perfect: "We have …".

Documentation → wiring → software load → functional test → sign-off. This is the order of the completion report.

§5 · listening review

Dialogue 3 again — the detail

Eight checks on the test and sign-off

  • Which test does B start after power-up?
  • Colour of the message? What does it say?
  • Reading? Limit? Inside or outside?
  • Where is the deviation recorded? Who signs?

spoken figures

−3   limit −5

Play once more before they answer. Strong class: dictate the two numbers.

§6 · speaking · role-plays

Four cards — one per stage

1 · Plan the job

Lead technician. Check effectivity, confirm the part number, agree the sequence.

2 · Do the wiring

Assistant. Follow the diagram, route the harness, prove continuity on every pin.

3 · Test & report a deviation

Lead technician. Decide if it is inside the limit, then record the deviation.

4 · Hand over & sign off

Off-going technician. Report what you have embodied, the deviation, what is left.

Rotate roles · confirm every reference · assess with the B2 rubric (range · accuracy · fluency · interaction · coherence).

§7 · writing & close

Completion report in Simplified Technical English

70–100 words · short sentences, one idea each · active voice · present perfect for the result.

"The software load finished with no error." — not "The loading procedure was successfully finalised."

Exit ticket: one present-perfect completion sentence, said aloud
