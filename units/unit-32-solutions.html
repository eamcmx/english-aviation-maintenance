Solutions — Exercises E1–E8

English for Aviation Maintenance · Unit 32 — Capstone simulation: base check to certified release
Teacher / self-study key · full answers with a one-line reason
Each answer below has a short WHY so the reason is clear, not just the word. Accept sensible spelling variants (for example sign-off / signoff). The anchor line — “The aircraft is ready for service.” — must keep the article, the present simple, and the preposition for.

E1 — Gap-fill: the right term

  1. work pack — the whole set of cards and paperwork for one event.
  2. base check — a deep scheduled inspection done in the hangar.
  3. task card — one job sheet with steps and a signature box.
  4. finding — a discovered defect written up for action.
  5. airworthiness directive — a mandatory instruction from the authority.
  6. service bulletin — the maker's recommended check or modification.
  7. work order — it authorises and tracks the job and its hours.
  8. functional test — it proves the system works after the work.
  9. built-in test equipment — the system's own self-test (BITE).
  10. sign-off — the dated signature confirming a step is done.

E2 — Matching: term and meaning

  1. rectification — this is the repair itself, the action that clears the defect.
  2. certificate of release to service — the signed statement returning the aircraft to use.
  3. certifying staff — only this licensed person may sign the release.
  4. airworthy — it means safe and legal to fly.
  5. deferred defect — a fault allowed to wait, controlled by the MEL.
  6. minimum equipment list — it lists what may be off for dispatch.
  7. serviceable — working and fit for use.
  8. unserviceable — not fit for use; it must be changed or deferred.
  9. tech log — the aircraft book of defects and actions.
  10. duplicate inspection — a second, independent check of a critical control.

E3 — Dialogue completion

  1. work — the fixed compound is work pack.
  2. sign — the compound is sign-off box.
  3. directive — airworthiness directive — the mandatory item.
  4. work — work order authorises and tracks the job.
  5. functional — the pump failed the functional test.
  6. equipment — minimum equipment list controls the deferral.
  7. duplicate — a duplicate inspection follows fuel-system work.
  8. release — certificate of release to service (the CRS).

E4 — Grammar transformation

  1. The aircraft is ready for service. — add the article the; the status uses present simple.
  2. The test was carried out. — passive needs the past participle carried, not carry.
  3. All findings have been cleared. — plural subject + present perfect passive; were cleared is also accepted.
  4. The pump is unserviceable. — a status is present simple, never is being.
  5. I have finished the rectification. — a finished action with a result now = present perfect.
  6. The defect was rectified yesterday. — yesterday = a dated action, so past passive.
  7. The duplicate inspection is signed. — add the article; present simple for the current state.
  8. The aircraft is ready for service. — the preposition is for, never to.

E5 — Reading 1: a task card

  1. True — the warning says de-pressurise the hydraulic system before you start.
  2. the travel value (in degrees) — step 4 records the rudder travel value.
  3. 25 and 32 — step 5 gives the limits: 25–32 degrees.
  4. raise a finding and stop — step 6: out of limits means raise a finding and stop.
  5. a duplicate inspection — step 9 is the duplicate inspection of the rudder controls.
  6. False — the work order stays open until the sign-off is complete.

E6 — Error-spotting

  1. The aircraft — add the missing article.
  2. task cards — plural agreement: all task cards are.
  3. carried out — passive needs the past participle.
  4. found — present perfect needs the past participle of find.
  5. is unserviceable — a status is present simple, not is being.
  6. is signed — singular subject: the inspection is signed.
  7. updated — present perfect needs the past participle.
  8. issued — present simple passive needs the past participle issued.

E7 — Reading 2: a tech log page

  1. There is no spare bulb in the store — the action line gives the reason for the deferral.
  2. minimum equipment list — the deferral is under MEL item 33-42.
  3. ten days — Category C: the deferred defect is allowed for ten days.
  4. False — the limitation forbids night flight unless the second landing light works.
  5. serviceable — after the pump change and test, the pump is serviceable.
  6. True — the release line says the CRS is signed by the certifying staff.

E8 — Listening review: Dialogue 3

  1. thirty-nine — thirty-nine cards had a sign-off; one was still open.
  2. the fuel pump card — that was the single open card.
  3. the tech log line — the rectification was done, but the tech-log line was missing.
  4. True — the certifying staff checks each finding and each sign-off.
  5. the certificate of release to service — the certifying staff signs the CRS.
  6. “The aircraft is ready for service.” — the anchor line closes the event.

Writing — model answer, annotated

This is one strong model. Accept any answer that keeps the Simplified Technical English habits: short sentences, one idea each, correct tense, no dropped articles, and the anchor line last.

Part 1 — tech-log line

The number two fuel pump was unserviceable. ← past passive for a dated status; article kept.

The pump was changed. A functional test was done, and a duplicate inspection was completed. ← short sentences, one action each; correct past participles (changed, done, completed).

The pump is now serviceable, and the finding is cleared. ← present simple for the status now.

Part 2 — release statement

All task cards are complete. All findings are cleared. ← present simple, plural agreement.

The base check is finished and the aircraft is airworthy. ← two ideas, still short.

The certificate of release to service is signed by the certifying staff. ← present simple passive, past participle signed.

The aircraft is ready for service. ← the anchor: article + present simple + for.

Common deductions: a dropped article (Aircraft is ready…), the continuous for a status (is being unserviceable), a bare infinitive after was (was carry out), or ready to service. Each is a one-mark fix and maps straight to the four first-language traps in the grammar focus.
