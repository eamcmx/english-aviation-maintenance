First-language interference — Latvian & Russian speakers

These three errors are predictable for this group. Each note says why it happens, so the correction can be explained, not just given.

1 · "to" after a modal — may to dispatch → may dispatch

In both Latvian and Russian the verb that carries the "allowed/possible" meaning is followed by an infinitive (Latvian drīkst lidot, Russian может вылететь), and when learners map this onto English they keep the infinitive marker "to". English modals are different: they are followed by the bare infinitive. Drill the pattern modal + bare verb until "may dispatch / must not climb / can sign" is automatic.

2 · dropped articles — aircraft can dispatch if crew follows procedure → the aircraft can dispatch if the crew follows the procedure

Neither Latvian nor Russian has articles, so learners under-produce "the/a" in English, especially before known, specific nouns (the aircraft, the crew, the placard, the MEL). This rarely blocks meaning but marks speech as non-native and can cost accuracy marks. Focus on the definite article before the concrete objects of this unit; that is where the omissions cluster.

3 · stacked modals for the future — we will can sign → we will be able to sign

The future in Latvian and Russian is often a single inflected form, so learners reach for one word and stack "will" onto "can". English forbids two modals in a row. Teach the fixed replacement will be able to for future ability, and note the same logic gives will have to for future obligation.

Also watch: "make/do" confusion (do a check, not make a check); "actual" used for "current"; and pronouncing the "L" silent in "calm/collar". Recast softly during the role-plays rather than interrupting.

CEFR B2 speaking rubric — the role-plays

Score each learner across five criteria. Band 3 is the B2 target; band 4 is B2+ (approaching C1); bands 1–2 fall short of B2. Score the dispatch content through the language, not instead of it.

Range (vocabulary & structures)

4 · B2+ Uses the MEL field fluently (proviso, limitation, rectification interval, defer, placard) and varies structures with ease.
3 · B2 Has enough language to handle the call; uses most bank terms correctly with occasional searching.
2 Manages the gist but leans on a few words; misses key terms (says "the rule" for "proviso").
1 Very limited; cannot sustain the call without long pauses or the first language.

Accuracy (grammar & the unit's modals)

4 · B2+ Modals are consistently right (may/must not/cannot/will be able to); articles reliable; errors rare and self-corrected.
3 · B2 Good control; the three interference errors appear but do not block meaning; some self-repair.
2 Frequent modal or article errors ("may to", "will can", dropped "the") that slow the listener.
1 Errors are dense and often obscure meaning.

Fluency

4 · B2+ Speaks at a natural pace with only occasional hesitation to plan complex points.
3 · B2 Keeps going with a fairly even tempo; pauses to search but recovers without help.
2 Noticeable stop-start; needs prompts to continue.
1 Halting; long silences break the exchange.

Interaction

4 · B2+ Runs the call: initiates, confirms, checks provisos, hands over cleanly, invites the partner in.
3 · B2 Takes turns well; confirms and questions ("What is the proviso?", "Do I placard it?"); little dead air.
2 Responds but rarely initiates; relies on the partner to drive the call.
1 Cannot sustain the exchange without the teacher.

Coherence

4 · B2+ Sequences the decision logically (defect → MEL → category → provisos → placard → release) with clear connectors (although, unless, as soon as).
3 · B2 Organises the call sensibly; uses common connectors; the go/no-go logic is easy to follow.
2 Ideas arrive out of order; the listener must reassemble the decision.
1 Disconnected; no clear line from defect to decision.
Quick conversion: add the five band scores (max 20). 15–20 = secure B2; 11–14 = B2 emerging, target accuracy; 6–10 = below B2, re-teach modals and the bank; 5 or under = needs the foundations first.
