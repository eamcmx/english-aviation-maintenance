01The situation

It is 07:00 in the hangar. An A320 is booked in for a scheduled base check. Two mechanics, Anna and Ravi, collect the work pack and position themselves at the aircraft. They must review the documentation, agree their responsibilities, and open the aircraft safely. Every operation follows a sequence, because you cannot remove a wheel before jacking the aircraft. As soon as they open the first panel, they discover something the cards did not anticipate. In this unit you listen to their morning, learn the vocabulary they use, and practise the identical procedure yourself.

How to use this unit

Listen to each recording first, before reading the transcript. Afterwards, open the transcript and read carefully. Study the vocabulary, then examine the grammar. Complete the eight exercises and verify your own answers. Finally, practise the speaking cards and the writing assignment.

02Model dialogues

Three short dialogues follow the same morning from three angles: planning the work, opening the aircraft, and reporting the defect. Listen first, then read.

Dialogue 1: Reading the work pack and splitting the cards

Transcript, Dialogue 1

AMorning, Ravi. Here is the A320 work pack. Let us review the documentation before we begin.

BGood idea. First, we examine the work order. It authorises a scheduled base check, plus two additional task cards.

ACorrect. The work order is the legal authority. Nothing begins until it is properly signed and open.

BAgreed. So, documentation first, then the aircraft. How many task cards are inside the pack?

ASix task cards. Each one is a separate job, with individual steps and its own sign-off box.

BLet us divide them. I will handle the landing gear cards, because I completed this inspection recently.

AFine. I will manage the panel and inspection cards. Each card references a section of the AMM.

BGood. Whenever we need a part number, we consult the IPC. It identifies the component and its position.

AExactly. Before we certify anything, we review the latest handover recorded in the tech log.

BThe tech log is clear. The night shift reported one open item, although it does not affect our cards.

ALet me confirm the priority. Safety and access first, then the inspections. The sign-off is always final.

BUnderstood. Once a card is genuinely complete, we certify it. We never approve unfinished work.

APrecisely. If a card is unclear, we consult the supervisor. We never assume anything on a base check.

BSo the plan is agreed. I study my cards, you study yours, and we meet at the aircraft shortly.

APerfect. One final point, every defect we discover goes immediately into the tech log.

BUnderstood. Documentation first, aircraft second. I will see you at the nose gear.

Dialogue 2: Jacking the aircraft and opening the panels

Transcript, Dialogue 2

ARight, we are at the aircraft. Before we jack it, we establish the safety cordon around the bay.

BThe safety cordon is complete. I have positioned a warning notice on each stand, so nobody enters.

AGood. Are the wheel chocks fitted? We never begin jacking until the wheel chock secures every tyre.

BChocks are fitted. Now I attach the jack pads to each jack point beneath the wings and the nose.

ACareful, the AMM instructs us to jack the nose last. First the wings, then the nose, unless the card differs.

BUnderstood. Wings first. Jacking slowly now, and I monitor the level as the aircraft rises.

ANicely level. As soon as it is elevated, we install the lockout pins on every jack.

BLockout pins are installed. The aircraft is secure on jacks. Now we can open the access panel.

AMy card covers panel 191AB. I count twenty panel fasteners. I will bag them, so nothing is misplaced.

BGood habit. A loose fastener becomes FOD, and FOD can seriously damage an engine or a tyre.

ACorrect. The panel is removed. I record the torque value from the AMM, so we reassemble it correctly.

BSensible. Document the torque immediately, while you remember it. Then it is ready for closing.

ADone. So, cordon established, chocks fitted, jacked, locked out, panel open. That is the correct sequence.

BTextbook. Although we work quickly, we never skip a procedure. A skipped step endangers people.

AAgreed. I will begin the inspection behind the panel. Can you verify my jack point on the left wing?

BVerifying now. The left jack point looks secure, the pad is seated, no movement. You may inspect.

AThanks. If anything appears abnormal inside, I will stop immediately and call you before I touch it.

Dialogue 3: Reporting a defect and asking for clarification

Transcript, Dialogue 3

ARavi, stop a second. Behind the access panel I can see a crack near a bracket. It is not on my card.

BLet me examine it. Yes, I see it too. That is a defect, and it is completely absent from the work pack.

AExactly. So we suspend this task immediately. We report the defect before we continue anything here.

BAgreed. First, we record it in the tech log. Then we raise a separate work order for the repair.

AThe AMM is not clear here. It illustrates the bracket, but it provides no limit for this particular crack.

BYou are right, it is ambiguous. Whenever the AMM is unclear, we never decide alone. We ask.

ASo, can you clarify one thing for me? Do we measure the crack first, or contact the supervisor first?

BMeasure it first, so we provide an accurate number. Then we call, so the supervisor receives the facts.

AGood. The crack measures about twelve millimetres. I will photograph it beside a ruler for the record.

BExcellent. And the part number, if required, do we obtain it from the IPC or from the panel label?

AFrom the IPC, definitely. The panel label may be outdated. The IPC is the controlled source.

BUnderstood. I will compose a clear defect entry now, location, dimensions, and our actions, in plain language.

APlease retain the torque note for this panel too. We cannot close it until the defect is cleared.

BCorrect. I will not certify the panel card, because the job remains incomplete. It stays open.

ASorry, one additional question, should the sign-off await the supervisor, or await the repair?

BAwait the repair. No sign-off until the defect is repaired and inspected. That is the rule, always.

04Grammar in context, sequencers for workflow

A base-check opening must proceed in the correct order. We use sequencers to communicate that order. They inform your partner exactly what happens first, next, and finally. This discipline keeps the operation safe and unambiguous.

Look at these lines from the dialogues:

  • "First, we check the work order. It says a scheduled base check." (Dialogue 1)
  • "First the wings, then the nose, unless the card says otherwise." (Dialogue 2)
  • "As soon as it is up, we fit the lockout pins." (Dialogue 2)
  • "First, we record it in the tech log. Then we raise a new work order." (Dialogue 3)

Notice the pattern: First jack the aircraft, then remove the wheel. The order is fixed, so the words that mark the order matter.

Form table

SequencerUseExample at work
First, … / First of all, …The very first step.First, we set up the safety cordon.
Then, … / Next, … / After that, …The following step.Then we fit the wheel chock on each tyre.
Before / After + -ingLink two steps by time.After removing the panel, we log the torque.
As soon as / Once …One step starts right after another.As soon as it is up, we fit the lockout pins.

Common mistakes (Latvian and Russian speakers)

At first we jack the aircraft, then we remove the wheel. First, we jack the aircraft, then we remove the wheel. "At first" means "in the beginning, but later it changed". For step one, use "first". Latvian and Russian speakers often carry over "sākumā / сначала" as "at first".
After remove the panel, log the torque. After removing the panel, log the torque. After "before" and "after", use the -ing form of the verb. There is no gerund in Latvian or Russian, so learners drop the -ing.
First jack aircraft, then remove wheel. First, jack the aircraft; then remove the wheel. Latvian and Russian have no articles, so "the" is often dropped. In English we say "the aircraft" and "the wheel".

07Writing, a tech-log defect entry

Compose a clear defect entry for the tech log. Use Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100): short sentences, one instruction per sentence, and approved, unambiguous vocabulary. Describe the crack discovered behind access panel 191AB.

Your task

Write 60–90 words. Include: what the defect is, where it is, its size, what you did, and why the sign-off is held. Use sequencers to show the order of your actions.

Show a model answer
MODEL, DEFECT ENTRY (Simplified Technical English)

Defect: Crack on a bracket behind access panel 191AB. Length about 12 mm. The defect is not in the work pack.

Action: First, we stopped the task. Then we measured the crack and took a photo. After that, we recorded the defect in the tech log. The AMM gives no crack limit here, so it is ambiguous.
