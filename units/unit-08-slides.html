§0 · the scenario · interactive clock

Forty-five minutes on the ground — and a defect appears

The aircraft is down. The slot is fixed. Watch the ground time drain as the team works the turnaround.

Every block is one action: handover, walkaround, decide, fit the part, document, release. A delay in one block eats the slot.

§1 · Dialogue 1 · the handover

Take the handover — listen first, then read

AHi Raivis, here is my handover on the A320, so please listen before boarding commences.

BGo ahead, Anna. The turnaround is extremely tight, because we only have forty-five minutes of ground time.

AOn the right engine cowl, one access panel is loose, and two fasteners are missing.

BRight: secure the panel, install two fasteners, then complete the walkaround before pushback.

Gist first (books closed):

  • How much ground time? → 45 minutes
  • What is the defect? → loose panel, 2 fasteners missing
  • Where is it recorded? → the tech log

Read-back habit: "Let me repeat it back, so we both agree."

§1 · Dialogue 2 · rectify, defer, or AOG · interactive

The decision — walk the branches

A cracked hinge changes everything. Is it a no-go item, or can the team carry it?

Check the MEL → fit a temporary clip → is it in stores? If yes, it is not an AOG. Defer the hinge, rectify at the base check.

§1 · Dialogue 3 · act & document · interactive BITE

Troubleshoot, clear the warning, then sign

The clip is fitted, but the panel warning is still on. Run a BITE test before you touch the wiring.

Open circuit → reseat the sensor connector → reset → the warning clears. Document, then sign the release to service before pushback.

§2 · interactive · match the term

Term → plain meaning — call it, then reveal

Read the term. The class calls the plain meaning. Then click to check.

Common trap: MEL (the list of what may be inoperative) is not AOG (a missing part that stops the flight). Keep them apart.

§3 · grammar focus · time clauses

Ordering the actions: before / after

"Secure the panel before the aircraft goes for pushback." — D1

"After you close the task card, I will sign the release." — D3

"I will update the task card as soon as the clip arrives." — D2

The one rule: in a time clause about the future, use the present tense — never will.

✓ before the aircraft departs

✗ before the aircraft will depart

The main clause keeps will or an instruction: "Fit the panel before it departs."

§3 · interactive · rAF · build the sequence

Pick a connector — watch the order and the tense

Two actions, one connector. The widget puts them in order and writes the sentence.

Notice: the verb in the time clause stays in the present — arrives, finish, closes — whichever connector you choose.

§3 · first-language traps · Latvian & Russian

Three errors to expect — and fix

✗ Wrong✓ RightWhy it happens
Fit the panel before the aircraft will depart.…before the aircraft departs.Latvian/Russian mark the future in the time clause; English uses the present.
After I will finish the walkaround…After I finish the walkaround…Same cause — will belongs only in the main clause.
Check the MEL before to defer the defect.…before you defer the defect.No infinitive after before; use a clause or an -ing form.

§5 · speaking · run the turnaround

Four role-play cards — in sequence

Card 1 · Handover

Incoming technician. Get the ground time, slot, defect and task card — repeat it back.

Card 2 · Decide

Check the MEL. No-go item? Agree to fit the clip and defer the hinge.

Card 3 · Request the part

Call stores. Give the part number, check it is serviceable, confirm before pushback.

Card 4 · Act, document, release

Run a BITE test, clear the warning, complete the tech log, agree the release before pushback. Swap roles after each card.

§5 · assessment · CEFR B2 speaking

What to listen for — five bands

BandStrong B2 sounds like…
RangeUses the specialist terms and connectors (although, as soon as, unless) naturally, not just basic words.
AccuracyTime clauses correct under pressure — present after before/after; few errors that block meaning.
FluencyKeeps the fast exchange moving; short natural pauses, not long silences.
InteractionAsks for repeats, reads the handover back, confirms the sequence before acting.
CoherenceActions in a clear order; before / after / then / once link the steps.
§6 · writing · Simplified Technical English

Write the tech log entry — STE habits

✗ "After the clip has been fitted and the system has been reset, the warning, which was previously illuminated, should then be confirmed as extinguished before dispatch is approved."

✓ "Fit the clip. Reset the system. Confirm the warning is off. Then dispatch."

ASD-STE100 habits:

  • One instruction per sentence.
  • Under 20 words per sentence.
  • Present tense in every time clause.
  • Actions in the right order — before / after.

Write 80–110 words: defect, MEL decision, action, sequence.
