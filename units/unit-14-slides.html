English for Aviation Maintenance · Unit 14 · CEFR B2 · 90 minutes

Assessing an Airworthiness Directive

A new AD has just arrived. Read it, find the required action and the compliance deadline, and brief the planner — including the grounding risk if the deadline is missed.

must / shall for obligation  ·  20 bank terms  ·  4 dialogues  ·  AD & planning-memo reading  ·  "The AD must be done."

By the end · the shape of the class

Three things you will be able to do

1 — Read the order. Identify the required action and the compliance deadline in an AD.

2 — Name the risk. Explain the airworthiness consequence of non-compliance — grounding.

3 — Plan it. Discuss with planning how and when to comply.

The route: listen → vocabulary → grammar (must/shall) → exercises → role-play → write the briefing note.

Four dialogues, two authentic documents, eight exercises. The quiz at the end is the check — everything before it is practice.

The scenario

One AD, one briefing, one deadline

Karlis, a base engineer, must brief Marta in planning on a directive that arrived this morning: what must be done, by when, and the grounding risk if the slot is missed.

AD 2026-0147 orders a recurring inspection of the main landing gear pivot pin on two A320 aircraft. The pin can crack under load — an unsafe condition. Everything today is a part of that briefing.

§1 · Listening · Dialogue 1

"A new AD has arrived" — catch two facts

Listen twice. First time: books closed. Second time: transcript open.

Find and write just two facts:

  • What must be done?
  • By when — the first deadline?

Then tell your partner in one sentence each.

Listen for the connectors that carry the meaning: although only two aircraft are affected · as soon as you can, tell me the effective date · unless the aircraft reaches ninety days first.

ANSWER Action = recurring inspection of the landing gear pin. Deadline = within 500 flight cycles (or 90 days) from 22 August.

§1 · Interactive · rAF · "whichever comes first"

Two clocks race — plan to the earlier one

The first inspection is due at 500 flight cycles OR 90 days — whichever comes first.

Drag the flight rate. A daily-flying aircraft hits 500 cycles first, so its deadline is earlier. That is why the blue aircraft is urgent.

§1 · Listening · Dialogue 2

"The repeat check and the final fix"

Two words people mix up:

TermPlain meaning
thresholdwhen the first check is due — 500 FC
intervalthe gap between repeats — 1500 FC / 12 mo

Also catch: cycles, not hours — one cycle is one takeoff and landing. The recurring inspection is the short-term corrective action; the improved pin is the terminating action that ends the repeats.

§1 · Interactive · rAF · build the timeline

Threshold once · interval repeats · terminating action ends it

Press the buttons to place each event. Watch the recurring inspection repeat at the interval.

The terminating action — the improved pin — stops the repeats for good. Planning schedules the whole chain, not one job.

§1 · Listening · Dialogues 3 & 4

The plan, the paperwork, and the voicemail

Dialogue 3 — they agree the plan: first check before 5 September, slot booked 2 September, a logbook entry that quotes the AD number, and the old bulletin is superseded.

Dialogue 4 — the voicemail. One voice, numbers spoken as words. Harder, on purpose.

Catch the spoken figures: AD 2026-0147 · effective 22 August · 500 flight cycles · two A320 aircraft. This feeds E8 and the quiz.

§2 · Interactive · retrieval practice

§3 · Grammar · must / shall for obligation

An AD is a legal order — so the modal is strong

UseStructureExample
Obligation (active)must + baseWe must comply before the deadline.
Obligation (passive)must be + past participleThe AD must be done on time.
Legal / writtenshall + baseThe operator shall report compliance.
Prohibition (a ban)must not + baseYou must not fly after the deadline.

Two rules, no exceptions: never to after must/shall · the verb never changes for he/she/it.

§3 · Interactive · build the obligation

must not (ban) ≠ need not (no obligation)

Build a sentence. The widget checks the form and the meaning.

must not = you are banned. need not = you may, but nobody requires it. After the improved pin is fitted, we need not repeat the check — the duty has ended.

§3 · First-language interference · LV / RU

Three errors to catch before they set

You may hearCorrectWhy
must to be donemust be doneLV/RU obligation takes an infinitive — no to after must.
must donemust be doneThe passive auxiliary be is dropped; it is required.
must not repeat (when duty ended)need not repeatBan vs no-obligation — and here it is safety-relevant.

Bonus: neither L1 has articles, so watch for "is the corrective action".

§5 · Speaking · four role-play cards

Brief the planner — then swap and go again

1 — Brief the planner: the action and the deadline.

2 — Explain the recurring inspection and the terminating action.

3 — Defend the early slot: non-compliance means grounding.

4 — Confirm the paperwork: logbook entry, superseded bulletin.

Rule for every turn: one modal (must/shall/must not/need not) and one bank term.

Pair up, run the card, then swap roles so both speak. No partner? Use the three AI-buddy prompts on the page — they recast modal errors gently.

§6 · Writing · Simplified Technical English

Write the briefing note — short, plain, exact

Task: a 90–120 word note to planning about AD 2026-0147 — action, effective date, deadline, grounding risk. Use must and shall.

STE habits: one idea per line · active voice · simple approved words · same name every time (the pivot pin, never "it").

"It is imperative the aforementioned component be inspected" → "The pivot pin must be inspected."

Recap · the check

Read the order · name the risk · plan the compliance

An AD is a legal, mandatory order · read it for the action and the compliance deadline · "whichever comes first" — check both clocks · threshold once, interval repeats, terminating action ends it · must/shall carry the obligation, never a to · must not = ban, need not = no obligation · non-compliance = grounding.
