Mentoring: explaining a task to a junior

Coach a new technician through a main-wheel change — give a clear walk-through, check understanding, and correct one deliberate error the constructive way.

90-minute class · sequencers + imperatives · walk-through · competence · sign-off authority

By the end · and how the 90 minutes runs

Three can-do outcomes

1 · Explain. Explain a procedure step by step at a level a junior understands.

2 · Check & correct. Check understanding, and correct errors constructively — show the fault, give the reason.

3 · Rationale. Relate the task to its safety and airworthiness rationale.

What does good on-the-job coaching sound like?

One step at a time · show then let them try · say why, not only what · check before moving on.

Today the mentor also corrects one deliberate error — the constructive way: show the fault, give the reason, let the junior fix it. Listen for how the order is signalled: First… Next… Then… Finally.

Dialogue 1 · listen ×2 · The walk-through

Set the scene, then the first steps

Mentor

Today I will mentor you through a complete main-wheel change, so watch my walk-through carefully first.

Junior

Although I have observed a wheel change before, this is my first without close supervision.

Mentor

First, read the task card completely, because it lists every step in the correct order.

Gist check (chorus):

  1. What is the job? a main-wheel change
  2. What comes before jacking? the FOD check + reading the task card
  3. Why read the task card first? it lists every step in order

glossed: walk-through · task card

Dialogue 2 · CENTREPIECE · torque, lockwire, a deliberate error

Set the value first — then torque

Correct the tool, hand back the thinking: “A ratchet is completely wrong here, so which tool do we use?”

Sequencer + imperative: “First, set the exact value; then torque the nut.”

spec: 300 Nm · say the number aloud

Dialogue 2 · the second error · lockwire direction

The wire must pull the nut tight, never loose

Constructive correction (the unit motto):

“I show the actual fault, and I always give a reason.” — not simply the word wrong.

glossed: lockwire · feedback · rationale

Dialogue 3 · checking understanding + sign-off authority

Check the reason — then the authority

Q: Why torque before the lockwire?
A: the lockwire only secures a nut that is already torqued.

Q: Rationale for the whole job?
A: airworthiness — a loose main wheel is an unacceptable risk.

Q: Can the junior sign it off?
A: not yet — no sign-off authority.

Q: What does sign-off need?
A: a valid licence and a formal competence assessment.

Grammar 1 · Interactive · sequencers

Signal the order — one sequencer per step

Set: First · Next · Then · After that · Once… · As soon as… · Finally

Click the six steps in the correct order. The chain only builds when the order is right — because the sequence really is fixed.

Grammar 2 · the teaching imperative

Sequencer + comma + plain verb — no subject

Do not saySayWhy
First, you read the card.First, read the card.a teaching step drops the subject
Then, you are torquing the nut.Then, torque the nut.use the plain verb, not the continuous
Finally, must to fit the lockwire.Finally, fit the lockwire.no must to; just the imperative

Short imperatives = one clear action the junior can follow correctly. This is exactly what E4 and the writing task rehearse.

First-language watch · Latvian & Russian

Three predictable slips — and the fix

✗ In first, remove the wheel.

✓ First, remove the wheel.

No in first — a calque of the L1 ordinal.

✗ Put wheel on axle.

✓ Put the wheel on the axle.

L1 has no articles — the is easy to lose.

✗ Then you are torquing it.

✓ Then, torque it.

Bare imperative — no continuous, no you.

Technique · before the role-plays

Check by asking them to say it back — correct by giving the reason

Not “OK?” → “Which action comes before jacking?”

Not “that’s wrong” → “Your lockwire pulls the nut loose; it must pull it tight — try again.”

Speaking · 4 role-play cards · pairs, then swap

Mentor ↔ junior — run all four cards

Card 1 · The walk-through. Teach the first steps in order, one clear instruction at a time.

Card 2 · Correcting a mistake. The junior grabs a ratchet, mis-fits the lockwire — show the fault, give the reason.

Card 3 · Comprehension check. Ask why each step matters; link the answers to airworthiness.

Card 4 · Sign-off authority. Explain why the junior cannot certify the job yet.

3 min/card, then swap · monitor for sequencer + imperative + the correction frame · delayed feedback, not interruption.

Writing · start in class · Simplified Technical English

Write the six steps as teaching instructions

Brief. First six steps of a main-wheel change, 60–90 words. A sequencer + an imperative in each step. Add the safety rationale to at least one step.

one action per step · keep it under 20 words

STE habits (ASD-STE100)

  • One idea per sentence.
  • Short, common words.
  • Same word for the same thing.
  • Imperative — read the card, not you should read the card.
