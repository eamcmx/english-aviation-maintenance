Giving a shift handover briefing — full answer key

Complete model answers for E1–E8, one line of WHY per item, and the annotated model handover note. Present perfect shown in green, past simple in amber.

E1Gap-fill — the right term in each gap

#AnswerWhy
1handoverYou pass work and news to the next team — that is a handover.
2outstandingWork not yet done is "outstanding".
3deferred defectA fault flown on the MEL and fixed later is a deferred defect.
4carried overUnfinished work passed to the next shift is "carried over".
5open defectAny unfixed fault is an open defect; aircraft one has none.
6tech logFaults are logged in the aircraft's tech log.
7task cardA job's numbered steps live on a task card.
8sign offTo certify a completed job you "sign off" the work.
9serviceableA clean tech log means the aircraft is fit to use — serviceable.
10release to serviceYou certify the aircraft is fit to fly = release to service.

E6Error-spotting — fix the night-shift note

#AnswerWhy
1replaced"at 22:00" is a finished time → past simple, not present perfect.
2have already signed"already" needs the present perfect (add "have").
3has not started (have not started)Negative needs the auxiliary: has/have + not + started.
4have finishedThe participle of "finish" is "finished", not "finish".
5have you closed"yet" → present perfect question; don't use "did".
6rectified"last night" is a finished time → past simple.
7open defect (an open defect)"outstanding defect" is not the term; an unfixed fault is an open defect.
8when you finish (you finish)After "when" for the future, use the present simple, not the past.

Writing task — model handover note (annotated)

90–120 words · Simplified Technical English · all three aircraft · correct tense contrast. The model below meets every checklist item; the amber notes explain each choice.

HANDOVER NOTE — DAY SHIFT TO NIGHT SHIFT

Aircraft 1 (A320, stand 12): I have completed the left engine oil change. I ran the leak check at 15:00; it was dry. The daily is done and the task card is signed. There is no open defect. The aircraft is serviceable and released to service.

✔ Present perfect "have completed" = done and relevant now · past simple "ran … at 15:00" = finished time · states serviceable + no open defect (outcome 2).

Aircraft 2 (737, bay 3): One deferred defect. The right landing light is unserviceable. I applied the MEL, category C, and fitted a placard. Work order 8841 stays open. It is not safety-critical. Please check the placard before departure.

✔ Flags the open/deferred defect and its safety status clearly · gives the next-shift action ("check the placard") · one idea per sentence (STE).

Aircraft 3 (ATR 72, hangar): Mid-task. I have finished steps one to four of the radar BITE job. Steps five and six are outstanding. Troubleshoot the wiring with the AMM, then run the BITE again. Carried over to you.

✔ Perfect for progress made ("have finished"), plain "are outstanding" for what is left · imperative gives the exact next step · "carried over" hands the work on.
Marking guide (STE + tense). Award credit for: (1) all three aircraft covered; (2) at least two correct present-perfect uses for done-and-relevant work; (3) at least one past-simple with a stated time; (4) every open defect and safety-critical status flagged; (5) short sentences, one idea per line, one word per thing (e.g. "placard" not "warning notice"). Deduct for finished-time-with-perfect errors (a top L1 trap) and for dropped auxiliaries in negatives/questions. Word count 90–120.
