2 · Latvian & Russian interference — why each error happens

Both first languages sit behind predictable B2 errors in this unit. Pre-teach against them; the corrections are drilled in E4 and E6.

Error the students makeWhy it happens (L1)Fix to teach
"The line leaks since yesterday."
"The line leaked since yesterday."		 Neither Latvian nor Russian has a present-perfect tense. The "started-before-and-still-true" idea is carried by aspect and context, not by have + participle. So students default to the present or past simple. Teach the trigger: the defect is still true now → present perfect. "The line has leaked since yesterday."
"I have find a leak."
"has develop cracks"		 The have + participle pattern is unfamiliar, so learners keep the base verb after have/has. Irregular participles (find→found) are extra load. Drill participle forms as fixed pairs: find/found, store/stored, develop/developed. After has/have, always the participle.
"leaked since two flights"
"for yesterday"		 One preposition in each L1 covers both for and since, so the choice feels arbitrary and is guessed. Fixed rule, not a feel: for + length of time; since + start point.
"I have found leak."
"raise snag"		 Neither language has articles, so a/the are dropped in speech and writing. Nudge the article back in: "a hydraulic leak", "a snag". Model it in every recast.
"The line is broken."
(for any fault)		 A single broad word (LV bojāts, RU сломан) covers many faults, so learners over-generalise "broken". Push the precise term: leak, chafing, crack, corrosion, play, wear — each names a different fault.

Pronunciation to model: chafing /ˈtʃeɪfɪŋ/ (not "chaffing"), intermittent /ˌɪntəˈmɪtənt/, unserviceable /ʌnˈsɜːvɪsəbl/. Russian speakers may harden the final consonant of "log"; Latvian speakers may shorten English long vowels in "leak" vs "lick".

3 · Extension activities (if you have time or a fast group)

  • Real tech-log dictation. Read a five-line defect entry at dictation speed; pairs write it, then compare against the board. Trains catching spoken figures (stand, reference, hours).
  • Rephrase relay. Give vague pilot lines ("something's not right up front", "the wheel feels funny"); teams race to a precise symptom + location + condition. LO3 stretch.
  • Fact-or-assumption card sort. Print 12 statements; groups sort into observation / assumption and justify. Ties LO2 to the deck widget.
  • Cross-trade swap. Mechanics write a brake defect; avionics learners write a display defect; they exchange and check each other's present-perfect use and STE.
  • MEL decision. Give the MEL extract (E7) plus a scenario; can the aircraft dispatch? Learners argue using the category, the numbers, and the conditions.
  • Voicemail role-play. One student leaves a 20-second shift-handover voicemail; the partner writes the tech-log entry from it. Real line-station task.

4 · CEFR B2 speaking rubric — role-plays (§6)

Use for the four role-play cards. Five criteria, three bands. B2 is the target; note where a pair drops to B1 or reaches C1 so feedback is concrete. Score each criterion, then give one "keep" and one "change".

CriterionB1 — approachingB2 — targetC1 — beyond
Range
(vocabulary)		 Relies on "broken/problem"; few bank terms; searches for words. Uses the field accurately — leak, chafing, intermittent, play, unserviceable — inside word partners. Precise and flexible; ranges into rectify/borescope/BITE and paraphrases smoothly when a word is missing.
Accuracy
(grammar)		 Present/past simple for live faults; dropped articles; for/since guessed. Present perfect for live defects mostly correct; for/since usually right; slips self-corrected. Consistent present perfect and participles; errors rare and non-impeding.
Fluency Frequent pauses; short, halting turns. Keeps going at a steady pace; hesitation to plan language, not to find basic words. Smooth, effortless; pace natural even under the "noisy hangar" constraint.
Interaction Waits to be asked; few follow-up questions. Questions the vague report; asks for symptom/location/condition; confirms and recasts partner. Manages the exchange — probes, summarises, redirects when the partner is vague.
Coherence Findings listed with no order; fact and guess mixed. Organises symptom → location → condition; keeps observation apart from assumption. Signposts clearly ("so the observation is…, but the cause is only an assumption") and links ideas with connectors.

Quick pass mark: B2 on Interaction + Coherence and at least B2 on two of the other three.
