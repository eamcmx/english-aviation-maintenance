§0 · warm-up · the situation

A tyre to change, and a manual the size of a wall

A new technician must change a nose-gear tyre. The task exists — but where does it live? Which chapter, which page block, which task card?

In pairs, 60 seconds: Where would you start looking? Collect answers, then reveal the professional habit — a working mechanic does not search the manual, they go straight to a number.

§1 · the ATA numbering idea

One number, read left to right

The number narrows from the whole system down to the exact job:

32 chapter — the system (landing gear)
41 subject — the nose wheel / tyre
01 task — the exact job
401 page block — the type of work (removal & installation)

32-41-01
page block 401

Same on every aircraft type. Block 401 always means removal and installation. The IPC shares these chapter numbers, so the AMM and IPC cross-reference each other.

"In the AMM, every task hides behind an ATA chapter number"

Listen twice — closed, then open.

§2 · listening · Dialogue 2

Effectivity first — then warnings, then cautions

LEADAlways read the effectivity first. It tells you which aircraft the task applies to.
LEADA warning protects people from injury or death.
LEADA caution protects the aircraft or the equipment from damage. People first, hardware second.

Gist question: what must you read before you start?

Drill the mnemonic aloud: "people first, hardware second." A note only informs — it is not a safety order. Old revision → check the date.

§2 · interactive · sort the safety boxes

Warning, caution or note?

Read the line. Vote as a class, then reveal the reason.

Answer in a full sentence: "It protects people, so it is a warning." Watch the little -s — it is the grammar of the next block.

§2 · listening · Dialogue 3

"A lot of documents talking to each other"

MENTORThe work order tells you what to do, but not how to do it.
MENTORAn airworthiness directive is a mandatory order from the authority. You cannot ignore it.
MENTORThe MEL says whether the aircraft may fly with that item unserviceable.

Gist question: work order vs AMM — what is the difference?

Service bulletin = recommended, changes the AMM once embodied. Airworthiness directive = mandatory. MEL = may we still fly?

§2 · listening · Dialogue 4 · the announcement

Shift handover, Bay 3 — catch the numbers

LEADAMM revision forty-seven went live overnight. Pull the current task cards from the terminal.
LEADThe effectivity on chapter thirty-two has changed for two of our aircraft.
LEADA new service bulletin has arrived for the nose-wheel assembly. Read the applicability before you touch that job.

A good handover says what changed, what to check, what to do. It is the model for today's writing task.

Mind the confusable pairs:

Collocations: raise a work order · embody a service bulletin · comply with an airworthiness directive · consult the MEL

§4 · grammar in context

Present simple for facts — and the little -s

We describe how the manual set works with permanent facts, so we use the present simple:

"The AMM lists every task."
"A warning protects people."

Add -s for it / one thing: the AMM, the chapter, a warning.

No -s for I / you / we or plural: "The two manuals cover…"

Question / negative: "Does the effectivity match?" · "The note does not order you to stop."

§4 · interactive · add the -s

Pick the form — then say the whole sentence

Choose the correct present-simple verb, then read the full sentence aloud.

Alternate +s (the AMM, the chapter) with no -s (we, the two manuals). Extend: turn it into a question — "Does the AMM list every task?"

§4 · first-language watch · Latvian & Russian

Three errors to expect — and correct fast

✗ dropped articleAMM lists every task.
✓ add "the"The AMM lists every task.
✗ missing -sThe manual list every task.
✓ third person -sThe manual lists every task.
✗ continuous for a factThe chapter is covering the landing gear.
✓ simple for a factThe chapter covers the landing gear.

Latvian and Russian have no articles and do not mark the third-person -s, and their aspect systems differ — so all three errors are predictable, not careless.

§7 · writing · Simplified Technical English

Write the handover note — STE style

MSN 0451 needs a nose-wheel tyre change. Tell the next shift where the task is, which revision to use, and what to check first.

Mention the effectivity, the warning and the tech log. 60–80 words.

Simplified Technical English:

  • Short sentences, one idea each.
  • Present simple.
  • Approved terms only — no synonyms.

Model start: "The task is AMM 32-41-01, page block 401. Use revision 47."
