English for Aviation Maintenance · Unit 18 · CEFR B2 · 90 minutes

Reporting inspection findings

Borescope and visual. Describe a defect's type, size, location and severity — then compare it against the manual's serviceable limit and recommend accept, repair or refer.

Scenario · Riga hangar · A320 YL-BBX · borescope of engine 2  ·  Grammar · comparatives for limits

By the end of the class

Three things every technician can do

1 · Describe precisely. Give the type, the size, the location and the severity of a defect, in a clear order.

2 · Compare against the limit. Judge the finding against the manual's serviceable limit, using a comparative with than.

3 · Recommend, with a reason. Say accept, repair or refer — and always give the reason.

How the 90 minutes runs:

05 warm-up · 15 dialogues · 15 vocabulary
15 grammar · 20 exercises · 15 role-plays · 05 writing set

Everything happens inside one scene: the borescope of engine two on YL-BBX.

The scene

An afternoon borescope in the Riga hangar

An A320, registration YL-BBX, is in for a scheduled check. The work order asks for a borescope inspection of engine two.

Jānis looks inside the engine with the borescope. He finds a crack on a turbine blade. He reports it to his lead, Elīna.

Then they open the maintenance manual and compare the finding against the serviceable limit.

REPORT ORDER

Type → Location → Dimension → Severity

Say them in this order every time. Then compare against the limit, then recommend.

Warm-up · talk to the class

Two questions before we start

1. What damage can you find inside an engine, or on a panel?

Collect words: crack, corrosion, dent, scratch, pitting…

2. Before you accept any damage, what do you always check?

Steer to: the limit in the maintenance manual.

Write their words on the board. You will meet every one of them again in the next fifteen minutes.

Vocabulary · the defects

Name the damage

Click a defect. Read the plain meaning aloud, then ask a student to use it in one short sentence.

Vocabulary · describing & judging

Words that make the report exact

Location — leading edge (front edge, meets the air first), tip, near the fastener holes.

Dimension — the size in millimetres. "about 4 mm along the edge."

Severity — shallow / deep, light / heavy, minor.

serviceable limit — the largest damage the manual allows.

within limits — inside the maximum; still serviceable.

out of limits — larger than allowed; not serviceable.

Collocations to drill: run a borescope inspection · check the serviceable limit · refer to engineering.

Dialogue 1 · listen, then read

Describe the borescope finding

Elīna: Give me the type of defect first, and then the location.

Jānis: It is a crack. The crack is on the leading edge of a turbine blade.

Elīna: Which stage is it, and which blade number?

Jānis: Stage one, blade number twelve. The crack starts near the tip.

Elīna: Now the dimension. How long is it on the screen?

Jānis: About four millimetres along the edge.

Elīna: And the severity — does it look deep?

Jānis: It looks shallow, although the line is very clear.

Task for the room: find the four report parts — type, location, dimension, severity.

Drill · fix the order

Put the report in order

Then go round the room: each student describes one defect in the four-part order, out loud.

Grammar focus · comparatives for limits

You compare every finding with a limit

Short adjective: add -er + than.
long → longer than · deep → deeper than

Spelling: big → bigger · wide → wider · shallow → shallower

Long adjective: use more + than.
severe → more severe than

bigger than the limit → out of limits

smaller than the limit → within limits

The comparative is not decoration. It carries the whole decision.

Grammar · errors to expect (Latvian / Russian)

Three traps — and the fix

✗ The crack is more longer than the limit.  →  ✓ The crack is longer than the limit. — never "more" with an -er word.

✗ The dent is deeper as the limit. / from the limit.  →  ✓ deeper than the limit. — English uses "than".

✗ The pit is deeper then allowed.  →  ✓ deeper than allowed. — "than", not "then".

See it · finding against the limit

Within limits or out of limits?

Set the crack to 4 mm, the limit to 3 mm. Ask the class to say the sentence before you reveal it.

Dialogue 2 · listen, then read

Compare it against the serviceable limit

Elīna: What is the limit for a crack on the leading edge?

Jānis: The manual allows three millimetres.

Elīna: Our crack is four, so it is longer than the limit.

Jānis: Four is longer than three, so the crack is out of limits.

Jānis: The limit for a fan-blade dent is two millimetres.

Elīna: Our dent is one point five. Is that within limits?

Jānis: One point five is shallower than two, so it is within limits.

Elīna: Good. Always compare before you decide.

Reading · maintenance manual (AMM 72-00)

The serviceable limits page

Crack (leading edge): up to 3 mm. Longer → out of limits → refer.

Dent (fan blade): up to 2 mm deep.

Scratch: up to 0.2 mm. Shallower → within limits → accept.

Corrosion: light → blend within limits. Deep pitting → out of limits.

Rule: always record the dimension. Never estimate by feel — a small error can change the decision.

Dialogue 3 · listen, then read

Recommend: accept, repair or refer

Jānis: Light corrosion near the fastener holes. The pitting is shallow.

Elīna: For light corrosion we can blend, as long as we stay within limits.

Jānis: The scratch is 0.1 mm, and the limit is 0.2.

Elīna: Shallower than the limit. We accept it, then, and record it.

Jānis: But the crack is out of limits, so we cannot accept it.

Elīna: For the crack we refer the finding to engineering.

Jānis: So: accept the scratch, blend the corrosion, refer the crack.

Elīna: And always write the reason for each decision.

Decide · accept, repair or refer

Where does each finding go?

Ask students to predict the box before they click. Every decision needs a comparative reason.

Exercises · on the student page

Stage E1–E8 — do not just set them loose

E1 · E2 vocabulary — gap-fill & matching. First, 5 min, alone.

E3 · E4 grammar — dialogue & comparatives. Model E4 on the board first.

E5 · E7 reading — BIR report & AMM limits. Quiet reading time.

E6 error-spotting — eight comparative mistakes. Review this one as a class.

E8 listening — replay Dialogue 3.

They press Check; the page marks each item. Circulate and note common errors.

Exercise block ≈ 20 minutes.

Speaking · four role-play cards (pairs)

Run the role-plays

Card 1 — Describe the finding

ATechnician reports type, location, dimension, severity.

Card 2 — Compare with the limit

A/BOne reads the limit; the other compares with a comparative and decides.

Card 3 — Recommend

A/BAccept, repair or refer for a scratch, corrosion and a crack — with a reason.

Card 4 — Shift handover

ASummarise the main finding, the comparison and the recommendation.

Swap roles after each card. Circulate and recast the three comparative errors softly. Score with the rubric in the key.

Writing · a borescope finding report

Write it in Simplified Technical English

Task: a combustion-chamber borescope finds a 5 mm crack on a liner panel. The limit is 4 mm. A nearby bracket has light corrosion within the blend limit. Write the report — 100–130 words.

STE habits:

• One idea per sentence. • Short sentences. • Active voice.

The borescope finds a crack. The crack is longer than the limit, so it is out of limits.

Cover: type · location · dimension · severity · a comparative with than · a recommendation and a reason.

Wrap-up · exit ticket

Describe · compare · recommend

Give each student one finding and one limit. They say the full sentence — comparative + decision + reason.

"The crack is shorter than the limit, so it is within limits — accept."

Terms are for training only. On the job, always work from the current approved manual, task card and company procedures.
