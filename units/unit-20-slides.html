English for Aviation Maintenance · Unit 20 · Simulation day · CEFR B2

Building a full documentation trail

One defect — a worn cabin door seal — from find to fix. Assemble the tech log, work order, task card, IPC and AMM into one connected trail, write it in Simplified Technical English, and defend it to a mock auditor.

90 minutes · balanced skills · vocabulary field: documentation trail · grammar: linking words for cohesion

By the end · how the 90 minutes run

Three things every technician can do by the bell

1 — Build. Produce a coherent, STE-compliant paper trail for one defect, from find to fix.

2 — Cross-reference. Link the task card, the IPC and the tech log correctly, so nothing is orphaned.

3 — Defend. Present the finished trail and justify its clarity to a mock auditor.

The day in order:

Listen (3 dialogues) → vocabulary bank → grammar: linking words → controlled practice E1–E8 → role-plays → write and defend the trail.

The role-play and the written trail are the real performance. Use the speaking rubric on the teacher key to grade.

§0 · the situation

Today the trail matters more than the tool

You have one defect on aircraft YL-BAM: a worn cabin door seal. Every certified repair leaves a connected chain of records — and an independent reader must be able to follow it without asking you a single question.

In aviation an incomplete trail is a defect in itself, even when the repair was perfect. A missing signature or a broken cross-reference can ground an aircraft for hours. Today you build the trail with the same discipline you give the repair.

§1 · the documentation trail

Eight records, one connected chain

Tech log — record the fault → Work order — open the job → Task card — list the steps → IPC — part number → AMM — repair steps & torque → Dual inspection — a second check → Sign-off block — who did the work → Release to service — safe to fly.

What holds it together is the cross-reference: each record points to the next by a matching reference number. That link is what gives you traceability — find to fix.

§1 · Interactive · put the trail in order

Trail builder — click the records into sequence

Click each record in the order it happens on the job. The chain animates as you get each one right.

The order is a chain of reasons: you cannot list steps before the task card exists, and you cannot release the aircraft before the dual inspection.

§2 · Listening · Dialogue 1

Laying out the documentation trail

AYou write the defect in the tech log, because every fault starts there.
BHow do I find the correct part for the seal?
AYou look in the IPC. The IPC is the illustrated parts catalogue.
BThere are so many documents. How do they stay connected?
AThrough cross-reference. Each document points to the next one, so the trail is complete.

tech log — the official log where every fault is first written down.

cross-reference — a link that sends the reader from one document to the exact place in another.

CCQ: Which document opens the job? Where are the repair steps written?

§2 · Listening · Dialogue 2

Adding traceability to the parts

AFor a tracked part, yes. The serial number identifies that one single item.
BThis seal came in a sealed bag with a batch number.
AThen record the batch number, since the seal has no serial number.
BAfter the work, a colleague checked it. Is that the dual inspection?
AYes. A dual inspection means a second person confirms the safety-critical work.

serial number — the unique code for one single tracked item, and no other.

batch number — the code on a group of identical parts made together.

CCQ: The seal has no serial number — which number do you record, and why?

§2 · Interactive · traceability of a part

Serial or batch? — record the right number

Set the two switches for the part in your hand. The label shows which number gives full traceability.

Always record the part number. Then a serial number if the item has one — otherwise the batch number. Say the reason with a linking word.

§2 · Listening · Dialogue 3

Defending the trail to the auditor

AHow do I know which part you fitted?
BThe cross-reference takes you from the tech log to the task card and the part number.
AI can see two sign-off blocks. Why are there two?
BOne is for the work, and the other is for the inspection. Therefore both are signed.
AVery clear. As a result, I can trust this trail without asking you more.

audit trail — the complete linked record an auditor follows to check the work.

sign-off block — the box where a person signs to confirm the work.

This is the model for the mock-auditor role-play. Steal B's moves.

§4 · Grammar · linking words for cohesion

Linking words carry the reasoning

Give a reason: because · since · as
We replaced the seal because it was worn.

Show a result: so · therefore · as a result · consequently
The seal was worn, so we replaced it.

Mark a sequence: first · then · after that · before · after
First we opened the panel. Then we removed the seal.

Show a contrast: however · although · but
The repair was correct. However, the trail was not complete.

The auditor grades the reasoning, not just the facts. A precise connector saves a long spoken explanation.

The rule for the whole lesson: one linking word per join, and keep the sentence short.

§4 · Interactive · pick the connector

Read the whole pair first — meaning decides the connector. This is exactly what E4 practises.

§4 · Latvian / Russian interference

Three joins to fix on sight

✗ Because the seal was worn, so we replaced it.
✓ Because the seal was worn, we replaced it. (one linking word, not two)
✗ The valve leaked therefore we removed it.
✓ The valve leaked. Therefore, we removed it. (new sentence, or use "so")
✗ The flight was delayed because of the seal was worn.
✓ The flight was delayed because the seal was worn. (because + clause; because of + noun)

Why it happens: both L1s allow a paired connector and mark cause differently. These three are the top marking priorities in the written trail and in E6.

§5 · Interactive · be the auditor

Cross-reference checker — do the numbers match?

Pick a scenario, then click each link to check the reference number. Green means it matches; red means the link is broken.

A broken cross-reference grounds an aircraft although the repair was perfect. This is the auditor's real question — and the role-play.

§6 · Speaking · four role-play cards

Present the trail — and defend it

Card 1 · Mentor & junior

Explain the trail from the tech log to the sign-off block, in order.

Card 2 · Technician & stores

Confirm the part number, and the serial or the batch number.

Card 3 · Mock auditor & technician

Test every cross-reference and every sign-off block; find any broken link.

Card 4 · Peer review

Find one broken link and one weak connector, then fix them together.

Success = every claim joined with a clear linking word. Grade with the B2 speaking rubric (teacher key). Swap roles after five minutes.

§7 · Writing · build it yourself

Write the trail for today's defect

Defect: worn cabin door seal on YL-BAM, entered in the tech log (TL-0912).

WO-4471 raised → task card TC-52-11-07 → part number from the IPC → new seal, batch B-2231, no serial number → torqued to the AMM value → dual inspection → both sign-off blocks → release to service.

Six to eight short lines. A linking word in most lines. STE habits: one idea per sentence, keep the articles, name every reference number.

Model two lines on the board, then peer-review with Card 4 and the self-check list.

Recap · exit ticket

One defect · one clear trail · find to fix

Tech log → work order → task card → IPC + AMM → dual inspection → sign-off → release. Cross-reference every record by a matching number — that is traceability. Part number, plus a serial or a batch number. Join every step with one linking word, in short sentences — that is how you defend it.

Exit ticket: name the first record you make, and one linking word you would use to justify the repair.
