WRITING Tech-log entry — annotated model answer

Target: 60–90 words, three parts, Simplified Technical English habits.

Fault found. BITE gave fault code 27-FC-114, display data loss. WDM traced to connector D-27A, data pins 4 and 5. Continuity check found an open circuit on pin 4. A burnt splice and a loose terminal were in the harness.

Action taken. The splice was repaired. The terminal was re-seated. The connector was re-fitted. The BITE was run again and gave a clean pass. The LRU is serviceable; the display unit was not changed.

Next action. Do one functional test before the first flight. If the fault returns, treat it as intermittent and re-inspect the splice.

Word count: 78 words — inside the 60–90 band.

Why it scores well (STE habits)

One idea per sentence. Each action is its own short sentence ("The splice was repaired."), so the next reader cannot mis-read a compound instruction.
Short sentences. Every sentence stays well under 20 words, which keeps the entry scannable on a busy line.
Facts named exactly. The fault code, connector D-27A, pins 4 and 5, and the LRU status are all stated — the next technician needs no guesswork.
The next action is explicit. "Do one functional test before the first flight" tells the morning team precisely what remains.
Acceptable variation. Learners may use the active voice throughout ("I repaired the splice. I re-seated the terminal."). STE prefers the active voice for instructions; the passive is acceptable in a record of completed work. Both are marked correct.
