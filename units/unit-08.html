00The scenario

You are on the A320 line, and the aircraft has just arrived with a forty-five minute turnaround. You receive the handover from the departing technician, and immediately you begin the walkaround. You discover a defect, so the remaining ground time is now extremely tight. You must decide quickly, although you cannot protect the departure slot indefinitely. You consult the MEL, you request the component, and you rectify or defer the defect accordingly. Afterwards you document every action in the tech log, and you sign the release to service before pushback, provided that the aircraft is fully serviceable.

A turnaround is essentially a race against the clock, because the ground time is limited and the departure slot is fixed. Effective teams succeed by communicating precisely and by confirming that every message was understood correctly. Throughout this simulation you rehearse the complete sequence, although you must use time clauses with before and after to arrange each action in the correct order. Listen to each recording initially, and examine the transcript afterwards, because an accomplished listener recognises the sequence before encountering it in writing.

DIALOGUE 1 The handover at the start of the turnaround

Transcript

AHi Raivis, here is my handover on the A320, so please listen before boarding commences.

BGo ahead, Anna. The turnaround is extremely tight, because we only have forty-five minutes of ground time.

AUnderstood, so I will be brief. The aircraft arrived punctually, and the departure slot remains available.

BExcellent. Did you complete the walkaround before the passengers disembarked?

AYes, I did, although I discovered one defect during the walkaround, so I must demonstrate it now.

BSorry, repeat that. You identified a defect precisely where?

AOn the right engine cowl, one access panel is loose, and two fasteners are missing.

BUnderstood, a loose panel and two missing fasteners. Did you register it in the tech log?

AYes, the defect is documented in the tech log, and I opened a task card for the panel.

BPerfect. So, before I certify anything, I must secure that panel, correct?

AExactly. Install the fasteners and secure the panel before the aircraft proceeds for pushback.

BCertainly. After I secure the panel, I will complete your remaining walkaround items too.

AThank you. The refuelling and the oil are finished, so only the panel remains open.

BA clear handover, Anna. Let me repeat it back, so we both agree completely.

APlease do, because a rapid turnaround deteriorates whenever the message is not confirmed.

BRight: secure the panel, install two fasteners, then complete the walkaround before pushback.

DIALOGUE 2 The decision: rectify, defer, or request a part

Transcript

ARaivis, the panel is more serious than we anticipated, because one hinge is cracked.

BA cracked hinge changes everything. Is this a no-go item, or can we carry it?

AI am consulting the MEL currently, so please wait before you contact the captain.

BFine. If the MEL permits it, we can carry it as a deferred defect.

AThe MEL permits one cracked hinge, but only after we install a temporary clip.

BDo we possess the clip in stores, or is this becoming an AOG?

AStores holds one clip, therefore it is not an AOG, which is excellent news.

BGreat. Then we need not rectify the hinge completely before this departure.

ACorrect. We install the clip immediately, and we rectify the hinge at the next base check.

BUnderstood. So the plan is: install the clip, then defer the hinge in the tech log.

AYes, and I will update the task card as soon as the clip arrives.

BLet me confirm the component. Could you read me the part number, please?

AThe part number is Charlie four four zero two, and stores will deliver it before the fuellers finish.

BGood, and is the clip serviceable, with a tag and a valid certificate?

AYes, it is serviceable, therefore we are authorised to install it and dispatch the aircraft.

BExcellent. Once we install the clip, we can proceed to the release.

DIALOGUE 3 Act and document before dispatch

Transcript

AThe clip is installed, Raivis, but the panel warning remains illuminated in the flight deck.

BThen we must troubleshoot it, because we cannot dispatch with an active warning.

AAgreed. I will run a BITE test before we disturb any wiring.

BGood idea. What does the BITE readout indicate about the panel sensor?

AThe BITE shows an open circuit, so one sensor connector is probably loose.

BRepeat that, please. An open circuit on the sensor connector, correct?

ACorrect. I will reseat the connector immediately, and then I will cancel the warning.

BProceed quickly, because we forfeit the slot if we cause a delay.

ACompleted. The connector is reseated, and the warning extinguished after I reset the system.

BExcellent. Before I sign the release to service, confirm the tech log is complete.

AThe tech log is complete, so the clip and the reseat are both documented.

BGood. After you close the task card, I will sign the release to service.

AThe task card is closed, and the aircraft is now serviceable for dispatch.

BSigning now. As soon as I sign, inform the tug that we are ready.

AThe release is signed, so I will request pushback and avoid any delay.

BWell done, Anna. That is a clean turnaround, and the aircraft departs on the slot.

02Vocabulary bank — line operations

These are the essential specialist terms for a turnaround. Each term includes a plain meaning, one example sentence at your level, and one frequent collocation. Memorise the collocation, because it represents the natural way technicians use the word on the line.

03Grammar in context — time clauses with before / after

A turnaround is essentially a sequence of actions in a fixed order, so you must indicate what happens first and what follows immediately afterwards. English expresses this using time clauses, with connectors like before, after, as soon as, once, until and when. Examine these lines from the dialogues, and notice the identical pattern in each example.

“Fit the fasteners and close the panel before the aircraft goes for pushback.” — D1
“After you close the task card, I will sign the release to service.” — D3
“I will update the task card as soon as the clip arrives.” — D2

The rule is brief, although learners frequently apply it incorrectly. In a time clause about the future, use the present tense, and never will. The main clause can still contain will or a direct instruction. Therefore you say “before the aircraft departs”, and never “before the aircraft will depart”.

FunctionStructureExample
Do the first action firstMain clause + before + presentClose the panel before the aircraft departs.
Do the second action nextAfter + present, + main clauseAfter you fit the clip, sign the log.
Do it the moment it happensAs soon as + present, + willAs soon as the part arrives, we will fit it.
Wait for a conditionUntil / once + presentWe cannot dispatch until the warning clears.

Three common mistakes (Latvian and Russian speakers)

✗ Fit the panel before the aircraft will depart.
✓ Fit the panel before the aircraft departs.
Why: after before, after or as soon as, use the present tense, although Latvian and Russian often use a future form here.
✗ After I will finish the walkaround, I sign the log.
✓ After I finish the walkaround, I sign the log.
Why: the time clause takes the present tense, so the future will belongs only in the main clause.
✗ Check the MEL before to defer the defect.
✓ Check the MEL before you defer the defect.
Why: before needs a clause or an -ing form, not the infinitive, because Latvian and Russian frequently use the infinitive here.

05Speaking: run the turnaround

Work in pairs, so one person represents the technician and the other represents the controller or the stores clerk. Use the useful phrases, and always confirm that the message was understood, because a rapid exchange deteriorates whenever nobody confirms it. Exchange roles after each card.

Role-play card 1 — Take the handover

Situation The aircraft has landed with a tight turnaround, and there is one open defect.
Your role Incoming technician taking the handover.
Your goal Get the ground time, the slot, the defect and the open task card, and repeat it back to confirm.

What is the ground time?Where is the defect?Say that again, please.Let me repeat it back.Is that in the tech log?

06Writing: a turnaround tech log entry

Write a concise tech log entry for the turnaround, and use Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100), because short sentences and approved words are considerably easier to read on the line. Include one instruction per sentence, and keep every sentence under twenty words. Use the present tense in every time clause, and follow the checklist below.

Your task

Write 80–110 words to record the turnaround. Give the defect, the MEL decision and the action, and say what you fitted. Then write the sequence with time clauses: what you do before pushback, and what you sign after you close the task card. Keep every sentence short, clear and in the right order.

TECH LOG ENTRY — A320 — TURNAROUND STAND 12

Defect: right cowl hinge cracked. Found on the walkaround before boarding.

Decision: the MEL allows the defect. Carry it as a deferred defect.

Action: fit the temporary clip, part number C-4402. The clip is serviceable.
