WRITINGModel tech-log entry — annotated

Target: 60–90 words, Simplified Technical English, past simple for every finished step, all documents named. One acceptable model (learner answers may differ in wording):

TECH LOG · YL-BAX · Number 2 display

Reported a recurring fault. The display blanked during taxi. Fault code DU-204 was set. We ran the BITE test; it passed and stored no fault. We cleaned the connector and swapped the LRU, but the fault came back. We called OEM support. Their recommendation was to inspect the loom against the wiring diagram. We found a chafed wire near the connector. We repaired the loom and raised work order WO-77413. Noted service bulletin SB 34-118. A follow-up check is due on the next taxi.

Word count ≈ 85 — inside 60–90.

Past simple. Every finished step uses the past form: blanked, was set, ran, passed, cleaned, swapped, came back, called, found, repaired, raised, noted. No present tense creeps in.
Articles. Known parts carry the: the display, the connector, the LRU, the loom, the wiring diagram. This is the exact spot Latvian/Russian speakers drop the article.
STE — short sentences. One idea per sentence, all under ~18 words. The semicolon in "ran the BITE test; it passed" keeps two linked facts tight without a long clause.
Documents named. Fault code (DU-204), work order (WO-77413), service bulletin (SB 34-118), and the tech log itself — the four references the record must hold.
Recommendation recorded faithfully. "…inspect the loom against the wiring diagram" is the OEM's exact advice, not a paraphrase — a paraphrase can introduce a dangerous ambiguity.
