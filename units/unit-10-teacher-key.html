Latvian & Russian interference — why each error happens

Both first languages are here. The three high-frequency errors in this unit all come from concrete differences between those systems and English. Recast gently in the moment; do not stop to lecture the rule.

1 · will in the if-clause

✗ "If the BITE will show 34…"  →  ✓ "If the BITE shows 34…"

Why: In Russian, the real (open) conditional uses a future verb in the if-clause: «Если BITE покажет (future) 34…». Latvian similarly allows a future in the subordinate clause. Learners map that straight onto English and insert will. English blocks will in the if-clause and uses the present for a rule that is always true — the exact structure this unit teaches.

2 · dropped third-person -s

✗ "If the self-test show a fault…"  →  ✓ "If the self-test shows a fault…"

Why: English is unusual in marking only the third-person singular with -s while leaving the other persons bare; the ending carries little information, so it is easily dropped. Russian and Latvian both conjugate the verb for every person, so learners are not used to singling out he/she/it — and because English drops the ending everywhere else, the odd -s feels redundant and disappears. Drill it on the unit's own verbs: shows, starts, passes, repeats, fails, clears, looks, says.

3 · dropped articles (the / a)

✗ "If BITE shows fault code, technician reads it."  →  ✓ "If the BITE shows a fault code, the technician reads it."

Why: Neither Russian nor Latvian has articles; definiteness is shown by word order, case or context, not by a separate word. So there is no habit of choosing the vs a vs zero, and learners omit both. In technical English the article also signals "this specific unit" (the connector) vs "any one" (a serviceable unit) — a meaning difference worth flagging on the readouts.

Also watch (lower frequency): "make a test" for run/do a test (calque); missing do in negatives ("if the reset not clears" → "if the reset does not clear"); flat intonation on yes/no comprehension checks. Model the collocations run a self-test, run a ground test explicitly.

Extension activities

A · Build-a-code relay (fast finishers)

In threes, one learner invents a plausible code (ATA chapter + number), the others must name the system and a suspect LRU and say the if-sentence. Extend the ATA set: 21 air-conditioning, 24 electrical power, 32 landing gear, 49 APU. Keeps the fixed-logic pattern alive with new content.

B · Confirm-or-clear debate

Give one pair a code where the evidence conflicts (self-test clean, but CMC lists it twice; connector slightly marked). They must argue the follow-up using zero conditionals and although / unless / as soon as. Trains the interaction and coherence bands of the rubric.

C · STE rewrite clinic

Project a wordy, non-STE log entry (long sentences, vague verbs). Pairs rewrite it into Simplified Technical English: one idea per sentence, approved verbs, present-tense if-clauses. Compare versions and vote on the clearest.

D · Handover voicemail (production listening→speaking)

Each learner records a 45-second maintenance-control handover for a partner (like track 4): registration in phonetic letters, code, system, suspect LRU, one instruction. The partner writes the tech-log line from it. Marries spoken-figure listening with STE writing.

E · AI colleague — role reversal

Advanced learners instruct the AI partner to make a deliberate grammar slip each turn; the learner must catch and recast it. Flips the Consultant-stage dynamic and sharpens error awareness.

CEFR B2 speaking rubric — for the role-plays

Use during stage 70–84. Five criteria, three reference bands (5 = strong B2 / approaching C1; 3 = solid B2; 1 = below B2 / B1). Score each criterion, note one example of good use and one recast per pair.

Criterion5 · strong3 · at level1 · below
RangeUses the full term set and collocations flexibly (isolate, intermittent, no fault found, serviceable); varied connectors (although, unless, as soon as, in case).Uses the core terms and simple connectors; some repetition but meaning clear.Very limited vocabulary; leans on "thing/problem"; avoids specialist terms.
AccuracyZero conditionals consistently correct (present + present, -s, articles); errors rare and self-repaired.Zero conditional mostly correct; occasional dropped -s or article that does not block meaning.Frequent will in the if-clause, missing -s/articles; errors impede meaning.
FluencySpeaks at a natural pace with few pauses; reads the code aloud smoothly.Generally keeps going; some hesitation when reaching for a term.Long, frequent pauses; halting; reading the code aloud breaks down.
InteractionInitiates, agrees/disagrees, and negotiates the follow-up; asks the partner to justify ("which unit first, and why?").Responds and takes turns appropriately; agrees the action with some prompting.Waits to be led; minimal response; does not take up or hand over the turn.
CoherenceSequences the logic clearly (code → system → LRU → action) with signposting (initially, then, so, afterwards).Logical order mostly clear; linking present but basic (and, so).Ideas listed without order; the cause→action chain is hard to follow.

Reporting: a simple mean of the five gives an at-a-glance level; but treat Accuracy on the zero conditional as the diagnostic criterion for this unit — it is the language target. Feed back two named strengths and one recast to the whole class.

Quiz key — quiz-rich.xml (23 items, 5 types)

Split by skill: Listening 7 · Reading 5 · Use of English 7 · Vocabulary 4. Every item carries general feedback and per-option feedback in the file; per-item rationale below. Correct answers are never the longest option (anti-bias). No Cloze.

Listening (7) — replay the named dialogue / voicemail
  • L1 multichoice · It lists the identical fault three times.Confirmation comes from repetition in the CMC history (Dialogue 1). Clearing automatically or an ECAM warning are the wrong mechanisms.
  • L2 truefalse · FalseIn Dialogue 2 the ground test runs clean ("no fault found"); it does not repeat the code — that is why the fault is intermittent.
  • L3 numerical · 3Dialogue 1: the CMC lists the identical fault three separate times today.
  • L4 numerical · 3Dialogue 2: the CMC shows the identical code on three separate flights.
  • L5 multichoice · The same code, 27-93.Dialogue 3: after the reset the BITE still shows 27-93, so the fault is genuine.
  • L6 truefalse · FalseThe bonus voicemail says do not sign the readout as clear yet — the fault is still open.
  • L7 numerical · 34The voicemail code is 34-12; it starts with ATA chapter 34 (navigation). Tests comprehension of a spoken figure.
Reading (5) — short maintenance-genre text in the stem
  • R1 multichoice · For up to 3 calendar days.MEL stem: ADIRU 1 is a Category B item, and the repair-interval key gives Category B = 3 calendar days. Distractors misread the category letters (C = 10, D = 120).
  • R2 truefalse · TrueThe work-order stem states the self-test repeated the code, so the fault is confirmed, not cleared.
  • R3 multichoice · Inspect the connector before you replace the unit.The task-card sequence puts a connector inspection ahead of any LRU change.
  • R4 truefalse · FalseThe Service Bulletin is described as recommended, not mandatory; only an AD is mandatory.
  • R5 multichoice · Because the fault is intermittent.The AMM extract explains that a clean ground test does not clear an intermittent fault; the history still counts.
Use of English (7) — zero conditional & bank terms (short answer)
  • U1 shortanswer · startsPresent simple with third-person -s; no will in the if-clause.
  • U2 shortanswer · passes-es after -ss; present tense for the fixed rule.
  • U3 shortanswer · If the BITE shows 34, the system is navigation.Transformation: remove will, keep present. Tests the core interference error.
  • U4 shortanswer · repeatsThird-person -s on the CMC.
  • U5 shortanswer · lineLRU = line replaceable unit — the missing word.
  • U6 shortanswer · serviceableFit to use and legal to fly; accepts the exact term only.
  • U7 shortanswer · does not clear (doesn't clear)Negative present with do-support; catches the "not clears" calque.
Vocabulary (4) — matching
  • V1 matching · BITE→self-check; LRU→box you change on the line; CMC→collects fault codes; intermittent→comes and goes; serviceable→fit to use.Core term↔meaning set.
  • V2 matching · self-test→a unit's internal check; readout→message on the screen; connector→plug to the wiring; isolate→narrow to one unit; no fault found→nothing wrong now.Second term↔meaning set.
  • V3 matching · read→the BITE; run→a ground test; isolate→the fault; open→the CMC; fit→a serviceable unit.Collocation (verb↔object) matching — the chunks taught in the bank.
  • V4 matching · 34→navigation; 22→autoflight; 27→flight controls; 23→communications.ATA chapter↔system — the fixed logic the unit rests on.

Suggested pass mark: 80% (formative). Re-teach target: if a class misses L2/R5 (intermittent) or U3/U7 (the will and do-support errors), revisit Dialogue 2 and the grammar widget.
