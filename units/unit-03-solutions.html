E3 · Put the entry in order

The correct working sequence for raising the defect, 1–8.

OrderStepWhy here
1Read the pilot report and note the raised snag.You start from what the crew reported.
2Question the pilot to find the real symptom.A vague report must be made precise before anything else.
3Give the exact location of the fault.Location is fixed before you inspect.
4State the condition: soft pedal during landing.When the fault appears completes the picture.
5Inspect the area and describe the leak.Only now do you look and record what you see.
6Separate the observation from any assumption.Keep the guess out before you write.
7Mark the unit unserviceable until repair.Ground the item once a real defect is confirmed.
8Enter the defect in the tech log and sign it.The record and signature come last.

E4 · Grammar transformation — present perfect

Rewrite in the present perfect: has/have + past participle.

#AnswerWhy
1The panel has developed cracks.develop → developed; the cracks are still there now.
2I have found a hydraulic leak.Irregular participle find → found; keep the article a.
3The bearing has developed play.Live fault → present perfect.
4We have recorded the defect in the tech log.have with we; record → recorded.
5The line has chafed on the bracket.chafe → chafed; the damage remains.
6The BITE has stored a fault code.store → stored; the code is still logged.
7The technician has not rectified the leak.Negative = has not + participle; the leak is still open.
8The valve has leaked for two flights.for + a length of time; the leak continues now.

Each line has one error: wrong tense, wrong participle, or for/since.

#WrongCorrectedWhy
1The panel has develop cracks.The panel has developed cracks.Participle after has: develop → developed.
2The line leaks since yesterday.The line has leaked since yesterday.Started before, still true → present perfect, not present simple.
3…has leaked since two flights.The valve has leaked for two flights.for + length of time (not since).
4I have find a hydraulic leak.I have found a hydraulic leak.Irregular participle find → found.
5The bearing develop play since the last flight.The bearing has developed play since the last flight.Add has + participle; since + a start point is correct.

Writing · model answer, annotated

Number two engine oil pressure "looks low sometimes"; a small oil leak found at a pipe union, with light chafing on the pipe.

Pilot report: number two engine oil pressure looks low at times. Symptom: the oil pressure has read low intermittently in the cruise. Location: number two engine, at the oil pipe union. Condition: the low reading appears during the cruise, not on the ground. Observation: a small oil leak is present at the union. The pipe shows light chafing. A worn seal is only an assumption, so the cause is not yet confirmed. Action: the engine is unserviceable. Do not release the aircraft until we rectify the leak.

Why this scores well:

  • Symptom, location, condition are each named on their own line — the three-part structure the whole unit trains (LO1).
  • Present perfect for the live fault: has read low. The reading is still happening, so past simple would be wrong.
  • Observation vs assumption kept apart (LO2): the leak and chafing are observations; "a worn seal" is explicitly labelled an assumption and left out of the finding.
  • Rephrased from the pilot's words (LO3): "looks low sometimes" becomes "has read low intermittently in the cruise" — precise, technical, using a bank term.
  • Simplified Technical English: short lines, one idea each, approved verbs, no filler; the unit is marked unserviceable with a clear "do not release" instruction.

Accept any entry that keeps the three parts, uses the present perfect for the live fault, separates observation from assumption, and marks the unit unserviceable. Wording will vary; structure and honesty are the marking points.
