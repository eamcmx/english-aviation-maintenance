Base check to certified release

§1 · the situation

One event, four stages

Briefing → discovery & tests → documentation & release → handover.

Click through the stages. The legal moment is the signature: the certifying staff signs the certificate of release to service, and only then is the aircraft ready for service.

§2 · Dialogue 1 · listen

The work-pack briefing

A There are forty task cards. Each one has a sign-off box. B Is there any airworthiness directive to close? A Yes, one is due — a mandatory check on the rudder fitting. B And a service bulletin? A One, recommended, not mandatory.

Books closed. Play once. Two gist questions:

1) How many task cards?   2) Is the service bulletin mandatory?

Then open the transcript to check. Drill: forty · airworthiness directive · work order.

§2 · Dialogue 1 · language

Coordination phrases to steal

  • Check: “This is the base check, isn't it?”
  • Request: “Please raise a work order for the rudder job.”
  • Confirm: “Understood.” · “Correct.” · “Fine, if time allows.”

60-second pair drill: A checks, B confirms, then swap.

§2 · Dialogue 2 · listen

Defect discovery and tests

A The built-in test equipment shows a fault on the number two fuel pump. B Let us run a functional test first. A The flow drops to zero. The pump is unserviceable. B Can we defer it? Check the minimum equipment list. A It allows three days — but we have the spare, so we rectify now.

Gist: What does the BITE show? Pump status after the test?

Detail: How many days would the MEL allow? Why rectify now? What follows fuel-system work?

§2 · Dialogue 2 · rehearse the chain

From finding to serviceable

finding → test → duplicate inspection → tech log → serviceable.

The repair alone does not close the finding. Skip the tech-log line and the card stays open — the trap in Dialogue 3.

§2 · Dialogue 3 · listen

From documentation to certified release

A Are all the task cards complete? B Thirty-nine have a sign-off. One is still open — the fuel pump card. A The tech log line is missing. Add it now. B Done. All findings are cleared and signed. A Everything is in order. The aircraft is airworthy again.

Gist: How many cards signed at first? What was missing?

Detail: What does the certifying staff check before signing? Write down the exact final line.

§2 · the anchor statement

The line the whole day builds to

“The aircraft is ready for service.”

The aircraft · is (status, not “is being”) · ready for service (not “to service”).

§4 · grammar in context

Status now vs finished action

Status now — present simple

The aircraft is ready for service.
The pump is unserviceable.
The finding is cleared.

Finished action — present perfect passive

The rectification has been carried out.
All findings have been cleared.
The test was carried out (dated).

The release is a legal record — the tense must be exact.

§4 · Latvian & Russian traps

Recast these four, fast

  • Aircraft is ready for service. → The aircraft is ready for service.
  • ready to service → ready for service
  • The pump is being unserviceable. → The pump is unserviceable.
  • The test was carry out. → The test was carried out.

You say the wrong line; the class fires back the right one.

§4 · listening · spoken numbers

Catch the number, say it back

Trap pairs: thirty / thirteen · forty / fourteen.

Times: eighteen hundred = 1800 · oh six thirty = 0630. Limits: three days · ten days · thirty-two degrees. Safe check: “Do you mean one-three, or three-zero?”

§6 · speaking · role-plays

Four cards, four stages

1 · Briefing — shift lead hands over the work pack.

2 · A finding — agree unserviceable; defer or rectify?

3 · Closing — check sign-offs, sign the release.

4 · Handover — report the finished event to the night shift.

Read 30s · act 2 min · swap · act again. Full sentences and the printed phrases. End on card 3's anchor line.

§7 · writing · Simplified Technical English

Write the release paperwork

Part 1 — tech-log line (2–3 sentences). The number two fuel pump was unserviceable. The pump was changed and tested. The finding is cleared.

Part 2 — release. All task cards are signed. The aircraft is airworthy. The aircraft is ready for service.

STE habits: one idea per sentence · active voice · present simple for a status · present perfect for a finished action · no “-ing” status.

Self-check: short lines · no dropped articles · last line is the anchor.
