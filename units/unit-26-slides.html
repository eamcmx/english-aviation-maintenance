§0 · the situation

A belt loader clips the fuselage

It is a busy morning on the ramp. A belt loader is driven away and clips a parked aircraft. There is a smooth dent in the skin, low on the left side.

Nobody is injured, but the aircraft goes on hold. Three jobs, in order: locate the damage by zone and station · measure it against the SRM · decide repair or referral, and justify it.

Ask the room: what must the mechanic report first — and why is "lower left" not enough?

§1 · listening · Dialogue 1

Reporting the ground-handling dent

MECHThere is a visible dent in the skin, on the lower left side of the fuselage.

LEADI need the precise location by zone and station, and not simply 'lower left'.

MECHIt is in zone 127, near station 480, below the forward cargo door.

Listen twice. First for gist, then catch:

  • zone & station numbers,
  • flat skin or over a stringer,
  • injuries · scratch or crack.

Why does the lead reject "lower left"? Because the exact spot decides the limit.

§1 · listening · Dialogue 2

Measuring the dent against the SRM

MECHThe dent depth is approximately 1.4 mm at the centre of the damage.

LEADThe allowable damage limit states the depth must remain under 2 mm.

MECHSo our measured depth is 1.4 mm, which means it is within limits.

Catch every figure: 1.4 mm deep · 40 mm long · 30 mm wide.

Within limits — but still inspect the stringer, and check for any crack or corrosion.

Conditional language: "unless it is deeper…", "as long as it stays within limits…".

§1 · listening · Dialogue 3 + hangar PA

Repair or referral

MECHThe dent depth is 2.6 mm, although the allowable limit here is only 2 mm.

LEADSo this dent, which exceeds the allowable damage limit, needs more than a blend out.

MECHThe damage, which the SRM does not cover, needs a referral to engineering.

Out of limits · over a frame · zone 300, station 720.

Report the facts → recommend → give a reason. Reason = over a frame and out of limits.

The PA notice repeats it for the whole hangar: on hold, bring the SRM & gauge, log it.

§1 · interactive · locate the damage

Zone + station — say exactly where

Pick a case, then drag the station slider. Read the position aloud as a full sentence.

Zone = which area · station = position along the length. Together they leave no doubt which panel to inspect.

§1 · interactive · measure vs the limit

Within limits or out of limits?

Set the location, then the depth. Watch the verdict change — and notice the same depth can flip when the location changes.

Flat skin → 2.0 mm · over a stringer or frame → 1.0 mm. Location decides the limit.

§2 · vocabulary bank

The words a report needs

TermPlain meaning
denta smooth hollow from an impact
stringera lengthwise strip stiffening the skin
framea curved ring shaping the fuselage
allowable damage limitthe largest damage the manual permits
referralsending a case to engineering
within limitsinside the allowed maximum

Drill the near-pairs:

  • gauge (tool) vs gouge (deep mark)
  • scratch < gouge < crack (severity)
  • frame (ring) vs stringer (strip)

Watch /θ/ in depth; stress cor-RO-sion, re-FER-ral.

§2 · interactive · damage vocabulary

Scratch · dent · gouge · crack

Click each term. These four are not interchangeable — calling a crack a scratch is a safety error.

You may blend out a light scratch. A crack, or anything out of limits, forces a referral.

§3 · grammar · relative clauses to specify

Pin down which damage exceeds limits

"The dent that sits over a frame is the case we must never rush." — identifies which dent · no commas

"So this dent, which exceeds the limit, needs a referral." — adds extra detail · commas

WordForCommas?
thatthings, pick one outno
whichthings, extra detailyes
whopeopledepends
wherea placedepends

Rule of thumb: never "that" after a comma — use "which".

§3 · interactive · defining vs non-defining

Commas change the meaning

Pick the relative word and toggle the commas. Watch which dent the sentence points to.

Defining = "which one?" (no commas). Non-defining = "extra detail about the one we mean" (commas).

§3 · Latvian & Russian interference

Errors to head off now

Instead of…Say / write
the mechanic which found itthe mechanic who found it
the dent, which it exceeds…the dent, which exceeds…
there is dent in skinthere is a dent in the skin
depth 1,4 mmdepth 1.4 mm ("one point four")

These are predictable, not careless. Name the cause and the learner fixes it.

Both L1s lack articles and share one relative word for people and things; Russian allows a resumptive pronoun; the decimal is written with a comma.

§4 · reading + listening review

SRM page · report form · replay

E5 — SRM page. Instructions + limits: flat 2.0 mm, stringer/frame 1.0 mm, min 25 mm to a crack.

E7 — report form. Past facts + a decision. The trap: 1.4 mm over a stringer is out of limits.

E8 — replay Dialogue 3. Numbers: 2.6 mm vs 2 mm, over a frame, SRM does not cover.

Genre contrast: the SRM tells you what to do; the report records what happened.

Label the verbs — imperatives on the SRM page, past facts on the report.

§5 · speaking · role-plays

Four cards — take one each, then swap

CardRole & goal
1Mechanic — report the dent by zone & station
2Inspector — measure vs the SRM, decide within limits
3Mechanic — recommend repair or referral, justify it
4Day mechanic — hand over: location + on-hold status

Hold the order: locate → measure → decide → justify.

Mark with the B2 rubric (range · accuracy · fluency · interaction · coherence). Two praise points, one fix.

Passed = locates by zone/station, compares to a limit, gives a reason.

§6 · writing · Simplified Technical English

The Ground Damage Report

STE habits:

  • short sentences, one idea per line;
  • active voice — "A tug struck the fuselage";
  • plain verbs — "start", not "commence";
  • only common words + today's terms.

Include, in order: what happened · damage type · location (zone + station) · measurements · SRM check · result · recommendation with a reason. 80–120 words.

Self-check: short sentences? location by zone/station? depth vs limit? repair or referral, and why?

§ wrap-up

Three checks, then out

Locate by zone & station · compare against the limit · recommend repair or referral, and say why.

Exit ticket: one sentence describing a dent with a relative clause — "The dent, which is 2.6 mm over a frame, needs a referral." Homework: finish the report, do the quiz.

Praise the win: exact numbers + correct relative clauses = a report a colleague can trust.
