Gated file · complete model answers · not for the learner's desk during practice

Accepted answers are shown in bold. Where the pack accepts variants (for example a term with or without the), the main form is given and the note flags what else is correct. The one-line reason after each answer is the teaching point to feed back on.

E1 Gap-fill — put the right term in the gap

Recycles the ten coaching terms. One term per gap.

  1. mentor — Why: the experienced technician who guides a junior; the person, not the act.
  2. walk-through — Why: a slow, step-by-step demonstration; hyphenated noun.
  3. on-the-job training — Why: learning a real task while doing it (OJT); a fixed three-part phrase.
  4. competence — Why: proven ability to do the task correctly and safely — not “competition”.
  5. sign-off authority — Why: the permission to certify a job complete; a licence gives it.
  6. demonstration — Why: showing the steps before the learner tries them.
  7. comprehension check — Why: a quick question that confirms the junior understood.
  8. supervision — Why: watching and guiding a less-experienced worker; note under close supervision.
  9. feedback — Why: clear, helpful comments on how the junior performed; uncountable, no “-s”.
  10. hands-on practice — Why: doing the task yourself to learn it; hyphen in hands-on.

E3 Dialogue completion — the coaching talk

One word per gap; rebuilds a short walk-through.

  1. walk-through — Why: “Watch my walk-through” — the mentor shows before the junior tries.
  2. first — Why: the junior asks about order — the sequencer first, not in first.
  3. card — Why: “read the task card” — it lists every step in order.
  4. jack — Why: the tool that lifts the aircraft; lift it on the jack.
  5. debris — Why: “foreign object debris” (FOD) — the fixed noun phrase.
  6. axle — Why: after removing the wheel you inspect the axle for cracks.
  7. rationale — Why: the mentor asks for the safety reason behind the step.
  8. fail — Why: “a cracked axle can fail in service” — the airworthiness rationale.

E4 Grammar transformation — you + verb → plain imperative

Keep the sequencer; drop the subject; use the plain verb. Type only the imperative part.

  1. read the task card — Why: a teaching step drops you and uses the bare verb.
  2. check for foreign object debris — Why: imperative + the fixed FOD phrase; no article before foreign.
  3. lift the aircraft on the jack — Why: keep the before both known parts — aircraft and jack.
  4. remove the old wheel — Why: the old wheel is a known part — article required.
  5. inspect the axle — Why: plain verb inspect; the axle keeps its article.
  6. set the torque wrench — Why: set the tool first — set, not setting.
  7. torque the nut — Why: torque as a plain imperative verb; not you are torquing.
  8. fit the lockwire — Why: final step, bare imperative; the lockwire keeps the article.

Writing Model answer — annotated

Brief: the first six steps of a main-wheel change as teaching instructions, 60–90 words, a sequencer + imperative per step, safety rationale on at least one step, Simplified Technical English.

Model answer · 63 words

First, read the task card. Next, check the area for foreign object debris, because loose items can damage the aircraft. Then, lift the aircraft on the jack. After that, remove the old wheel. Now, inspect the axle for cracks; a cracked axle can fail in service. Finally, fit the new wheel, and set the torque wrench before you torque the nut.

Why this scores well

  • Every step opens with a sequencer — First, Next, Then, After that, Now, Finally — so the order is unmistakable (one sequencer per step).
  • Each step is a plain imperative — read, check, lift, remove, inspect, fit — no subject, no continuous.
  • Two safety rationales, not one — the FOD check (loose items can damage the aircraft) and the axle (a cracked axle can fail in service) exceed the “at least one” requirement.
  • Simplified Technical English — one idea per sentence, short common words, the same word for the same thing (the aircraft, not “the plane / the jet”), and every sentence under 20 words.
  • Articles are correct throughout — the task card, the jack, the old wheel, the axle — the exact point Latvian and Russian learners must watch.

Band note: to reach the top of the B2 range, a learner could add the torque value (torque the nut to 300 Nm) and the lockwire step with its rationale (it stops the nut turning loose) — closing the loop from card to lockwire. Below-band scripts usually show dropped articles, a stray you are …-ing, or steps with no sequencer.
