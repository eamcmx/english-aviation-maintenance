Coordinating a component removal and installation.

Two technicians change an engine generator. First you find the correct part in the illustrated parts catalogue by its figure and item number. Then you coordinate the removal, install the new unit, torque the fasteners, tag the old part and run the test — talking to your partner in clear spoken B2 English at every step.

ScenarioTwo-technician component change VocabularyComponent change — 22 terms GrammarActive imperative over passive (STE) Class length90 minutes · balanced skills

By the end of this unit you can …

  • Can identify the correct component using the IPC figure and item number.
  • Can coordinate a removal and installation, confirming steps with a partner.
  • Can describe the torque, tagging and test requirements on completion.

01The situation

It is morning in the hangar at Riga. An Airbus A320, reg YL-BBX, is on jacks in bay two. There is a work order to change the integrated drive generator on engine one. The generator is unserviceable, and a fresh unit is waiting on the store shelf.

This is a two-technician task, so nobody works alone. You are the lead technician. First you and your partner identify the correct component in the illustrated parts catalogue, because fitting the wrong part is a serious error. Then you coordinate the removal methodically, one step at a time. Afterwards you install the replacement unit, torque the fasteners to the specified value, tag the old part as unserviceable, and complete a functional test before you sign the task card.

In plain words — four ideas before you start

IPC (illustrated parts catalogue) — the manual with drawings that show every part and its number. IDG (integrated drive generator) — the unit on the engine that makes electrical power. Torque value — the exact tightness for a bolt, in newton metres. STE (Simplified Technical English) — the plain writing rule for manuals: give one instruction, and start it with the action verb. Every hard word is glossed in the vocabulary bank.

02Listen — find the part, remove it, install it

Listen to each part first. Do not read yet. Then open the transcript and check the words. Listen for how each technician gives a clear instruction to a partner. The same two voices come back in the exercises.

Dialogue 1 — Find the correct component in the IPC

A = Rob, lead technician · B = Lena, technician

Transcript

RobMorning, Lena. We have a work order to change the drive generator on engine one.

LenaMorning, Rob. Is it the integrated drive generator, the one on the gearbox?

RobYes. Before we touch it, we find the correct component in the illustrated parts catalogue.

LenaGood idea. Which figure do we open in the illustrated parts catalogue?

RobOpen figure 24-11-01. The generator sits in the middle of that figure.

LenaI see it. The generator is item number 40 in the figure. Is that right?

RobThat's right, item number 40. Now read the part number next to it.

LenaThe part number is 761782C. Let me check it against the unit on the shelf.

RobGood. But check the effectivity first, in case our aircraft needs a different part.

LenaThe effectivity column lists our tail number. So this part number is correct for us.

RobPerfect. The store unit is tagged serviceable, so we can use it today.

LenaAnd the old one? We mark the removed unit unserviceable and fit a warning tag.

RobExactly. Print the task card now, so we follow every step in order.

LenaThe task card is printing. It lists the removal first, then the installation and test.

RobGood. Read me the first step while I get the tools.

LenaStep one: open the fan cowl and gain access to the component. Let's start.

Dialogue 2 — Coordinate the removal

A = Rob, lead technician · B = Lena, technician

Transcript

RobThe cowl is open. Let's coordinate the removal step by step, so nobody rushes.

LenaAgreed. I will support the component while you release the fasteners. Warn me before each move.

RobFirst, disconnect the electrical connector. I am pulling the plug from the generator now.

LenaConnector is off. Shall I fit a blanking cap on the open plug straight away?

RobYes. Fit the blanking cap now, so no dirt gets into the electrical connector.

LenaDone, the cap is on. What about the bonding lead on the case?

RobRemove the bonding lead next. Keep the small fastener safe in the parts tray.

LenaThe fastener is in the tray. The bonding lead is clear of the unit.

RobGood. Now the oil lines. I am cutting the lockwire on the two unions.

LenaLockwire is off. Watch for oil. I have a blanking cap ready for each line.

RobLines are disconnected. Cap both lines now, and catch the drip in the tray.

LenaBoth lines are capped. The last item is the mounting bolt on the clamp.

RobCorrect. Hold the component firmly, because it is heavy once the mounting bolt is out.

LenaI have it. Take the mounting bolt out slowly. I am taking the weight now.

RobBolt is out. Lower the component together, on three. One, two, three.

LenaIt's down. We mark this unit unserviceable and attach a warning tag before it leaves.

Dialogue 3 — Install, torque, tag and test

A = Rob, lead technician · B = Lena, technician

Transcript

RobThe new unit is ready. Let's begin the installation. Lift the component onto the clamp together.

LenaLifting on three. One, two, three. The component is seated on the mounting pad.

RobGood. Start the mounting bolt by hand first, so we do not cross the thread.

LenaThe bolt is started by hand. It turns freely. Now we need the torque value.

RobThe task card gives the torque value. It is 34 newton metres for this clamp.

LenaSet the torque wrench to 34. Tell me when you want the final pull.

RobTorque the mounting bolt now. Use the torque wrench slowly until it clicks.

LenaIt clicked at 34. The mounting bolt is at the correct torque value.

RobGood. Fit fresh lockwire on the bolt, so it cannot turn loose in service.

LenaLockwire is on. Shall I add a torque seal across the bolt head as well?

RobYes. Add a torque seal, so the next shift can see the bolt was torqued.

LenaTorque seal is on. Reconnect the oil lines, and I will remove the blanking caps.

RobLines are on and lockwired. Now refit the bonding lead and the electrical connector.

LenaConnector is locked and the bonding lead is tight. Every fastener is secure.

RobWe run a functional test before we close the cowl. Power the generator, please.

LenaFunctional test is good. The generator is serviceable, so we can sign the task card.

03Vocabulary bank — component change

These twenty-two terms carry the unit. Each one has a plain-words meaning, an example at your level, and one common word partner (collocation). You hear every term in the dialogues and use every term in the exercises.

TermPlain meaningExampleCollocation
illustrated parts catalogueThe manual with detailed drawings that identify every part and its number (the IPC).Before removal, we identify the component in the illustrated parts catalogue, although the location is familiar.open the illustrated parts catalogue
figureA labelled drawing in the IPC that shows the parts of an assembly.The figure shows the generator and its clamp, so we confirm the assembly before we begin.open a figure
item numberThe number that identifies one particular part within an IPC figure.Read the item number carefully, because a similar component sits directly beside it in the figure.read the item number
part numberThe manufacturer's unique reference number for a particular part.Check the part number against the label, unless a colleague has already verified the unit.check the part number
effectivityThe information showing which aircraft a part or figure actually applies to.The effectivity confirms that this particular part applies to our aircraft, not a different variant.check the effectivity
componentA single assembled unit that you can remove and replace as one item.Two technicians support the component together, because the generator is heavy and awkward to handle.remove a component
removalThe procedure of taking a component off the aircraft.The task card describes the removal first, although the installation is the more demanding procedure.start the removal
installationThe procedure of fitting a component onto the aircraft.During installation, we position the component accurately before we tighten any fastener.complete the installation
torque valueThe exact tightness specified for a fastener, measured in newton metres.The torque value is specified in newton metres, so we never estimate the tightness by feel.the correct torque value
torque wrenchThe calibrated tool that tightens a fastener to a specified torque value.Set the torque wrench precisely, because an incorrect setting produces a dangerous installation.set the torque wrench
fastenerA bolt, nut or screw that mechanically holds parts together.Keep every fastener in the tray, unless you are refitting it to the component immediately.release a fastener
mounting boltThe bolt that secures the component to its supporting clamp or bracket.Start the mounting bolt by hand, so that the thread cannot cross and damage the clamp.torque the mounting bolt
lockwireLocking wire that physically prevents a fastener from turning loose.Fit fresh lockwire after torque, because the fastener must not loosen while the engine vibrates.fit lockwire
blanking capA protective cap that closes an open pipe or plug to exclude contamination.Fit a blanking cap immediately, so that contamination cannot enter the open connector or pipe.fit a blanking cap
warning tagA label attached to identify a condition, such as "do not operate".Attach a warning tag to the removed unit, because it is unserviceable and awaiting repair.attach a warning tag
serviceableCertified as fit for use on the aircraft.The replacement unit is serviceable, provided it carries a valid release certificate from the workshop.tagged serviceable
unserviceableNot fit for use; it must be repaired before reissue (u/s).Mark the removed generator unserviceable immediately, although it may be repairable at the component workshop.mark it unserviceable
electrical connectorThe plug that electrically joins a component to the aircraft wiring.Disconnect the electrical connector first, unless the maintenance manual specifies a different sequence.disconnect the electrical connector
bonding leadThe strap that provides an electrical path from the component to the airframe.Refit the bonding lead securely, because it provides the electrical path to the airframe structure.refit the bonding lead
functional testAn operational check confirming that the new component works correctly.Complete a functional test before closing the cowl, so that any fault appears immediately.run a functional test
task cardThe printed job card that lists each individual step of the task.Follow every instruction on the task card, although experienced technicians remember the general procedure.sign the task card
torque sealA paint mark applied across a fastener to confirm it was torqued.Add a torque seal across the fastener, so the next inspector confirms the bolt was tightened correctly.add a torque seal

04Grammar in context — the active imperative (STE)

In maintenance work you give instructions to your partner. Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100) has a clear rule for this. Name the action, and start the sentence with the verb. This is the active imperative. Do not hide the action in a passive form. Listen to how Rob and Lena do it.

Rob: "Disconnect the electrical connector." (not: "The electrical connector must be disconnected.")

Rob: "Fit the blanking cap now." (not: "A blanking cap should be fitted now.")

Rob: "Torque the mounting bolt now." (not: "The mounting bolt is to be torqued now.")

Rob: "Run a functional test." (not: "It is necessary to run a functional test.")

The underlying pattern is straightforward: verb + object. Because the doer is understood to be "you", the technician never states it. Each instruction occupies a single sentence, which keeps the individual step short, unambiguous and easy to follow under pressure — and that is precisely why STE requires it.

Passive / indirect (avoid)Active imperative (STE)Why STE prefers it
The connector must be disconnected.Disconnect the connector.Name the action directly, and begin the instruction with the verb.
The bolt should be torqued.Torque the bolt.Remove the modal "should be", because it weakens a mandatory instruction.
It is necessary to fit the cap.Fit the cap.Delete the empty introduction, although the meaning stays identical.
The unit is to be installed.Install the unit.A single, active instruction is clearer than an impersonal construction.

Two additional points deserve attention. Retain the article "the" with the object, as in Install the pump, because a bare noun sounds incomplete. Furthermore, always select the plain verb rather than the "-ing" form: write Remove the bolt, although learners frequently produce the incorrect Removing the bolt.

Three mistakes to avoid (Latvian & Russian speakers)

Why it happensWrongRight
Your first language uses the passive for instructions, so you write "must be".The electrical connector must be disconnected.Disconnect the electrical connector.
You start with "it is necessary to", which STE does not allow.It is necessary to torque the mounting bolt.Torque the mounting bolt.
You use the infinitive with "to" as a command (as in Russian).To fit the blanking cap.Fit the blanking cap.

E7 · Reading 2 Genre: Component change task card

Read the task card, then answer

TASK CARD — IDG REMOVAL AND INSTALLATION

A/C reg: YL-BBX. ATA: 24-11-01. Station: RIX, bay two.

Component: integrated drive generator, item 40, part number 761782C.

Removal: open the fan cowl and disconnect the electrical connector, then fit a blanking cap. Remove the bonding lead. Cut the lockwire before you disconnect the oil lines. Remove the mounting bolt, and lower the component carefully with two people.

Tagging: mark the removed unit unserviceable, and attach a warning tag before it leaves the bay.

Installation: lift the new component onto the clamp, and start the mounting bolt by hand. Torque the mounting bolt to 34 newton metres, unless the engineering order specifies otherwise. Fit fresh lockwire, and add a torque seal. Reconnect the oil lines, the bonding lead and the electrical connector.

Test: carry out a functional test of the generator, because the installation is not complete until the test is satisfactory. The unit becomes serviceable when the test is good.

Sign-off: sign the task card only when the installation and the functional test are both complete.

  2. What is the torque value for the mounting bolt?

  4. What do you add after the lockwire?
  5. When is the new unit serviceable?
  6. When do you sign the task card?

07Writing — a component-change task-card step list

Write a short step list for the technical records. Use Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100): give one instruction per step, start each step with the action verb, and use the active voice. Keep each step under about fifteen words.

Task

A fuel boost pump in the centre tank is unserviceable on an A320. You and a partner change it. Write the task-card steps as active imperatives. Cover: find the component in the illustrated parts catalogue (figure and item number); check the effectivity; remove the electrical connector and fit a blanking cap; remove the mounting bolts; lower the component with two people; tag the old pump unserviceable; install the new pump; torque the fasteners to the value; fit lockwire and a torque seal; run a functional test; sign the task card. Write 100–130 words, one instruction per line.

Show a model answer

1. Open the illustrated parts catalogue at figure 28-22-01.
