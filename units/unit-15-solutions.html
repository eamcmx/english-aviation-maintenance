E1 Gap-fill — put the right term in each gap 10 items

#AnswerWhy
1inoperativeThe pack is not working — the plain-word state that starts every write-up.
2dispatchTo release the aircraft to fly; the permission the MEL grants.
3rectification intervalThe time limit to fix a deferred item; a category C item gives ten days.
4placardThe small "INOP" warning label fitted next to the broken control.
5deferred defectA fault you fly with and repair later; it is raised in the tech log.
6minimum equipment listThe book you open to decide whether the item lets the aircraft fly.
7provisoA condition you must meet before you may dispatch.
8limitationA limit the MEL places on the flight, here on the flight level.
9serviceableFit for use; the aircraft's state once the item is placarded and deferred.
10unserviceableNot fit for use — the opposite of serviceable.

E4 Grammar transformation — rewrite with a modal 8 items

#AnswerWhy
1must not"Not permitted" = a firm prohibition → must not + bare verb.
2could (or may / might)"It is possible" = possibility, not certainty → could / may / might.
3may (or can)"Is allowed to" = permission → may / can, no "to".
4can"Are able to" now = present ability → can.
5will be able toFuture ability cannot be "will can"; use will be able to.
6must not"Not allowed" = prohibition → must not run.
7May (or Can)A permission question fronts the modal: May the aircraft…?
8must not"Forbidden" = the strongest prohibition → must not remove.

E6 Error-spotting — find and fix eight errors 8 items

#CorrectionWhy
1may dispatchA modal takes the bare verb — no "to" after may.
2the crewEnglish needs the article; "crew" here is a specific, known crew.
3must not (climb)A firm prohibition is must not; "can not" is too weak for a rule.
4placard"Placate" is the wrong word; the label is a placard.
5will be able toNo double modal — "will can" is impossible; use will be able to.
6three (days)Category B, not C, so the interval is three calendar days.
7deferred defectThe fixed noun phrase is deferred defect, not "defer defect".
8the placardAdd the article: "once the placard is on".

WRITE Model answer — deferred-defect entry (VHF 2) annotated

Task: VHF 2 inoperative on an A320; MEL category C (ten days); proviso that one VHF works and the crew is briefed. Write 90–120 words in Simplified Technical English.

DEFERRED DEFECT — VHF 2 INOPERATIVE

Defect: VHF 2 is inoperative. VHF 1 is serviceable.

MEL reference: 23-12-02, repair category C. Rectification interval: 10 calendar days.

Proviso: one VHF radio must work. The crew must use VHF 1.

Limitation: none on the route. Placard: fit "INOP" on the VHF 2 control.

(M) Maintenance procedure: deactivate VHF 2. Raise the deferred defect in the technical log.

(O) Operational procedure: brief the crew. The crew must use VHF 1 for all calls.

Release: sign the certificate of release to service after the placard is on.

Why it scores well — STE and MEL discipline.
FeatureWhereWhy it earns marks
Short sentencesthroughoutEvery sentence is under about twenty words and carries one idea — a core STE rule.
Active voice"Fit…", "Deactivate…", "Brief the crew."Instructions are direct commands, not passive "the crew should be briefed".
Correct MEL termsproviso · limitation · placard · deferred defect · rectification intervalEach bank term is used in its exact technical sense.
Both procedures(M) and (O) blocksThe maintenance action and the crew action are separated, as in a real entry.
Modal accuracy"one VHF radio must work", "The crew must use VHF 1"Prohibition and requirement use must with the bare verb — no "to", no double modal.
Ends with the releasefinal lineThe entry closes with the certificate of release to service, the required last step.
Common learner slips to flag: "VHF 2 may to be deactivate" → must be deactivated; a missing article ("crew must use VHF 1" → "the crew"); and long, stacked sentences that break the one-idea STE rule.
