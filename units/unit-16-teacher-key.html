2 Latvian & Russian interference — why each error happens

These learners share a first language pattern that clashes with English reported speech. Name the clash explicitly — it is far more effective than correcting the surface error.

1 · No backshift of tense

✗ The planner said the item is deferred. → ✓ …said the item was deferred.

Why: Latvian and Russian do not shift the tense of the reported clause. The embedded verb keeps the tense of the original words, so a present stays present. Russian "Он сказал, что предмет отложен" and Latvian "Viņš teica, ka priekšmets ir atlikts" both keep the present. English forces the one-step backshift (is→was, can→could, will→would, has done→had done). Drill it as a mechanical "step one tense back" rule tied to the past reporting verb.

2 · "say" used with a person (say/tell merge)

✗ He said me the tank was empty. → ✓ He told me the tank was empty.

Why: both first languages use one main verb where English splits into two patterns. Russian "сказать" and Latvian "teikt" cover both "say" and "tell", and they take an indirect object directly (сказать мне, teikt man = "say me"). English blocks "say + person": use tell + person (tell me) but say with no person (say that…). Teach the two frames side by side and let learners sort sentences into them.

3 · Reported questions keep interrogative order / add no "do"

✗ The captain asked can we dispatch. → ✓ The captain asked whether we could dispatch.

Why: in Latvian and Russian an indirect question keeps the same word order as a direct one — there is no auxiliary inversion and no "do" support to undo. So learners carry the direct form straight in ("asked can we…", "asked do we can…"). English needs whether/if plus statement word order and no question auxiliary. Practise the three moves: add whether/if → drop the auxiliary → put subject before verb.

4 · Dropped articles

✗ She was happy to accept aircraft. → ✓ …to accept the aircraft.

Why: neither Latvian nor Russian has articles, so "the/a" are simply absent from the learner's grammar and get dropped, especially before technical nouns (accept aircraft, sign certificate, fit placard). In documentation this reads as clipped but in speech it is a clear B2 marker. Flag definite reference: the specific aircraft, the certificate, the placard.

5 · Time-word shift forgotten

✗ He said we will fix it tomorrow. → ✓ He said we would fix it the next day.

Why: the backshift of deictic time words (today→that day, tomorrow→the next day, this→that) has no equivalent trigger in the first languages, so learners keep "tomorrow/today". Because a dispatch decision is relayed later and across shifts, the time shift genuinely changes meaning — worth a short focused drill.

Quick contrast drill

Put a direct line from Dialogue 1 on the board ("We can defer it."). Learners relay it three ways with different reporting verbs (said / told me / asked whether). This surfaces backshift, say/tell and question order in one move.

3 Extension activities

  • Faster / stronger classes: add a second AD that is out of compliance, so the decision flips to no-go. Learners must relay the new fact and justify grounding the aircraft.
  • Avionics variant: swap the fuel-indication defect for an inoperative number-two VHF radio (MEL, provisos, placard). Keeps the same language, covers the avionics trade.
  • Chain relay (whole class): a fact starts with the engineer and must pass through planner → captain → next shift. Each learner adds one reported-speech sentence; the class checks the backshift stayed correct down the chain.
  • Documentation focus: learners turn Dialogue 1 into a written deferred-defect page in STE, then compare with the model in E7.
  • Pronunciation: drill the NATO spelling used for references (delta-nine-one-one, bravo-bravo-x-ray) — a real line-station skill for phone dispatch.
  • Homework: record a 60-second spoken shift handover of the whole decision, using at least five reported-speech structures.

4 CEFR B2 speaking rubric — role-plays

Use for role-play cards 1–4. Five criteria, three bands. The target is solid B2; note where a learner is approaching from below (B1+) or reaching above (B2+). Weight range and accuracy for the grammar focus, and interaction for the relay task.

CriterionB2+ (strong)B2 (target)B1+ (approaching)
Range Uses the dispatch-control vocabulary precisely and varies reporting verbs (said, told, asked whether, wanted to know) with ease. Has enough language to handle the dispatch task; uses the key terms and two or three reporting structures. Relies on a few terms; repeats "said"; gaps force simplification or first-language words.
Accuracy Backshift, say/tell and reported questions are consistently correct; article and time-word shifts controlled. Reported speech is generally correct; errors appear under pressure but do not block meaning. Frequent no-backshift and say/tell errors; some reported questions keep direct order.
Fluency Speaks at a natural pace with only brief pauses to plan; the turnaround pressure does not derail delivery. Keeps going with some hesitation; can relay a fact without long stalls. Halting; pauses are long and frequent; needs prompting to continue the relay.
Interaction Relays and questions smoothly, checks understanding, confirms and hands over like a real dispatch call. Takes turns well; relays the other speaker's words and responds to questions appropriately. Manages simple exchanges but struggles to relay a third party's words accurately.
Coherence Organises the decision clearly (defect → MEL → AD → restriction → sign-off) with natural connectors (although, unless, so, because). Links ideas with common connectors; the decision is easy to follow. Ideas are listed but loosely joined; the order of the decision is not always clear.

Scoring suggestion: 5 criteria × 4 points = 20. B2 target band ≈ 12–16; B2+ ≈ 17–20; below 12 signals a B1+ profile that needs more controlled reported-speech practice before the simulation.

Anti-bias note

In every multichoice item the correct option is never the longest and never the most hedged; distractors are plausible and length-matched. There is no "all of the above". Each item carries general feedback and every option carries its own feedback for review after the attempt.
