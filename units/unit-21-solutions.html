E1Gap-fill — put the right term in each gap

#AnswerWhy
1clearanceA safe distance from a moving part — the standing collocation is "keep your clearance".
2serviceable"Fit and safe to use"; the BITE check confirms a component is serviceable.
3snagA written-up fault; you "raise a snag" in the tech log when a test fails.
4riggingThe setting that aligns a surface; a rigging error makes one flap lag.
5BITEBuilt-in test equipment; its readout shows a fault on a channel.
6AMMThe Aircraft Maintenance Manual lists every step and every warning.
7indicationA light or reading showing system state; "a green indication".
8placardA warning card fitted to mark a hazard; you "fit a placard".
9lockoutA tag/lock stopping a system being used; "fit the lockout".
10calloutA spoken command said clearly during the test; the caller makes it.

E2Matching — meaning → term

#AnswerWhy
1actuationThe movement of a part when a force drives it (the event, not the device).
2actuatorThe device that turns pressure into movement (contrast with actuation).
3functional testA check that a system works as it should.
4pass criteriaThe exact result a test must reach to pass.
5flapA wing panel that changes lift.
6deflectionHow far a surface moves from neutral.
7asymmetryOne side moving differently from the other.
8control surfaceA moving panel that steers or slows the aircraft.
9limit switchA switch signalling a part has reached its end point.
10hydraulic pressureThe fluid force that drives many systems.

E3Dialogue completion — the pre-test brief

#AnswerWhy
1functionalFixed name of the check: a flap functional test.
2criteria"pass criteria" — the result the test must reach.
3deflection"full deflection" = the surface at its end travel.
4clearance"keep clearance from" the flaps — a safe distance.
5lockout"fit the lockout tag first" — a safety precaution before actuation.
6switch"limit switch" — watch it during travel.
7actuation"I call each actuation; you confirm" — the call-and-confirm pattern.
8standImperative warning: "Do not stand near the flaps during travel."

E4Grammar transformation — rewrite as a short imperative

#AnswerWhy
1Do not stand near the flaps.Negative warning = Do not + base verb; the auxiliary "do" is compulsory.
2Fit a lockout."It is necessary to" → drop it; use the bare base verb "Fit".
3Keep clearance."You should" → drop the subject and the modal; base verb only.
4Do not touch the actuator."Workers must not" → "Do not" + base verb; no subject.
5Run the BITE check first."You have to" → base verb "Run"; keep the adverb "first".
6Confirm each callout."It is important to" → base verb "Confirm".
7Do not pressurise the system."You must not" → "Do not" + base verb.
8Raise a snag."You should" → base verb "Raise".

"Don't …" is equally correct for the negatives (1, 4, 7). Mark it right.

E6Error-spotting — find and fix the eight mistakes

#Wrong → RightWhy
1you not stand → do not standNegative imperative needs the auxiliary "do"; drop the subject "you".
2No touch → do not touch"No + verb" is L1 transfer; English uses "Do not + verb".
3must to run → we must runA modal ("must") takes the bare infinitive — no "to".
4flaps is → flaps arePlural subject "flaps" needs the plural verb "are".
5is a fail → it is a failEnglish needs the dummy subject "it"; L1 can omit it.
6signs → signAfter "Do not" the base verb has no -s: "Do not sign".
7Raise snag → raise a snagCountable singular noun needs the article "a".
8isn't it? → keep clearDrop the question tag; a safety instruction is a plain imperative.

WRWriting — annotated model snag entry

Task: 50–70 words in Simplified Technical English. Say what failed, what you saw, the safety action, the next step; use the imperative for the warning.

SNAG 4471, D-AXLR. The flap functional test failed. At full deflection, the left flap lags the right flap. There is visible asymmetry between the control surfaces. This breaks the pass criteria in AMM 27-51-00. The aircraft is not serviceable. A lockout is fitted on the green system. A placard is on the flap lever. Do not pressurise the system. Inspect the left actuator and the rigging, then re-run the test.

64 words.

Why this scores well (STE habits):
  • Result first. "The flap functional test failed." — one clear fact, one short sentence.
  • What you saw. "the left flap lags the right flap … visible asymmetry" — observation stated plainly, links to the criteria.
  • Traceability. "breaks the pass criteria in AMM 27-51-00" — cross-references the source document.
  • Safety action. "A lockout is fitted … A placard is on the flap lever." — the aircraft state is recorded.
  • Imperative warning. "Do not pressurise the system." — the safety order uses the base verb, no subject.
  • Next step. "Inspect the left actuator and the rigging, then re-run the test." — one action per clause.
  • STE discipline. Every sentence is under 18 words; each carries one idea; approved plain words only.
