Solutions — Querying a Service Bulletin with the OEM

Full model answers for E1–E8, one line of reasoning per item, and the annotated model email. This is a gated teacher file — complete answers are shown.

Accepted variants are shown with a slash (serial number / serial). For E4/E6, mark on statement order and whether/if, not on spelling alone.

E1 Put the right term in the gap

  1. applicabilityThe applicability table lists which aircraft the bulletin covers.
  2. serial number The serial number names one exact aircraft — here 5127.
  3. supersededA newer revision replaces the old one; rev 2 is superseded by rev 3.
  4. mandatoryAn airworthiness directive makes the action a legal must, not a choice.
  5. lead timeLead time is the wait before parts arrive — about three weeks here.
  6. logbook entryThe written record in the aircraft log; the collocation is "make a logbook entry".
  7. accomplishment instructionsThe step-by-step method to do the task, followed in order.
  8. complianceCompliance = doing what is required; the logbook entry proves it.
  9. part numberThe code that names one exact part in the kit.
  10. clarificationA clear answer that removes doubt — what you ask the OEM for.

E3 Complete the call

  1. bulletin"a service bulletin" — the fixed collocation opens the call.
  2. serial"your serial number" — the OEM needs it to check effectivity.
  3. effectivity"which effectivity applies" — the indirect question of the unit.
  4. mandatory"mandatory or recommended" — the fixed pair for compliance level.
  5. directive"airworthiness directive" — an AD is what makes a task mandatory.
  6. affectedThe verdict: the aircraft is on the list.
  7. modification"the modification kit" — what you order.
  8. entry"a logbook entry" — the record made after the work.

E4 Build the indirect clause

Rule: opener + statement order (subject before verb); no do/does; add whether for yes/no.

  1. which revision is currentVerb is stays after the subject — no inversion.
  2. whether the bulletin is mandatoryYes/no → whether + statement order.
  3. what the part number is"is" goes to the end; no "what is the…".
  4. whether our aircraft is affectedYes/no → whether; subject before verb.
  5. which serial numbers are affectedPlural subject → are; still statement order.
  6. how the modification worksNo does; verb takes the -s: "works".
  7. whether revision 2 is supersededYes/no → whether; passive kept in statement order.
  8. what the lead time is"is" to the end after "the lead time".
  9. whether compliance is mandatoryYes/no → whether; subject "compliance" first.
  10. where the effectivity is listed"is listed" after the subject; no inversion.

E5 Read the service bulletin

  1. the bracketSubject line: the pitot probe support bracket is replaced.
  2. recommended"Compliance: Recommended" — no AD is linked.
  3. 5100 to 5140Effectivity: serial numbers 5100 through 5140.
  4. three weeksParts line: lead time is about three weeks.
  5. revision 2The note states revision 2 is superseded (rev 3 is current).
  6. a logbook entry"Record the embodiment with a logbook entry."

E6 Find and fix the mistakes

  1. does apply → which effectivity appliesRemove does; the main verb carries the tense.
  2. what is the current revision → what the current revision isDrop the question inversion inside the clause.
  3. is our aircraft affected → whether our aircraft is affectedYes/no needs whether; then statement order.
  4. how does the kit arrive → how the kit arrivesRemove does; add -s to the verb.
  5. what is the lead time → what the lead time is"is" moves to the end after the subject.
  6. is revision 2 superseded → whether revision 2 is supersededYes/no → whether; keep statement order.
  7. which parts do I need → which parts I needRemove do; subject "I" before verb "need".
  8. where is the applicability listed → where the applicability is listedNo inversion; "is listed" after the subject.

E7 Read the work order

  1. 5127Work Order 22-0987 is for aircraft YL-BAK, serial 5127.
  2. no, recommended"Compliance is recommended, not mandatory. No AD applies."
  3. noThe order states there is no wiring rework.
  4. the modification kit"Order modification kit 34-2189-K3 … today."
  5. revision 3Task: embody SB 34-2189, revision 3.
  6. a logbook entrySign-off: make a logbook entry and record the embodiment.

E8 Questions on Dialogue 3 and the voicemail

  1. a technicianRihards, the line technician, makes the call in Dialogue 3.
  2. the accomplishment instructionsHe has read them and wants to confirm the next step.
  3. no"No rework of the wiring" — only the bracket and fasteners change.
  4. three weeksThe OEM gives the kit lead time as about three weeks.
  5. with a logbook entryRecord the embodiment with a logbook entry quoting the SB and revision.
  6. revision 3The voicemail says revision 2 is superseded; revision 3 is current.
  7. no, recommendedThe voicemail keeps the modification recommended, not mandatory.
  8. the new effectivityTwo more serials are affected — check the new effectivity first.

WRITING Model answer — annotated

Task: query the unclear effectivity of SB 34-2189 in 90–120 words; three indirect questions; state the serial; ask for the applicability in writing.

Subject: SB 34-2189 — effectivity query, serial 5127 Dear OEM Support, We are reviewing service bulletin 34-2189, revision 2. The effectivity is not clear to us. Our aircraft serial number is 5127. Registration is YL-BAK. Could you tell me whether this serial number is affected? Could you confirm whether the bulletin is mandatory or recommended? Could you tell me which revision is current? Please send the applicability list in writing for our records. Please copy our engineering mailbox. Kind regards, R. Ozols Baltic Line Maintenance
Why this answer scores well (mark against these):
  • Three indirect questions, all in statement order: whether this serial number is affected, whether the bulletin is mandatory, which revision is current — no do/does, no inversion.
  • Serial number stated early (5127) with the registration — the fastest route to a clear answer.
  • Applicability requested in writing — closes the loop and creates a record.
  • STE habits: one idea per sentence, short sentences, a clear subject line, active voice. ~95 words — inside 90–120.
  • Register: polite opener and close; precise, not wordy.
Common errors to deduct for: "Could you tell me what is the current revision" (inversion), "I want to know is our aircraft affected" (missing whether), long run-on sentences, or no serial number given.
