② Latvian & Russian interference — why each error happens

These are the predictable first-language slips for a Latvian/Russian group. Knowing the source lets you correct fast and kindly.

Question tags — the big one

"It is the correct pin, no?"  →  isn't it?

Why: both Russian and Latvian confirm with a single, invariant tag — Russian да? / нет?, Latvian vai ne? / ne?. The tag never changes with the verb. English, uniquely, rebuilds the tag every time from the auxiliary and tense, so learners reach for one frozen word ("no?", "yes?", "right?"). Teach the universal casual right? as the safe fallback, but drill the matched tag for formal use.

"You sent the report, isn't it?"  →  didn't you?

Why: once students accept that English changes the tag, they over-generalise the most common one, isn't it?, to every sentence. The fix is the two-step check: (1) which auxiliary and tense? (2) flip the polarity. Here sent is past simple with no auxiliary → did → didn't you?

"That is not the pin, isn't it?"  →  is it?

Why: the polarity flip is invisible in the L1s, where the tag stays the same after a negative. Students hear the negative not and still add a negative tag. Drill: negative statement → positive tag.

Other predictable slips

SlipCorrectWhy (L1 source)
"I readed back the code."read backLearners regularise the irregular verb read; neither L1 has this English irregular past.
"Make me a confirm."confirm it / a confirmationRussian/Latvian let a verb noun stand alone; English needs the right part of speech (verb vs noun).
Article drop: "Call OEM desk."Call the OEM desk.Neither Latvian nor Russian has articles, so a/the are routinely dropped or misused.
"He explain the code."He explainsThird-person -s has no equivalent trigger in the L1s; it is often omitted.
/v/ for /w/: "wiring" → "viring"wiringBoth L1s lack the English /w/–/v/ contrast; drill minimal pairs (west/vest).
Flat intonation on the tagrising = real questionA genuine check needs a rising tone; a falling tag sounds like it expects agreement, not confirmation.

③ Extension activities

  • Phonetic relay. One student spells a random reference (e.g. BP8) in the phonetic alphabet; the partner writes it and reads it back. Swap. Times how fast the pair reaches a confirmed match.
  • Broken-telephone repair. Whisper a code down a row. When it arrives wrong, the last student must use the clarification loop (state → tag → wait → confirm) to recover the correct code aloud.
  • Register flip cards. Give a formal tech-log sentence; students rewrite it as hangar talk, then back again. Fastest accurate pair wins.
  • Tag tennis. Student A makes a statement, Student B must return the correct tag within three seconds; a wrong or frozen tag loses the point. Great for the L1 tag error.
  • Real documentation hunt. Bring a redacted AMM/IPC page or BITE screenshot; students find one ambiguous reference and script a clarification call for it.
  • AI partner (fast finishers). Point strong students to the three conversation prompts in the student body; they run a call and self-correct from the recast.

④ CEFR B2 speaking rubric — role-plays

Score each pair across five criteria. B2 is the target band (the middle column). Use the bands below/above to give direction, not just a grade.

CriterionBelow B2 (developing)B2 (target)Above B2 (strong)
RangeRelies on a few fixed phrases; gaps force a switch to L1 or silence.Uses the unit's strategy verbs and tags flexibly; enough vocabulary to clarify a reference without groping.Varied, precise language; reformulates in several ways to avoid any ambiguity.
AccuracyFrozen tags ("no?"), missing articles/3rd-person -s obscure meaning.Tags mostly correct (polarity + auxiliary); errors do not cause misunderstanding.High control; self-corrects tags and word forms before they mislead.
FluencyFrequent long pauses; the check-back stalls the exchange.Speaks at a steady pace; pauses to confirm are purposeful, not breakdowns.Smooth, near-effortless; manages confirmation without losing flow.
InteractionWaits to be prompted; does not initiate a check or a paraphrase.Initiates paraphrase, tag and read-back; responds to the partner's checks.Steers the exchange; invites and gives confirmation naturally; repairs smoothly.
CoherenceIdeas jump; the fix is not clearly sequenced.Uses connectors (although, so, unless); the clarification follows a clear order.Well-linked throughout; the report and the fix are logically staged and easy to follow.

Target-language checklist per exchange: at least one paraphrase, one correct question tag, one read-back, and one deliberate register choice (formal report vs hangar talk).
