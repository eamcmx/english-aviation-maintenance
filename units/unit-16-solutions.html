E1 Gap-fill put the right term in each gap

  1. defect — a fault the crew found and wrote up.
  2. airworthiness directive — a firm order from the authority about the pumps.
  3. repetitive inspection — a check repeated at a set interval under the AD.
  4. compliance — "in compliance" = the rules are fully met.
  5. threshold — the due point in hours; here 40 hours away.
  6. deferral — "record the deferral" so the item is fixed later.
  7. restriction — a limit on operation: the centre tank stays empty.
  8. placard — an INOP label fitted next to the switch.
  9. proviso — a condition you must meet to dispatch.
  10. sign-off — the final signature that releases the aircraft.

E2 Matching match each term to its meaning

  1. the book of items that may be inoperative and still allow flight — minimum equipment list: the MEL.
  2. the time limit to repair a deferred item — rectification interval: set by the repair category.
  3. a fault the aircraft may fly with, repaired later — deferred defect: carried within the interval.
  4. a fault that stops dispatch — no-go item: the aircraft may not fly.
  5. to release the aircraft to fly — dispatch: the verb for sending it out.
  6. the aircraft's running record of faults and actions — technical log: the tech log.
  7. the signed statement that the aircraft is safe to fly — certificate of release to service: the CRS.
  8. the desk that plans and controls the flights — operations control centre: the OCC.
  9. the departure time window from air traffic control — slot: "hold the slot".
  10. the ground time to prepare the aircraft between flights — turnaround: a quick turnaround.

E3 Dialogue completion complete the dispatch call

  1. minimum equipment list — the book you open to check a deferrable item.
  2. airworthiness directive — the open order to check before dispatch.
  3. compliance — "in compliance" after the inspection was done.
  4. restriction — the MEL adds it: centre tank empty.
  5. deferred defect — raised in the tech log.
  6. placard — an INOP label on the fuel panel.
  7. certificate of release to service — the final signed release.
  8. deferral — "the deferral reference" before the crew boards.

E4 Grammar transformation direct speech → reported speech

  1. was — backshift: is → was.
  2. could — modal: can → could.
  3. had to — modal: must → had to.
  4. had — tense: have signed → had signed.
  5. to placard — command: "tell me to + verb".
  6. was — reported question: is → was, statement order.
  7. had been — passive past: was done → had been done.
  8. would — modal: will → would.

E5 Reading 1 dispatch decision sheet

  1. the centre-tank fuel quantity indication — stated as the inoperative item.
  2. C — MEL 28-42-01 is repair category C.
  3. by the drip sticks — the proviso: confirm the fuel by hand.
  4. every 500 flight hours — the AD's repetitive inspection interval.
  5. the centre tank must stay empty — the operating restriction.
  6. after the placard, the deferred defect and the crew brief — the sign-off is last.

E6 Error-spotting find and fix eight errors

  1. was — backshift: is → was.
  2. told me — say vs tell: "tell" takes a person.
  3. had been done — past perfect passive for an earlier action.
  4. whether we could dispatch — reported question: no "can", statement order.
  5. was — backshift: is → was.
  6. the aircraft — article: add "the".
  7. the next day — time shift: tomorrow → the next day.
  8. would be able to fix — no double modal: "will can" is wrong.

E7 Reading 2 technical-log deferred-defect page

  1. no — the assessment says it is not a no-go item.
  2. 18 AUG — 10-day interval from 08 AUG.
  3. AD 2019-0123 — the cross-referenced directive (fuel pumps).
  4. yes — the next threshold is still 40 hours away.
  5. she was happy to accept the aircraft — the crew brief line.
  6. the certifying engineer — A. Berzina, licence B1, signed the CRS.

E8 Listening review replay Dialogue 3

  1. the captain — Anna reports the captain has accepted the aircraft.
  2. she was happy to accept the aircraft with the restriction — relayed in reported speech.
  3. whether the repetitive inspection was still valid — the captain's reported question.
  4. D-911 — "delta-nine-one-one", the deferral reference.

W Writing — model answer dispatch decision record in Simplified Technical English

Genre: technical-log dispatch decision record — the task: a left main-gear brake temperature indication is inoperative (MEL category C, 10-day interval), the AD on the brakes is in compliance (next threshold 60 flight hours), the restriction is a lower maximum taxi speed. Target 90–120 words in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English.

DISPATCH DECISION — LEFT BRAKE TEMPERATURE INDICATION INOPERATIVE
STE · titleShort, capitalised heading. One noun group, no filler.
Defect: the left main-gear brake temperature indication is inoperative.
STE · one ideaOne sentence, one fact. Approved word "inoperative", not "u/s".
MEL reference: 32-42-01, repair category C. Rectification interval: 10 calendar days.
STE · data firstThe MEL data is stated plainly, no hedging.
Airworthiness directive: AD 2021-0088 (brakes). The repetitive inspection is valid. The next threshold is 60 flight hours. The aircraft is in compliance.
Content · combine sourcesThe AD read-across is shown: valid, threshold, compliance — three short sentences, each under 18 words.
Proviso: the crew checks the brakes on the ground after landing.
STE · active voice"the crew checks", not "the brakes are to be checked".
Restriction: use a lower maximum taxi speed.
STE · one instructionAn imperative — one clear action.
Action: fit an "INOP" placard on the brake panel. Raise the deferred defect in the technical log. Brief the crew.
STE · imperativesEach step is a separate short command in sequence.
Sign-off: sign the certificate of release to service. Deferral reference recorded.
Content · closes the loopEnds with the CRS — the release that lets the aircraft dispatch.

How to mark it (STE + content)

Give a tick for each of these six: (1) the defect named plainly; (2) the MEL reference and category; (3) the AD compliance read-across; (4) the restriction stated; (5) the placard and deferred defect actions; (6) the sign-off / CRS. Then check the STE habits: sentences under about 20 words, active voice, one instruction per sentence, approved plain words, no strings of nouns. A strong answer reads like a checklist a colleague could follow with no questions.
