English for Aviation Maintenance · Unit 4 · Simulation Day · CEFR B2

Simulation: morning base-check start

A two-person team opens a base check — read the work pack, split the task cards, make the aircraft safe, and log an unexpected defect found on panel removal.

90 minutes · EASA Part-66/145 · hangar workflow · sequencers for a safe opening · "First jack, then remove the wheel."

By the end · how the 90 minutes runs

Three things you will be able to do

1 — Run the sequence. Read the work pack, sign off a task, and report a defect, all in one realistic order.

2 — Plan with a partner. Prioritise the task cards and tell your partner the plan, clearly and in order.

3 — Ask when unsure. When the documentation is ambiguous, ask a clear question instead of guessing.

Warm-up · think, then tell your partner

It is 07:00. The hangar is cold and the A320 is waiting.

Before you touch the aircraft, what do you do first?

And before you jack it off the ground — what must be safe? Tell your partner in one or two short sentences. Use the word first.

§ Listening · Dialogue 1

Reading the work pack, splitting the cards

ADocumentation first, then the aircraft. How many task cards are inside the pack?

BSix task cards. Each one is a separate job, with its own sign-off box.

AI will manage the panel and inspection cards. Each card references a section of the AMM.

Listen for:

  • How many task cards? (six)
  • Who takes the landing-gear cards? (B — Ravi)
  • What comes first — paperwork or aircraft?

The work order is the legal authority. Nothing begins until it is signed and open.

§ Listening · Dialogue 2

Jacking the aircraft, opening the panels

AThe AMM instructs us to jack the nose last. First the wings, then the nose, unless the card differs.

BAs soon as it is elevated, we install the lockout pins on every jack.

ATwenty panel fasteners. I will bag them, so nothing is misplaced.

Listen for the order:

  1. Safety cordon
  2. Wheel chocks
  3. Jack the wings
  4. Jack the nose (last!)
  5. Lockout pins
  6. Open the access panel

A loose fastener becomes FOD — foreign object debris. Bag every one.

§ Listening · Dialogue 3

Reporting a defect, asking for clarification

ABehind the access panel I can see a crack near a bracket. It is not on my card.

BWhenever the AMM is unclear, we never decide alone. We ask.

ADo we measure the crack first, or contact the supervisor first?

Listen for:

  • What is behind the panel? (a crack near a bracket)
  • How long is it? (about 12 mm)
  • Measure first or call first? (measure first)

No sign-off until the defect is repaired and inspected. Always.

§ Listening · Dialogue 4 · voicemail

The night-shift handover

MThe work order is open and authorised by the supervisor.

MOne open item remains in the tech log. It is a minor defect on a cabin panel, not urgent.

MPlease review the tech log first, then divide the task cards as usual.

True or false?

  • The work order is still closed. (false)
  • The cordon is already up. (true)
  • There is one open item in the tech log. (true)

A good handover = what is done, what is open, what to do first.

§ Vocabulary · Interactive · hangar workflow

Match the term to its plain meaning

Twenty hangar-workflow terms. Read the meaning, pick the term — and learn the collocation, the word partner that sounds natural.

B2 learners know the noun but choose the wrong verb: it is raise a work order, set up a cordon, record the torque.

§ Vocabulary · staging E1 & E2

Now drill it — E1 gap-fill, E2 matching

E1 · Gap-fill (individual, 4 min): one bank term per gap, in real base-check sentences. Pair-check after.

E2 · Matching (pairs, 3 min): term ↔ meaning drop-downs. Quick race — first correct pair reads their answers.

Watch for these two:

FOD — foreign object debris. Not "rubbish".

lockout — the pins that stop a jack lowering. One word.

Model item 1 of each, then monitor. Keep it moving.

§ Grammar · sequencers for workflow

Words that put the steps in order

SequencerUse
First, … / First of all, …the very first step
Then / Next / After thatthe following step
Before / After + -inglink two steps by time
As soon as / Once …one step right after another

"First jack the aircraft, then remove the wheel."

The order is fixed, so the words that mark the order matter. Get them wrong and the safety of the opening changes.

§ Grammar · Interactive · order the opening

Build the base-check opening in the right order

Click the eight steps into a safe order. The chain fills as you get each one right; a wrong step flashes and names the rule.

As you click, say it: "First we set up the cordon, then we fit the chocks…" — sequencer every time.

§ Grammar · first-language interference

Three errors to expect — Latvian & Russian

At first, we jack the aircraft.First, we jack the aircraft."At first" = in the beginning, but it later changed. From sākumā / сначала. For step one, use "First".
After remove the panel, log the torque.After removing the panel, log the torque.After before/after, use the -ing form. Neither language has a gerund, so learners drop -ing.
First jack aircraft, then remove wheel.First, jack the aircraft; then remove the wheel.Latvian and Russian have no articles, so "the" is dropped. Over-correct it today.
§ Safety · Interactive · jack it safely

Make the aircraft safe — in order

The jack will not move until the cordon and chocks are set. Raise the wings first; the nose follows; then fit the lockout and open the panel.

Narrate it with sequencers: "As soon as it is up, then we fit the lockout pins."

§ Reading · authentic genres

Two documents you must read — and write

TASK CARD 04-A

Applicability · zone · AMM reference · warning · numbered steps · sign-off box.

"Do not begin until the aircraft is on jacks and the lockout pins are fitted."

TECH LOG · DEFECT ENTRY

Date/time · raised by · defect · action taken · status.

"Crack about 12 mm behind panel 191AB. Sign-off held until repair is done and inspected."

§ Speaking · four role-play cards

Run the whole morning — in pairs

1Plan the morning — read the work order, split the cards, agree the order.

2Make it safe — cordon, chocks, jack in the right order.

3Report the defect — stop, report clearly, agree next steps.

4Ask for clarification — the AMM is ambiguous; ask a clear question.

Rules for the pairs:

  • A sequencer in every planning turn.
  • One clear question in Card 4.
  • Swap roles and repeat two cards.

Rubric in the teacher key. Note one strength + one fix per pair.

§ Writing · Simplified Technical English

Write the defect entry — short, clear, one idea per line

STE habits:

  • Short sentences — one idea each.
  • One instruction per sentence.
  • Approved, unambiguous words — no synonyms.

Task (60–90 words): describe the crack behind panel 191AB — what, where, size, what you did, why the sign-off is held. Use sequencers.

"First, we stopped the task. Then we measured the crack and took a photo. After that, we recorded it in the tech log."

Recap · exit ticket

One morning, in the right order

Read the pack · split the cards · make it safe (cordon → chocks → wings → nose → lockout → panel) · find and log the defect · ask when it is unclear. The language that carried it: hangar-workflow words, sequencers, and Simplified Technical English.

Exit ticket: one full sentence with a sequencer + a bank term — e.g. "First we set up the safety cordon, then we fit the wheel chocks." We never skip a step; we never sign off unfinished work.
