Warm-up · the problem

Would you sign off on this?

"So basically when I was doing the walk-round on the right side I noticed the little metal thing that holds the antenna, it looked kind of rough near the bottom, might be some corrosion or maybe just dirt, hard to tell in the dark, anyway I wiped it and it still looked bad so probably someone should look at it…"

Turn to a partner. Find three reasons an inspector cannot act on this note.

Listening · Dialogue 1  ▶ Audio track 1

From shift note to defect report

AA defect report is a formal record, so it must use Simplified Technical English.

BMy note says the part near the door looked bad.

ADid you find corrosion, a crack, or a loose bolt?

AWrite what you saw, not what you guessed.

Listen for

  • a fact an inspector can check;
  • the words that create doubt: seemed, might;
  • the fix: The bracket has corrosion.
The core move

Fact, not guess

GUESS  It seemed like it might be leaking.
FACT  There is fuel on the floor below the bracket.

An inspector can check a fact. Nobody can check a guess. Every line of a defect report must be checkable.

Listening · Dialogue 2  ▶ Audio track 2

Write the procedure in STE

The removal of the bolt should be done.
Remove the bolt from the bracket.

Active voice · one instruction per line · start with a technical verb.

Two more STE habits

  • Break a noun cluster: wing attachment fitting bolt hole → the bolt hole in the fitting.
  • Warning protects a person. Caution protects the part.

Listening · Dialogue 3  ▶ Audio track 3

Peer review before sign-off

AHere you dropped an article. You wrote: Remove bolt from bracket.

BIn my language we do not use articles, so I forget them.

AWrite serviceable if it is good, or unserviceable if it is not.

AThe warning protects the person, so it comes before the step.

Three fixes in this scene

  • add the missing articles;
  • use serviceable / unserviceable, not "okay";
  • put the warning before the step.

Grammar in context · obligatory articles

English still needs its articles

CaseRuleSTE example
Known thingUse the when the reader knows which one.Remove the bolt from the bracket.
First mentionUse a/an first; use the after that.Find a crack. Measure the crack.
One of manyUse a/an for any one, not a special one.Install a new seal.
General / uncountableNo article for a material or a plural in general.Check for corrosion. Torque all bolts.

Writing task · individual, then peer review

Rewrite the note as a defect report

Write 6-8 short lines

  • a defect line (a fact);
  • an action line;
  • a warning or caution;
  • the state: serviceable / unserviceable.

Self-check & peer review

  • Every sentence short, one idea each.
  • Every noun has the right article.
  • Approved words, one meaning, no guesses.
  • State given; warning/caution if needed.
Wrap-up

Four things to carry to the hangar

1 · Write facts an inspector can check — never guesses.

2 · Approved words, one meaning each — no ambiguity.

3 · Short sentences, active voice, one instruction per line.

4 · Every article in place — the bolt, the bracket.
