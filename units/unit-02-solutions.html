E4Grammar transformation — into the imperative

Rewrite each line as a task-card step: start with the base verb, drop the subject. Trailing full stop optional.

#AnswerWhy
1Remove the access panel."You must remove" → bare verb + the article the.
2Torque the bolt to 25 Nm.Passive "should be torqued" → active command; keep to + value.
3Do not touch the connector.Negative = do not + base verb; the stray to is deleted.
4Inspect the weld."You should inspect" → drop the subject and the modal.
5Fit the lockwire.Passive "must be fitted" → direct command.
6Sign off the step."You have to sign off" → bare imperative.
7Do not reuse the old panel."Must not be reused" → do not + base verb (active).
8Check the reference."You need to check" → bare imperative, no to.

E6Error-spotting — the L1 interference errors

Each line has one fault: a missing article, a wrong negative, or an extra word. The correction and the error type:

#Correct lineError type / why
1Remove the access panel first.Missing article — English needs the.
2Do not touch the connector.Wrong negative — a command needs do not + base verb.
3Sign off the step.Extra word — delete "Please to"; a step uses the bare verb.
4Inspect the weld for cracks.Wrong form — no -s; the imperative is the base verb.
5Do not reuse the lockwire.Wrong negative order — do not comes before the verb, not "reuse not".
6Torque the bolt to 25 newton metres.Missing article — add the before bolt.
7Refit the panel.Wrong form — active command, not passive "must be refit".
8Write the defect in the tech log.Missing article — add the before defect.
Pattern: almost every error is a dropped the or a mis-built negative — the two things Latvian and Russian do differently from English. Drill these two, and most task-card mistakes disappear.

WRITEModel answer — a tech-log defect entry (annotated)

A 4 mm crack found on the flap track at Step 3 of card 27-51-11. Four to six lines in Simplified Technical English. Below, each line is followed by the reason it meets the standard.

Task card 27-51-11, Step 3. Reference AMM 27-51-11. ↳ Names the card, the step and the reference first, so any reader can trace the job.
Defect found on the flap track. There is a 4 mm crack near the weld. ↳ States what and where in two short sentences — one fact each, the STE habit.
The crack exceeds the limit. Do not sign off this step. ↳ Gives the consequence, then a clear negative imperative (do not + base verb).
Raise a repair task card. Inspect the track again after the repair. ↳ Two positive imperatives; each begins with an approved base verb, with the article the.
Entry made by: [name and stamp]. Keep this entry legible. ↳ Authorises the entry and states the legibility condition — a signed record must be readable.
Why it scores full marks: every sentence is short (≤ 14 words) and carries one instruction; every command uses the base verb and keeps the article; the entry names card + step + reference, states what and where, and states clearly that the step is not signed off and why. Nothing is hidden, and no unnecessary words are added — exactly how a genuine tech-log entry reads.
Common learner slips to correct: "Found crack on flap track" (missing a/the); "Not sign off" (should be Do not sign off); one long run-on sentence joined by "and" (split into separate STE lines); vague "it is broken" (state the size and location — 4 mm crack near the weld).
