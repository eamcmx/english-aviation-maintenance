E1 · Gap-fill

Put the right term in each gap

#AnswerWhy
1documentation trailThe whole linked set of records for one defect — the unit's headline term.
2cross-referenceA link that points from one document to the exact place in another.
3traceabilityThe ability to follow a job (or part) from start to finish.
4sign-off blockThe box on a record where a person signs to confirm the work.
5task cardThe document that lists the steps of one job in order.
6IPCThe illustrated parts catalogue — it shows and numbers each part.
7AMMThe aircraft maintenance manual gives the approved repair steps.
8serial numberThe unique code for one single tracked item, and no other.
9corrective actionThe exact work carried out to clear the defect.
10audit trailThe complete linked record an auditor follows to check the work.

E4 · Grammar transformation

Add the right linking word

#AnswerWhy (the job the connector does)
1ThereforeResult: the replacement follows from the worn seal. New sentence, so capital + comma.
2becauseReason: "because" introduces the cause clause (it was leaking).
3ThenSequence: the second action follows the first in time.
4HoweverContrast: no serial number, but it did have a batch number.
5AfterSequence: "after that" marks the next step after the repair.
6soResult: mid-sentence connector after a comma (clear → trusted).
7SinceReason: "since" = "because" at the start of the sentence.
8AsResult: fixed phrase "as a result" shows the consequence.
9beforeSequence: record the number first, i.e. before closing the order.
10ConsequentlyResult: the complete record leads to the return to service.

E6 · Error-spotting

Fix the eight errors in the rough record

#AnswerWhy
1soDelete "so": "Because…" already links the cause — one connector per join.
2soJoin two clauses with one short connector after a comma, not a bare "therefore".
3because"because of" takes a noun; before a clause use "because".
4theA specific, known seal takes the definite article "the".
5aOne new, non-specific seal takes the indefinite article "a".
6serviceableUse the approved word; "okay" is vague and not STE.
7dualThe correct name for a second check is a dual inspection.
8AMMRepair steps are in the AMM; the IPC only holds part numbers.

Writing task · annotated model answer

The documentation trail for YL-BAM — with margin notes

The defect: worn cabin door seal on YL-BAM; tech log TL-0912; work order WO-4471; task card TC-52-11-07; new seal batch B-2231 (no serial number); torqued to the AMM value; dual inspection done; both sign-off blocks signed; release to service completed. A strong answer is six to eight short lines, one idea per line, a linking word in most lines, every reference number present. Blue marks a cross-reference/reference number; green marks a linking word doing its job.

Model answer · documentation-trail summary
Defect: There is a worn cabin door seal on YL-BAM. The defect is recorded in the tech log, entry TL-0912.
why Names the record and its reference number, so the trail starts traceable. Short sentences, articles kept.
Documents: A work order (WO-4471) was raised, so task card TC-52-11-07 was issued. The task card cross-references the AMM for the steps.
why "so" shows the result (order → card). The cross-reference to the AMM links to the repair steps.
Traceability: The part number came from the IPC. The seal has no serial number, so the batch number (B-2231) was recorded.
why Correct serial-vs-batch rule; "so" gives the reason-to-result join in one short sentence.
Corrective action: The old seal was removed, and a new seal was fitted. Therefore the rectification is complete.
why Plain, approved description of the work; "Therefore" (new sentence, comma) marks the result.
Close-out: A dual inspection was done, because the door is safety-critical. Both sign-off blocks were signed. Consequently, the release to service was completed, and the aircraft is serviceable.
why "because" gives the reason for the dual inspection; "Consequently" closes the chain to release. Uses "serviceable", not "okay".
Marking priorities (Latvian / Russian speakers): (1) one linking word per join — watch for "Because…, so…"; (2) a full stop before "therefore/consequently", or use "so"; (3) "because" + clause, never "because of" + clause; (4) articles present ("the seal", "a new seal"); (5) every reference number present and matching.
