2 First-language interference — Latvian & Russian

The predictable errors, and why each one happens. Naming the cause helps students self-correct rather than just memorise a fix.

The grammar focus: time clauses

1 · Future tense in the time clause

✗ Fit the panel before the aircraft will depart. → ✓ …before the aircraft departs.

Why: both Latvian and Russian mark the future in the subordinate clause too. Russian uses the perfective future (когда прибудет = "when [it] will arrive"); Latvian likewise puts the future in the kad / pirms clause. English blocks will after before, after, as soon as, once, until, when and uses the present. This is the single most frequent error in E4 and E6.

2 · Infinitive after a preposition

✗ Check the MEL before to defer the defect. → ✓ …before you defer / before deferring.

Why: Russian and Latvian happily follow a preposition with an infinitive (перед тем как + verb, or a bare infinitive), so students transfer that pattern. English needs a full clause or an -ing form after before.

Wider errors to expect

ErrorExampleWhy (L1 cause)
Dropped articles"Sign release to service before pushback."Neither Latvian nor Russian has articles, so a / the are omitted or misused. Drill "the release", "a defect".
"make" vs "do""make a walkaround", "make the handover"Russian делать and Latvian darīt/veikt map to both. Teach collocations: do a walkaround, give a handover, raise a defect.
Present Simple for present states"The warning is illuminating still."Over-use of continuous. Model "the warning is still on / remains illuminated".
Stress & /θ/ /ð/"tech log" → /tek/, "the" → /zə/No dental fricatives in either L1. Not an error of grammar — flag lightly, focus on intelligibility (B2 goal).
Word-for-word "no-go""it is item that cannot go"Compound noun unfamiliar. Teach no-go item as a fixed lexical chunk with its MEL meaning.

3 Extension activities

Fast finishers

  • Reverse the handover. The incoming technician had a quiet shift; now they hand back to Anna. Write and perform the reverse handover in six turns, keeping the read-back.
  • Rewrite in STE. Take any three long sentences from a real AMM extract you have and split them into Simplified Technical English — one instruction per sentence, under 20 words.

Stretch to C1

  • MEL debate. The captain wants to carry a second defect that is borderline. In pairs, argue for and against deferral using hedging language (provided that, unless, as long as, on condition that).
  • Genre swap. Turn the spoken decision (Dialogue 2) into three genres: a tech-log entry, a one-line task-card note, and a text message to stores. Compare register and length.

Support / lower B1–B2

  • Sequence cards. Print the eight turnaround actions on cards; pairs order them and link each with before / after / then spoken aloud.
  • Term bingo. Students fill a 4×4 grid with the 20 terms; call the plain meanings; first line wins. Locks the vocabulary before the role-plays.

Homework

  • Finish the STE tech-log entry (80–110 words).
  • Run one AI conversation-partner prompt from the student book and record three time clauses used correctly.

4 CEFR B2 speaking rubric — role-plays

Use during role-play cards 1–4. Five criteria, three visible bands. B2 is the target: the middle band is a pass; the top band is a strong B2 edging to C1; the bottom band signals B1 and needs feedback. Give each pair one strength and one fix.

CriterionB2+ strongB2 passbelow (B1)
RangeWide use of the specialist terms and connectors (although, unless, as soon as, provided that); flexible phrasing.Uses most unit terms and basic connectors; some repetition but enough for the task.Few specialist terms; relies on general words; struggles to name the tools/steps.
AccuracyTime clauses consistently correct under pressure; errors are rare and do not block meaning.Time clauses mostly correct; some slips (will in the clause) but meaning is clear.Frequent time-clause errors; article/verb errors that sometimes obscure meaning.
FluencySmooth, natural pace; copes with the time pressure; only brief, natural pauses.Generally keeps going; some hesitation when searching for a term.Slow, effortful; long pauses stall the fast exchange.
InteractionInitiates, asks for repeats, reads the message back and confirms the sequence unprompted.Responds well; checks understanding when prompted; reads back the key facts.Mostly reactive; rarely checks or confirms; misses read-back.
CoherenceActions clearly ordered; connectors link steps logically end to end.Order is clear; connectors used but occasionally basic (and then).Order unclear; steps listed without linking; hard to follow the sequence.

Quick score: tick a band per row; 5 rows. Feed back one strength + one fix, tied to a named band.
