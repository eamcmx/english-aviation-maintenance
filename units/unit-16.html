Simulation: the dispatch decision under pressure.

A defect appears near departure. You must combine the MEL, an airworthiness directive and the defect into one dispatch decision, justify it to operations and the crew, and record the deferral. Today is the simulation day, you relay the decision from voice to voice, in clear spoken B2 English.

ScenarioDispatch decision under pressure VocabularyDispatch control, 20 terms GrammarReported speech (relaying decisions) Class length90 minutes · balanced skills

By the end of this unit you can …

  • Can combine MEL, AD and defect information to reach a dispatch decision.
  • Can justify the decision clearly to operations and the crew.
  • Can document the deferral and the procedures applied.

01The situation

It is evening at Riga. An Airbus A320, reg YL-BBX, is on stand nine, close to departure. The crew has written up a defect in the technical log: the centre-tank fuel indication reads zero. The turnaround clock is running, and the captain needs an answer.

This is a simulation. You are the certifying engineer. You cannot decide alone, so the decision passes from voice to voice. First you call the planner in operations. Together you combine three sources: the defect, the minimum equipment list, and an open airworthiness directive on the fuel pumps. Then you justify the decision to the captain and record the deferral.

In plain words: four ideas before you start

MEL (minimum equipment list), the book that says which items may be broken and still let the aircraft fly. AD (airworthiness directive), a firm order from the authority to check or fix something by a deadline. Repair category, the MEL class (A, B, C or D) that sets the repair time limit. Reported speech, telling someone what another person said: "He said the item was deferred." Every hard word is glossed in the vocabulary bank.

02Listen, the decision passes from voice to voice

Listen to each part first. Do not read yet. Then open the transcript and check the words. Listen for how each speaker relays what another person said. The same voices come back in the exercises.

Dialogue 1: Engineer and planner: a defect and an open AD

A = Anna, certifying engineer (line) · B = Mark, duty planner at operations

Transcript

AnnaOperations, it's Anna at stand nine. I have a defect on the A320, reg bravo-bravo-x-ray.

MarkGo ahead, Anna. Mark here at the operations control centre. What is the defect?

AnnaThe crew wrote that the centre-tank fuel indication read zero. The indication is inoperative.

MarkUnderstood. Have you checked the minimum equipment list for it?

AnnaYes. One tank indication may be inoperative. It is a repair category C item.

MarkGood. So the rectification interval is ten days. Can we defer it today?

AnnaWe can, but there is a proviso. We must confirm the fuel by the drip sticks.

MarkThere is one more thing. There is an open airworthiness directive on the fuel pumps.

AnnaI saw it. What did the records office say about the compliance status?

MarkThe records office said the repetitive inspection had been done on Monday.

AnnaAnd the threshold? When is the next inspection due?

MarkThey told me the next threshold was not due for forty flight hours. So we are in compliance.

AnnaGood. So the defect is deferrable, and the airworthiness directive is still valid.

MarkCorrect. But the minimum equipment list adds a restriction. The centre tank must stay empty.

AnnaUnderstood. I will placard the fuel panel and raise the deferred defect.

MarkI asked operations about the slot. They said they could accept a short delay.

AnnaCopy. I will confirm the fuel, then plan the sign-off before the crew boards.

MarkPerfect. Brief the captain on the restriction, then call me for the deferral reference.

Dialogue 2: Engineer and captain: justifying the decision

A = Anna, certifying engineer · B = Ilze, the captain

Transcript

AnnaGood evening, Captain. I'm Anna, the certifying engineer. I need to brief you on a deferral.

IlzeEvening, Anna. Go ahead. What did you find?

AnnaThe crew wrote that the centre-tank fuel indication read zero. The indication is inoperative.

IlzeI see. Is this a no-go item, or can we still dispatch?

AnnaWe can dispatch. The planner said we could defer it under the minimum equipment list.

IlzeGood. What did the records office say about the airworthiness directive on the pumps?

AnnaThey said the repetitive inspection had been done, and the aircraft was in compliance.

IlzeSo the deferral is clean. Is there any restriction on the flight?

AnnaYes. Mark told me the centre tank had to stay empty for this dispatch.

IlzeUnderstood. So we plan the fuel on the wing tanks only.

AnnaCorrect. I have placarded the panel and raised the deferred defect in the technical log.

IlzeYou said the fuel had to be confirmed. How will you do that?

AnnaI confirmed it with the drip sticks under the wing. The figure matches the load sheet.

IlzeEarlier you said the slot might slip. Is the turnaround still on time?

AnnaThe planner said operations had held the slot. The turnaround is still on schedule.

IlzeGood. I am happy to accept the aircraft with that restriction.

AnnaThank you, Captain. I will complete the sign-off and give you the deferral reference.

Dialogue 3: Engineer and planner: documenting the deferral

A = Anna, certifying engineer · B = Mark, duty planner at operations

Transcript

AnnaMark, it's Anna. The captain has accepted the aircraft. I'm ready to document the deferral.

MarkGood work, Anna. Tell me what the captain said.

AnnaShe said she was happy to accept the aircraft with the restriction.

MarkDid she ask anything about the airworthiness directive?

AnnaYes. She asked whether the repetitive inspection was still valid. I told her it was.

MarkGood. For the record, the deferral reference is delta-nine-one-one.

AnnaCopy. I will write the minimum equipment list item and the reference on the deferral page.

MarkAdd the airworthiness directive number too, so the read-across is clear.

AnnaUnderstood. I will note that the aircraft is in compliance, with the threshold recorded.

MarkAnd the restriction? The crew must know the centre tank stays empty.

AnnaIt is in the technical log and on the placard. The captain has been briefed.

MarkPerfect. Then you can raise the certificate of release to service.

AnnaI asked the captain if she needed anything else. She said the brief was complete.

MarkGood. Once the sign-off is done, the aircraft is serviceable for dispatch.

AnnaI am signing the certificate of release to service now. The deferral is closed on paper.

MarkWell done. I will tell the next shift that the item was deferred and the flight went out.

AnnaThanks, Mark. The turnaround is complete. Bravo-bravo-x-ray is cleared to go.

03Vocabulary bank, dispatch control

04Grammar in context, reported speech

On a simulation day the decision passes from voice to voice. So you often relay what another person said. This is reported speech. You do not use the exact words; you report them. Listen to how Anna does it in the calls.

Direct: "The item is deferred." → Reported: "The planner said the item was deferred."

Direct: "We can defer it." → Reported: "The planner said we could defer it."

Direct: "The tank must stay empty." → Reported: "Mark told me the tank had to stay empty."

Direct: "Is the inspection valid?" → Reported: "She asked whether the inspection was valid."

The key move is the backshift: the verb steps one tense back. Is becomes was; can becomes could; must becomes had to; will becomes would. The present perfect has done becomes had done.

ChangeDirect speechReported speech
Present → past"The item is deferred."He said the item was deferred.
can → could"We can dispatch."She said we could dispatch.
must / command"Placard the switch."He told me to placard the switch.
Question → whether"Is it a go item?"He asked whether it was a go item.

Two more rules. Use say with no person after it, but tell with a person: He said the tank was empty / He told me the tank was empty. And shift the time words: today becomes that day, tomorrow becomes the next day, this becomes that.

Three mistakes to avoid (Latvian & Russian speakers)

Why it happensWrongRight
You keep the present tense (your first language does not backshift).The planner said the item is deferred.The planner said the item was deferred.
You mix up "say" and "tell" (they are one verb in your first language).He said me the tank was empty.He told me the tank was empty.
You keep question word order with "do" in a reported question.The captain asked can we dispatch.The captain asked whether we could dispatch.

Defect: the centre-tank fuel quantity indication reads zero. The indication is inoperative.

MEL reference: 28-42-01, repair category C. Rectification interval: 10 calendar days.

Proviso: the fuel load must be confirmed by the drip sticks before each flight.

07Writing, a dispatch decision record

Write a short dispatch decision record for the technical log. Use Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100): short sentences, one instruction each, the active voice, and approved words. Keep each sentence under about twenty words.

Task

A single brake-temperature indication (left main gear) is inoperative on an A320. The MEL item is repair category C (rectification interval ten days). The proviso is that the crew checks the brakes on the ground after landing. An open airworthiness directive on the brakes is in compliance; the next threshold is 60 flight hours away. The restriction is a lower maximum taxi speed. Write the record: state the defect, the MEL reference, the AD compliance, the restriction, the placard and the sign-off. Write 90–120 words in STE.

Show a model answer

DISPATCH DECISION, LEFT BRAKE TEMPERATURE INDICATION INOPERATIVE

Defect: the left main-gear brake temperature indication is inoperative.

MEL reference: 32-42-01, repair category C. Rectification interval: 10 calendar days.

Airworthiness directive: AD 2021-0088 (brakes). The repetitive inspection is valid. The next threshold is 60 flight hours. The aircraft is in compliance.

Proviso: the crew checks the brakes on the ground after landing.

Restriction: use a lower maximum taxi speed.

Action: fit an "INOP" placard on the brake panel. Raise the deferred defect in the technical log. Brief the crew.

Sign-off: sign the certificate of release to service. Deferral reference recorded.
