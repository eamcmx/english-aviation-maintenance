How to use Marking notes

Every answer below carries a short WHY so you can explain the point, not just tick the box. Accept the alternatives shown after a slash (/). Spelling of the specialist terms should be exact (for example no-go item, sign-off, lead time). Contractions such as hadn't are fine in the grammar answers.

E4Use of English · mixed conditionals

Write the correct verb form in each gap. The pattern is the key point, not memorised sentences.

#AnswerWhy
1had ordered / would havePast cause (we did not order) → present result (no spare now).
2were / would have fittedPresent state (not in stock) → past result (not fitted). Use "were".
3had not delayed / would not be (hadn't / wouldn't)Past cause → present result; keep "would" out of the if-clause.
4were / would have escalatedPresent state (I am not the manager) → past result (escalate sooner).
5had written / would understandPast cause (unclear entry) → present result (we would understand now).

WritingModel answer · annotated

A strong response in Simplified Technical English (STE): short sentences, one idea each, approved words, and the unit's terms. Award marks for accurate terms, correct articles, correct mixed conditionals if used, and a clear recovery plan with a time and a next action.

1 · Tech log defect entry (~60 words)

Defect: Green hydraulic system pressure dropped in cruise, then partly recovered. Action: Ran a BITE test. Found two fault codes. Saw fluid at the pump seal. Root cause: Engine-driven pump internal leak. Confirmed against the AMM. MEL: The pump is a no-go item. The defect cannot be deferred. Status: Aircraft is AOG. No spare on station. Part ordered. Sign-off pending.
Why it scores: every sentence is short and holds one idea (STE). It uses the exact terms — BITE, root cause, AMM, MEL, no-go item, AOG, spare, sign-off — and adds articles correctly (a BITE test, the AMM).

2 · Escalation note to Maintenance Control (four lines)

We have a failed green hydraulic pump. It is a showstopper and a no-go item. The aircraft is AOG. No spare is on station, so please approve transport from the main base. The lead time is about six hours, then two hours to fit and test. Recovery plan: fit tonight, test against the AMM, then return to service by noon.
Why it scores: it states the problem, the constraint (AOG, no spare), the ask (approve transport), the numbers (lead time, downtime) and a clear recovery plan with a time (by noon) and a next action (fit and test). Register is calm and factual.
Sentence-level checks to reward: ✓ under 18 words per sentence · ✓ "the" and "a" before nouns · ✓ no "would" in any if-clause if a conditional is used · ✓ ends with a decision, not just a description.
Band 5 (very good): all terms correct, STE clean, plan actionable Band 3 (adequate): meaning clear, 2–3 term/article slips Band 1 (weak): no clear plan, long sentences, missing terms
