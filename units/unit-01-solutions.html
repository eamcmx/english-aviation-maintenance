E2 Matching — term to meaning

#Term → letterWhy
1task card → CA task card is a single printed job taken from the AMM.
2subtask → FA subtask is one numbered step inside an AMM task.
3revision → EA revision is an update that changes the manual; it carries a number and date.
4service bulletin → HA service bulletin is the manufacturer's notice of a fix or improvement.
5tech log → IThe tech log is the aircraft's record of defects and completed work.
6applicability → DApplicability states who or what a document applies to — the same idea as effectivity.
7cross-reference → AA cross-reference is a link from one document to the matching part of another.
8note → JA note adds extra helpful information; it is not a safety order.
9warning → BA warning is the safety box that protects people.
10caution → GA caution is the safety box that protects the aircraft.
Trap watch: B (warning, people) vs G (caution, aircraft), and D (applicability) = effectivity. These are the pairs learners most often swap.

E6 Error-spotting — fix the handover note

#AnswerWhy
1lists"AMM list" → the singular subject needs the -s: lists.
2is"The manual are" → a singular subject takes is, not are.
3peopleA warning protects people; a caution protects the aircraft.
4the"read effectivity" → the noun needs the article: read the effectivity.
5coversA permanent fact takes the present simple, not the continuous: covers, not is covering.
6has"This task card have" → the singular subject takes has, not have.
7gives"The IPC give" → the singular subject needs the -s: gives.
8sign"We signs" → "we" never takes the -s: sign.
Pattern: lines 1, 2, 6, 7 are the missing third-person -s; line 8 is an over-applied -s; line 4 is a dropped article; line 5 is the continuous used for a permanent fact — the three predictable first-language errors together.

✍ Writing — model handover note, annotated

Task: MSN 0451 needs a nose-wheel tyre change. Write a 60–80 word handover note in Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100): short sentences, one idea each, present simple, approved terms only.

Handover, Bay 3. Aircraft MSN 0451 needs a nose-wheel tyre change. The task is AMM 32-41-01, page block 401. Use revision 47. Check the effectivity first; MSN 0451 is on the list. Read the warning: release the tyre pressure before you remove the wheel. For the part number, see the IPC. Note the tyre pressure in the tech log. Then raise the work order and sign it at the end.

  • STEShort sentences, one idea each. Every sentence carries a single instruction — easy to follow under time pressure and easy for a non-native reader.
  • TASKNames the exact location. "AMM 32-41-01, page block 401" — the ATA number and page block, so the next shift goes straight to the job (outcome 1).
  • REVStates the revision. "Use revision 47" prevents anyone working from a superseded printout.
  • SAFEEffectivity then warning. Checks the effectivity before the work, then gives the warning that protects people — the correct reading order (outcome 2).
  • GRAMPresent simple, correct -s. "The task is…", "MSN 0451 is on the list" — facts in the present simple; imperatives ("Check", "Read", "Note") for instructions.
  • DOCSLinks the documents. IPC for the part, tech log for the record, work order raised and signed — the paperwork web from Dialogue 3 (outcome 3).
  • TERMSApproved terms only. No synonyms for the specialist words; STE requires one word for one meaning.
Band guide: a strong B2 answer names the task number, the page block and the revision, checks the effectivity and gives at least one warning, and holds the present simple throughout. A weaker answer drops the revision or the effectivity, or slips into the continuous ("is changing") for a fact.
