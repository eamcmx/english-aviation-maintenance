English for Aviation Maintenance · Unit 9 · CEFR B2 · EASA Part-66/145

Talking through a troubleshooting approach

Propose a logical fault-isolation sequence · reason with the first conditional (if it fails, replace it) · agree or disagree with a colleague, giving reasons.

90-minute class  ·  4 audio tracks  ·  slow flap · intermittent radio · spongy brake

By the end · how the hour runs

Three outcomes — and the running order

Outcome 1. Propose and justify a logical fault-isolation sequence.

Outcome 2. Use conditional language to reason about causes and effects.

Outcome 3. Agree or disagree with a colleague's diagnosis, giving reasons.

Warm-up → Listening 1 → Vocabulary → Grammar → Listening 2 & 3 → Reading → Role-plays → Writing.

The role-plays (cards 1–4) are the centre of the class. The quiz and the tech-log entry are formative practice.

§0 · warm-up · 5 min

The right flap moves slowly. Electrical or mechanical?

One flap is sluggish; the other is normal. That is the symptom — a visible sign that something is wrong.

Hands up: electrical or mechanical? Give one reason. Hold the guesses — the first dialogue will settle it.

symptomroot causeisolate the fault

§1 · interactive · build the sequence · 6 min

Isolate the fault — put the tests in a logical order

Cheap, quick checks first; expensive removal last. Click the tests in order and watch the sequence build.

Language to elicit: first…, then…, after that…, finally… — and one reason per step.

§2 · listening · Dialogue 1 · 8 min

Dialogue 1 — “Is it electrical or mechanical?”

Listen 1 (gist): What is the fault? Electrical or mechanical?

Listen 2 (detail): Board the tests in order and the result of each.

Answer: the wiring is good and the track is free, so the actuator is weak — it goes for a bench test.

Reasoning lines to notice:

“If the motor runs but the flap still sticks, the root cause is mechanical.”

“If the motor stays silent, the fault is probably electrical.”

Decision & paperwork: bench test · swap · serviceable · tech log · AMM · defer · MEL

§3 · vocabulary · part 2 · check meaning

Tap a term — check the meaning

Front-of-room retrieval. Then set E1 (gap-fill) and E2 (matching) in the workbook.

Fast finishers: build one spoken sentence per term with a partner, using the collocation.

§4 · interactive · first conditional · 8 min

“If it fails, replace it” — the diagnostic conditional

Rule: present tense in the if-clause, never will. The result can take will, the present, or an order.

Complete the circuit with the correct form. Will after if breaks it.

§4 · interactive · reason to a verdict · 5 min

Electrical or mechanical? — reason it out

Toggle the three observations and predict the verdict. Narrate each move with if.

“If the motor is silent, it is electrical.” · “If the track is stiff, it is mechanical.”

§5 · listening · Dialogues 2 & 3 · 10 min

Swap or test? — and — Rectify or defer?

Dialogue 2 — radio. Chafed harness is the root cause; the radio is serviceable. Decision: do not swap — build a new harness.

Dialogue 3 — brake. Bleeding fails; a seal leaks; the kit is out of stock. Decision: check the MEL and defer one brake within the limits.

Set E8 on Dialogue 3. Listen for the agree/disagree moves:

“Let us not swap the radio yet.” · “Can we defer this under the MEL?” · “Agreed.”

§6 · practice · E1–E4 · staging

Controlled practice — vocabulary and grammar

E1 gap-fill — the ten core terms.

E2 matching — term to plain meaning.

E3 dialogue completion — terms in context.

E4 transformation — first conditional, present tense.

Individual → pair-check → class sweep. The workbook self-marks on Check.

Two to three minutes each; protect time for reading and speaking.

§7 · reading · E5 & E7 · 10 min

Read the documents behind the talk

E5 — AMM task 27-51-00. The approved fault-isolation steps for a slow flap. Six short questions.

E7 — BITE readout, VHF-1. A fault code, three tests, a conclusion: the harness is the root cause.

Skill: follow a diagnosis to its root cause in a real document.

Short on time? One in class, one for homework.

§8 · speaking · role-plays · 18 min · the core

Four cards — propose, reason, agree or disagree

Card 1 — electrical or mechanical? Propose the sequence.

Card 2 — swap or bench test? Save the good radio.

Card 3 — rectify or defer? Check the AMM and the MEL.

Card 4 — disagree with a diagnosis, giving reasons.

Useful phrases to seed:

“If the motor is silent, it is electrical.” · “Let us not swap it yet.” · “I see your point, but…” · “Can we test the wiring first?”

Monitor with the rubric — push for a reason on every agree/disagree.

§9 · writing · STE tech-log entry · start in class

Write a fault-isolation tech-log entry (Simplified Technical English)

Shape: symptom → tests → result → next step → note. 80–110 words.

STE habits: one instruction per sentence · under twenty words · plain approved words · present tense in every if-clause.

The next step must use the first conditional:

“If it fails, replace it.”

Board the first two lines together; finish for homework.

Recap · close

Isolate it · reason with if · agree with a reason · record it

Symptom vs root cause · cheap tests first, expensive removal last · present tense after if, never will · agree or disagree with a reason · keep the fault open until it is fixed — no defer without the MEL.

Exit ticket: one first-conditional sentence about the next test you would run.
