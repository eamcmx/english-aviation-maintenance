2 · Latvian & Russian interference — why each error happens

The three "common mistakes" in Section 03 are not careless slips; they are predictable transfers from the learners' first languages. Knowing the cause lets you correct the pattern, not just the token.

🇱🇻 Latvian speakers

  • Could you to check… → Could you check…
    Latvian typically links a second verb with the infinitive (vai jūs varētu pārbaudīt, "could you to-check"). Learners map that infinitive onto English to. English modals take the bare infinitive, so the to must go.
  • The filter is unserviceables. → …is unserviceable.
    Latvian adjectives agree in number with the noun, so a plural noun pulls a plural ending onto the adjective. English adjectives never inflect for number.
  • Articles: I need quantity of two → a quantity of two. Latvian has no articles, so a/an/the are routinely dropped. Drill the fixed chunk "a quantity of…".
  • Register: a direct imperative is normal and polite in Latvian workplace speech, so a bare "Check the stock" does not feel rude to the learner. Make the cooperative purpose of could/would explicit.

🇷🇺 Russian speakers

  • Could you confirms… → Could you confirm…
    Russian verbs carry rich person/number endings, so learners feel a verb "should" agree with you. After an English modal the verb is always the bare form — no -s.
  • You could send me two, please? → Could you send me two, please?
    Russian yes/no questions are marked by intonation, not word order, so a statement + rising tone feels like a question. English needs subject–modal inversion: Could you…?
  • Articles & the copula: Russian has no articles and drops "to be" in the present, giving Filter is unserviceable or dropped a/the. Reinforce the article in "a serviceable tag", "the Form 1".
  • Politeness by imperative: Russian often softens with an imperative + пожалуйста, so "Send it, please" transfers as an order + "please". Show that English prefers the modal question over the imperative.

Shared correction move: recast, don't lecture. When a learner says "Could you to check…", reply "Could you check the stock — yes, let me look," and continue. The AI conversation partner is tuned to do exactly this.

3 · Extension activities

  • Speaking Chain requisition. One learner requests a part; the next must confirm interchangeability; the next handles a sudden backorder. Keep the phone call alive around the room.
  • Listening Digit dictation. Read out five part numbers and two batch numbers at speed; learners write and read back. Ties to the numerical quiz items.
  • Writing Backorder reply. Learners write the stores email (like E7) offering an alternative, in Simplified Technical English, using at least four bank terms.
  • Vocabulary Collocation race. Teams match verbs to nouns (quote/confirm/raise/check/attach) against the clock.
  • Reading Genre hunt. Give real (redacted) AMM / IPC / MEL snippets; learners find the part number, the mod status and the certification note.
  • Grammar Politeness ladder. Rewrite one request at four levels: order → Could you → Could you please → Could you possibly.

Quick descriptor: a B2 pass = gives the three items in order, requests with could/would, and negotiates an alternative or a time — intelligibly and cooperatively.
