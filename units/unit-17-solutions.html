Solutions — E1–E8, with a one-line reason for each answer

Full model answers for the eight exercises in the unit, plus the annotated model for the writing task. The exercises self-mark on the student page; this file gives the teacher the reasons to explain a wrong answer and to feed correction in the role-play and writing feedback.

E1  Gap-fill — put the right term in each gap

#AnswerWhy
1illustrated parts catalogueThe manual with drawings that identifies every part is the IPC — the first place you confirm a part.
2item numberA part is located inside an IPC figure by its item number; here the generator is item 40.
3part numberThe maker's unique reference sits next to the item and is checked against the unit's label.
4effectivityEffectivity is the data that shows the part applies to this aircraft, not a different variant.
5torque valueThe task card states the tightness in newton metres — the torque value, never a guess by feel.
6torque wrenchThe calibrated tool set to 34 that delivers the torque value is the torque wrench.
7mounting boltThe bolt that secures the component to its clamp is the mounting bolt; start it by hand.
8blanking capA cap that closes an open pipe or plug to keep out dirt is a blanking cap.
9functional testThe operational check that the new unit works is the functional test, run before closing the cowl.
10task cardThe printed job card listing every step is the task card; follow it in order and sign it.

E2  Matching — match each term to its meaning

#Term → answerWhy
1figure → a drawing in the IPC that shows the parts of an assemblyA figure is the labelled drawing; the part number is the maker's number, and the wrench is a tool — so only the drawing fits.
2component → a single unit you can remove and replaceA component is one assembled unit handled as a whole; not a paint mark and not a label.
3removal → taking a component off the aircraftRemoval is taking off; fitting on is installation — the opposite procedure.
4installation → fitting a component onto the aircraftInstallation is fitting on; removal is the reverse; tightness is the torque value.
5fastener → a bolt, nut or screw that holds parts togetherA fastener mechanically joins parts; a cap closes a pipe and a connector carries power.
6lockwire → wire that stops a fastener turning looseLockwire physically locks a fastener against engine vibration; it is not a drawing or a strap.
7warning tag → a label you attach to mark a stateA warning tag is a label ("do not operate"); not a bolt and not a step list.
8serviceable → fit to useServiceable = certified fit for use on the aircraft; the opposite is unserviceable.
9unserviceable → not fit to use; it must be repairedUnserviceable (u/s) means it must be repaired before reissue; the removed unit is marked u/s.
10electrical connector → the plug that joins a component to the aircraft wiringThe connector is the electrical plug; the bonding lead is the earth strap; the wrench is a tool.
11bonding lead → the strap that gives an electrical path to the airframeThe bonding lead earths the case to the airframe; it is not the power plug or a cap.
12torque seal → a paint mark across a fastener to show it was torquedA torque seal is a visible paint mark for the next inspector; not wire and not a cap.

#AnswerWhy

E4  Grammar transformation — passive/indirect → active imperative

The rule under test: name the action, start with the verb, keep the object and its article. The learner types only the verb (or verb phrase).
#Answer (full instruction)Why
1Disconnect the electrical connector."must be disconnected" (passive) → drop "must be" and lead with the verb Disconnect.
2Fit the blanking cap."should be fitted" weakens a mandatory step; the imperative Fit is direct.
3Remove the bonding lead."It is necessary to…" is empty introduction; delete it and start with Remove.
4Torque the mounting bolt to 34."is to be torqued" → active Torque; the value stays with the object.
5Install the component.The bare infinitive "To install…" is not a command; the imperative is Install.
6Fit fresh lockwire on the bolt."must be fitted" → Fit; keep "fresh lockwire" as the object.
7Carry out (Run) a functional test."has to be carried out" → Carry out or the everyday Run; both are accepted.
8Attach the warning tag."should be attached" → Attach, verb-first, article kept.

E6  Error-spotting — rewrite eight passive steps as active imperatives

#AnswerWhy
1Disconnect the electrical connector."must be disconnected" → verb-first Disconnect.
2Fit a blanking cap."should be fitted" → Fit; keep the article "a".
3Remove the bonding lead.Delete "It is necessary to"; lead with Remove.
4Lower the component together."is to be lowered" → active Lower; keep the adverb "together".
5Install the new unit."is to be installed" → Install, the plain imperative.
6Torque the mounting bolt."should be torqued" → Torque; the modal is removed.
7Fit lockwire on the bolt."has to be fitted" → Fit; the object "lockwire" stays.
8Carry out (Run) a functional test."must be carried out" → Carry out / Run, verb-first.

#AnswerWhy

Writing — annotated model answer

Task: a centre-tank fuel boost pump on an A320 is unserviceable; a pair changes it. Write the task-card steps as active imperatives (100–130 words), one instruction per line. The model below is fully compliant STE — use it to show, not to hand out before students draft.

TASK CARD — CENTRE-TANK FUEL BOOST PUMP CHANGE

1. Open the illustrated parts catalogue at figure 28-22-01. — verb-first; names the IPC and the figure.

2. Find the pump at item number 20. Read the part number. — item number then part number = correct identify sequence.

3. Check the effectivity for the tail number. — confirms the part fits this aircraft.

4. Disconnect the electrical connector. Fit a blanking cap. — two short imperatives; protect the open plug at once.

5. Remove the mounting bolts. Keep each fastener in the tray. — plural "bolts" fits a pump; parts control.

6. Lower the component with two people. — safety: a heavy unit is a two-person lift.

7. Mark the old pump unserviceable. Attach a warning tag. — tagging before it leaves the bay.

8. Install the new pump on the mount. — active "Install", article kept.

9. Torque the mounting bolts to the value. Fit lockwire. — torque to the specified value, then lock.

10. Add a torque seal. Reconnect the electrical connector. — visible torque evidence; reconnect.

11. Run a functional test. Sign the task card. — test before sign-off, the correct closing pair.

What earns full marks / what to correct

Marking pointLook for
Verb-first every lineEach step begins with an action verb (Open, Find, Check, Disconnect, Remove, Lower, Mark, Install, Torque, Add, Run, Sign). Any "The pump is to be…" loses the point.
Active voice, no modalsNo "must be", "should be", "it is necessary to". Recast these — the top Latvian/Russian error.
Article kept"Fit the blanking cap", not "Fit blanking cap".
One instruction per line, shortEach line ≤ ~15 words; split long steps into two imperatives.
Correct sequence & closingIdentify → remove → tag → install → torque → lock/seal → test → sign. Ends with the functional test and the signed card.
Word count100–130 words. The model is ~110.
