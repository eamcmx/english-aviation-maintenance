Warm-up · books closed

Could you sign a tech log from this?

"Something feels wrong with the brakes."

No symptom you can observe · no location · no condition. A feeling is not a defect. The mechanic's job today: question the report until those three are clear.

§1 · Listening · Dialogue 1 (track 1)

From a vague snag to a clear entry

A: It only says "something feels wrong with the brakes." That is not a clear symptom.

B: We cannot write that. We need the real symptom and the location.

A: The pilot says the pedal feels soft during a normal landing.

A: "The seal has failed" is only an assumption, not an observation.

Listen 1 (closed): what three things must the entry have?

Listen 2 (open): underline the symptom, the location, the condition.

Final entry → The left brake pedal has felt soft during landing.

§1 · Interactive · fact or assumption?

Record the observation — never the guess

An observation is what you saw or measured. An assumption is a guess about the cause.

"The line shows chafing" — observation. "The seal has failed" — assumption. Only observations belong in the entry.

§1 · Listening · Dialogue 2 (track 2)

Three findings — each with an exact location

A: I have found a leak at the brake line union, near the wheel.

A: The line shows chafing. It has rubbed against a bracket.

A: The bearing has developed play. There is clear wear on the race.

Catch the three findings and the location of each.

  • leak → brake line union
  • chafing → line against the bracket
  • play → wheel bearing / race
§2 · Vocabulary · defect description

Learn the term inside its word partner

leak — fluid escaping where it should not. find a leak · a hydraulic leak

chafing — rubbing damage where two parts touch. chafing on a line

intermittent — a fault that comes and goes. an intermittent fault

Supporting field: crack · corrosion · play · wear.

Say the precise term, not "broken". "The line shows chafing" beats "the line is broken".

§2 · Interactive · two quick rounds

Meaning → term, then term → sentence

Round 1: teacher says the plain meaning, class calls the term. Round 2: teacher says the term, class builds a full sentence with its word partner. Toggle the three parts of a good entry and watch the ring complete.

§3 · Grammar · the live defect

Present perfect — the defect is still there now

has / have + past participle. The action is past; the result is true now.

"The panel has developed cracks." The cracks are still there → present perfect. A closed job ("I replaced the seal") → past simple.

§3 · Grammar · the form

Form · negative · question · for / since

PurposeFormExample
Positivehas / have + participleThe panel has developed cracks.
Negativehas / have not + participleThe technician has not rectified the leak.
QuestionHas / Have + subject + participleHave you found the leak yet?
for / since+ length / + start pointleaked for two flights · since yesterday

After has / have always use the participle: developed, stored, found, leaked — never the base verb.

§1 · Listening · Dialogue 3 (track 3) · avionics

An intermittent fault — read the BITE

A: The display goes blank in flight. The fault is intermittent.

B: Let us run the BITE. The self-test may have stored a fault code.

A: The BITE shows a fault on the data bus, logged during the last flight.

A: "The unit has failed" is still an assumption at this stage.

Intermittent = comes and goes → cannot be seen on the ground.

The BITE code is an observation. The cause (loose plug, chafing on the loom) is still to be inspected — not assumed.

§4 · Writing habit · Simplified Technical English

One idea per line · exact · no extra words

"There seems to be some kind of soft feeling in the brakes that might possibly be a seal problem."

Symptom: the left brake pedal has felt soft during landing.
Location: left main gear, at the brake line union.
Condition: during normal braking on the landing roll.

STE = one idea per sentence, an approved verb, no filler.

The reader is another mechanic or an inspector, months later, under pressure. Short, exact, honest.

§6 · Speaking · four role-play cards (14 min)

Pairs · swap roles after each card

1 · Question the pilot. Drill "the brakes feel wrong" → symptom, location, condition.

2 · Describe your findings. Leak, chafing, play — each with its exact location.

3 · Fact or assumption? Stop "the seal has failed"; explain observation vs guess.

4 · Intermittent avionics. Symptom → run the BITE → why it is unserviceable.

1 min set-up · 3 min per card · swap roles · spotlight one pair.

Insist on the present perfect for findings: "I have found a leak at the union."

§7 · Writing · a tech-log defect entry (8 min + homework)

Write the entry — symptom, location, condition

Brief: number two engine oil pressure "looks low sometimes". You find a small oil leak at a pipe union, with light chafing on the pipe.

Write 5–7 lines. Present perfect for the live fault. Separate observation from assumption. Mark it unserviceable. Simplified Technical English.

Self-check before submit:

  • symptom · location · condition named
  • present perfect used
  • observation kept apart from assumption
  • short, legible, marked unserviceable
