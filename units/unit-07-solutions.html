E4· Grammar transformation — make it a polite request

Begin with Could you and use the base verb. (Would you is also accepted.)

#AnswerWhy
1Could you send me two filters?Modal + base verb; no order, no "to".
2Could you confirm the Form 1?Base verb "confirm" after the modal.
3Could you check the interchangeability?Softens a technical check into a request.
4Could you give me the part number?Modal first, then subject "you".
5Could you raise a new requisition?Base verb "raise"; polite, cooperative.
6Could you tell me the lead time?Asks for information politely.
7Could you offer an alternative?The key negotiating request in a backorder.
8Could you check the shortage list?Base verb "check"; no "to".
9Could you send it to the line?Delivery request in cooperative form.

E6· Error-spotting — fix eight errors

Write the corrected line.

#CorrectionWhy
1Could you check the stock?No to after a modal — use the base verb.
2Could you send me two filters?A request, not a bare order; word order fixed.
3Could you give me the part number?Modal first — not the statement "You could…".
4I need a quantity of two.Add the article a before "quantity".
5The filter is unserviceable.Adjective takes no plural -s.
6Could you confirm the Form 1?No -s on the verb after a modal.
7Would you send it today?No to after would.
8Could you raise a requisition?Use a real request, not "Please you…".

Writing· model answer — a parts request note (annotated)

Target: 80–110 words, Simplified Technical English, part number + quantity + reason, a polite request each time.

PARTS REQUEST — A320 LINE — AOG To: stores. From: line technician. Work order: 4471. The aircraft is AOG, because one hydraulic filter is unserviceable after a leak. Part number: 26114501. Quantity: two — one to fit and one spare. Could you check the stock, please? Would you send both filters to the A320 line? Each filter must carry a serviceable tag and a valid Form 1, so could you confirm the certification status? If the part is on backorder, could you offer an alternative, provided the interchangeability is confirmed in the IPC? I will raise the requisition now.
Why this scores well:
  • Structure: gives part number, quantity and reason before any request — the three items stores needs first.
  • Grammar focus: every request uses could/would + base verb (check, send, confirm, offer) — no "to", no "-s", no bare order.
  • Simplified Technical English: short sentences, one idea per line, active voice, terms used the same way throughout.
  • Certification thinking: asks for the serviceable tag, the Form 1 and the certification status — the papers that make a part legal to fit.
  • Contingency: plans for a backorder and conditions the alternative on confirmed interchangeability (provided…).
  • Register: cooperative, not commanding — right for a stores request.
Mark down for: bare orders ("Send two filters."), a missing reason or quantity, "Could you to…", "Could you confirms…", or accepting an alternative without checking its interchangeability and papers.
