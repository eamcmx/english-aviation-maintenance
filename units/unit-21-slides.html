Coordinating a functional test

Brief the steps and the safety precautions · call and confirm each action · state and read the pass/fail result. The grammar that carries the safety: the imperative — "Do not stand near the flaps."

By the end, a student can …

  • Brief a partner on a test's steps and safety precautions.
  • Call and confirm test actions clearly while the test runs.
  • State and interpret the pass/fail criteria.

The register is short, direct, unambiguous — like the manual it comes from.

01 · Scenario The situation

Late shift, Hangar 2. Aircraft D-AXLR is on jacks for a flap system check. The task card in the AMM asks for a flap functional test — with two people.

One reads the manual and calls each action. The other stands clear, watches, and confirms. At the end, you compare the result with the pass criteria: pass or fail.

Why the language matters

The flaps move fast under hydraulic pressure. A single unclear callout can send a person into a hazard — so the words are part of the safety, not extra.

02 · Listen Track 1 — The pre-test brief

A We run the flap functional test now, so I will brief you before we begin.

B Which AMM task card covers this operational test?

A Both flaps must reach full deflection together, with no visible asymmetry.

B Do not stand near the flaps during travel.

Play it twice

1st (books closed): What must B fit, and where must B stand?

2nd (books open): Find the pass criteria and two safety warnings.

Function today: briefing — steps + precautions before you touch the lever.

02 · Listen Track 2 — Running the test (call & confirm)

A Flaps to five. Actuating now. Confirm the left flap begins to move.

B Left flap moving smoothly. The deflection looks even, with no asymmetry yet.

A Flaps to fifteen. Watch the limit switch indication as it travels.

B The limit switch made at fifteen, and the indication light is green.

Listen for numbers

Which three flap settings are called? (five · fifteen · full)

At what setting does the limit switch make? (fifteen)

Pattern: A calls the command → B confirms what is seen.

02 · Listen Track 3 — Reading the result (pass or fail?)

B Actuating. Wait — the left flap is lagging behind the right one.

A Stop the test and select flaps up. Confirm that you can see asymmetry.

B The AMM allows no visible asymmetry, so this result is definitely a fail.

A Do not sign it serviceable, because we must raise a snag first.

Decision language

State the criteria → decide → agree the next step.

What does the BITE readout show? (a fault on the left actuation channel)

Fail → snag in the tech log · lockout stays · placard on the lever.

02 · Listen Track 4 — Shift-handover voicemail

M Here is a quick handover on the flap functional test.

M We ran the test on aircraft D-A-X-L-R. The result is a fail. At full deflection, the left flap lagged the right.

M I raised a snag in the tech log, number four four seven one. The lockout tag is still fitted. Please do not pressurise the system.

Note-taking grid

Aircraft · result · cause · snag number · safety state.

Catch the number: 4471. This models the Card 4 handover you produce later.

Idiom to flag

"The limit switch made." Here make = closed / completed the circuit. It is not "made" as in built.

60-sec pair race: A says the noun, B adds the verb.

04 · Grammar The imperative — order & safety warning

Order: base verb, no subject → Fit the lockout. Keep clearance.

Warning: Do not + base verb → Do not stand near the flaps.

Say the warning first, then the order. Short = safe, because the flap moves fast.

04 · Grammar Three L1 traps (Latvian · Russian)

✗ Not stand near the flaps.
✓ Do not stand near the flaps.

L1 negates with one particle (nestāviet / не стойте) → learners drop "do".

✗ No touch the actuator.
✓ Do not touch the actuator.

"No + verb" transfers nē / нет as a blanket negator.

✗ You stand clear now!
✓ Stand clear now!

English imperative drops the subject; L1 keeps/implies the pronoun.

The fix

Rehearse aloud until automatic. Under pressure the L1 form returns — so drill the callouts, don't just explain them.

05 · Read the result Pass criteria vs asymmetry

Criteria: both flaps at full deflection together, with no visible asymmetry.

Drag the left flap's lag. Read the verdict, then say it in one sentence.

Interpretation: if the AMM allowed a tolerance, a tiny lag could still pass — the criteria decide, not the eye.

05 · Call & confirm The actuation sequence

Press the calls in order. One student reads the CALL; the partner gives the CONFIRM from memory. Then swap.

Confirm = observe + state: "Left flap moving." · "Limit switch made." · "No asymmetry."

07 · Speaking Four role-play cards

Card 1 · Lead

Brief the steps + precautions. "We follow task card 27-51-00." "Do not stand near the flaps."

Card 2 · Partner

Confirm each call. "I am clear." "Limit switch made." "No asymmetry."

Card 3 · Both

Read pass/fail. "That fails the pass criteria." "We raise a snag."

Card 4 · Phone

Hand over the shift. "The result is a fail." "Do not pressurise the system."

Model Card 1 first · swap roles · circulate with the B2 rubric (teacher key) · delayed correction at the end. Extra reps: the three AI-partner prompts on the page.

08 · Writing A snag entry in Simplified Technical English

STE habits

  • Short sentences — about 18 words or fewer.
  • One instruction per sentence.
  • An approved plain word for each idea.
  • Imperative for the warnings: Do not pressurise the system.

Four-line plan

1 test + result · 2 what you saw · 3 safety action · 4 next step.

Model input: E7. Annotated model answer: gated solutions file. Run the self-check before hand-in.

Wrap-up · exit ticket

Brief · call & confirm · read the result

The imperative carries the safety: order = base verb; warning = "Do not" + base verb; warning first. Pass/fail is the criteria's call, not the eye's — no visible asymmetry → raise a snag.
