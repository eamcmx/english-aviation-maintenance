2 · Latvian & Russian interference — why each error happens

Error 1 — present tense with a past time word.
✗ "Yesterday we replace the valve." → ✓ "…replaced…"
Why. Latvian and Russian mark past time mainly through the verb's aspect and through the time adverb, and the verb form itself often looks unchanged to a beginner's ear. Once the time word ("yesterday") is present, students feel the pastness is already signalled and leave the English verb in its bare form. Fix: drill "time word + past verb" as a fixed pair; the English verb must change too.
Error 2 — double past after did / did not.
✗ "We did not replaced the unit." → ✓ "…did not replace…"
Why. Once students learn -ed means past, they over-apply it and mark the past twice — on the auxiliary and on the main verb. Russian/Latvian have no equivalent "do"-auxiliary, so the idea that did alone carries the tense is new. Fix: "did already did the past work" — after did/did not, the main verb goes back to base form.
Error 3 — dropped article.
✗ "We replaced valve yesterday." → ✓ "…the valve…"
Why. Neither Latvian nor Russian has articles, so a/the have no first-language anchor and are simply omitted. The error is strongest before a known, specific part ("the connector", "the LRU"). Fix: teach the rule "known part → the"; mark every missing article in the writing task.
Pronunciation. The -ed ending has three sounds — /t/ after voiceless (passed, swapped, checked), /d/ after voiced (cleaned, repaired, logged), and /ɪd/ only after t/d (fitted, recorded, inspected). Latvian/Russian speakers tend to add an extra vowel to every -ed; drill that only the /ɪd/ group gets the extra syllable. Also watch: LRU spelled letter by letter (el-ar-YOO), serial = SEER-ee-al, and the th in "the/this".

3 · Extension activities (fast finishers / a second lesson)

  • Live call, no script. Pairs run the full call end to end (report → history → recommendation → follow-up) with books closed. Timed: 3 minutes each way.
  • Rewrite in STE. Give a wordy, real-sounding tech-log entry and have students shorten every sentence to one idea, in the past simple.
  • Fault-code dictation. Read part numbers and serial numbers aloud at speed; students write them down and read them back — trains the spoken-figures skill from the listening quiz.
  • Escalate or not? Give five short fault scenarios; students decide "workaround", "follow the recommendation", or "escalate", and justify in one sentence.
  • Document hunt. Match six phrases ("raise…", "note… against the task", "inspect the loom against…") to the correct document: work order, tech log, service bulletin, wiring diagram.
  • AI role-play. Solo learners use the three chat prompts on the body page to rehearse the call and get gentle recasts.

4 · CEFR B2 speaking rubric — the role-plays

Use while monitoring the four role-play cards. Five criteria, three bands each. B2 is the target band (middle).

CriterionBelow B2 (developing)At B2 (target)Above B2 (strong)
RangeRelies on a few memorised phrases; gaps force silence or first-language words.Uses the unit's specialist terms (recurring fault, workaround, escalate) and enough general language to describe the fault and history without long searching.Flexible use of the field; reformulates smoothly when the exact word is missing.
AccuracyPast simple unstable; articles dropped; base verb after did not wrong; errors block meaning.Past simple controlled across the history; articles mostly present; occasional slips that do not block meaning.Consistent control; self-corrects the interference errors before they matter.
FluencyFrequent long pauses; speech breaks down under the pressure of the call.Keeps going at a steady pace; pauses to plan but does not stall the conversation.Even, effortless flow even when reporting a long troubleshooting chain.
InteractionCannot take turns; misses the desk's questions; no read-back.Answers the desk's questions, asks for the workaround, and reads the recommendation back to confirm.Manages the whole exchange; checks, confirms and repairs misunderstanding naturally.
CoherenceSteps out of order; listener cannot follow the history.Reports the history in a clear chronological order with first / then / after that; the follow-up is logically sequenced.Well-linked throughout with a range of connectors (although, unless, as soon as, in case).

Quick score: 1 = below, 2 = at, 3 = above, per criterion (max 15). A learner "meets the outcome" at ≥ 2 on Interaction and Accuracy together.
