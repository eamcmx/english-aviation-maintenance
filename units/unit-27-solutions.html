E1 Gap-fill: use a bank word

  1. slot — the fixed time the aircraft must be ready is the release slot.
  2. manpower — manpower is the number of workers available for the job.
  3. critical path — the chain that sets the earliest finish is the critical path.
  4. bottleneck — the one slow step that restricts everything is the bottleneck.
  5. lead time — the wait before a part or task is ready is its lead time.
  6. turnaround — arrival to release of the aircraft is the turnaround.
  7. slippage — time fallen behind the plan is slippage.
  8. milestone — a decisive checkpoint of progress is a milestone.
  9. blocker — anything that stops a task moving is a blocker.
  10. buffer — reserve time built in for problems is a buffer.

E3 Complete the meeting exchange

  1. critical path — a task that determines the plan sits on the critical path.
  2. priority — the most important job is given the highest priority.
  3. bottleneck — the failed inspection is the slow step, the bottleneck.
  4. manpower — extra people from Bay 3 add manpower.
  5. buffer — a job that can wait has a buffer.
  6. spares — the replacement parts in the store are the spares.
  7. milestone — the certified test is the next milestone.
  8. slot — the fixed 22:00 release time is the slot.
  9. handover — the plan is documented in the handover notes.

E4 Grammar: choose the future form

  1. will finish / 'll finish — a promise made now takes will + base verb.
  2. am going to / 'm going to — a plan decided before now takes be going to.
  3. are commencing / 're commencing — a fixed arrangement with a time takes the present continuous.
  4. is — a fixed timetable takes the present simple.
  5. will verify / 'll verify — a promise made now takes will.
  6. is going to — an established plan takes be going to.
  7. is working — an arrangement already on the board takes the present continuous (present simple works is also acceptable as a rota).
  8. requires — a fixed fact from the manual takes the present simple.
  9. will protect / 'll protect — a commitment made now takes will.

E5 Reading 1: the daily production plan

  1. 22:00 — the plan header states the release slot is 22:00.
  2. the avionics modification (Task 1) — Task 1 has the failed BITE test, named as the bottleneck.
  3. 4 hours — Task 2 lists a lead time of four hours.
  4. No — the spares line says all spares are available, no blocker.
  5. the rigging (Task 2) — Task 2 keeps priority; rushing a control cable is never permitted.
  6. 21:30 — the handover line gives a target release to service of 21:30.

E6 Error-spotting: fix the handover note

  1. will do / 'll do — “I promise” = a promise, so use will, not the present tense.
  2. are starting / 're starting — “It is arranged” with a time = a fixed arrangement, so present continuous.
  3. is going to run — “the plan is established” = a decided plan; and never will to.
  4. is — “the schedule is fixed” = a timetable, so present simple.
  5. will protect / 'll protect — “you have my word” = a promise; are protect is not a valid form.
  6. is working / works — it appears on the board = an arrangement or a rota, not the bare verb.
  7. am going to reassign / 'm going to reassign — “I already decided” = a decided plan, so be going to.
  8. will check / 'll check — “I give you my word now” = a promise made now, so will.

E7 Reading 2: an MEL deferral and planning note

  1. No — the note says the cabin light is not on the critical path.
  2. defer it (as category C) — under the MEL the item is deferred.
  3. the avionics modification and the rigging — those two activities determine the release to service.
  4. No — the note states the deferral does not consume manpower today.
  5. the tech log — the deferral is closed on the tech log.
  6. at the next scheduled check — the replacement light goes in at the next check.

E8 Listening review: Dialogue 3

  1. 18:00 — the test must be complete by 18:00.
  2. Toms — the mechanic is assigned to the rigging with Toms.
  3. 18:15 — they commence the rigging at 18:15, right after the test passes.
  4. Anna — Anna is going to manage the duct activity.
  5. the shift lead (A) — speaker A verifies the spares before the shift concludes.
  6. the release to service — the final remaining milestone is release to service at 22:00.
  7. 21:30 — they transfer the aircraft by 21:30, provided the rigging is complete.
  8. the planner — if it slips, they escalate to the planner immediately.

Writing task · annotated model answer

The task: 6–8 short lines for the night shift in Simplified Technical English, including the slot, the mod status, the rigging plan, the spares, and one future-form commitment.

SHIFT HANDOVER, YL-BBX, Bay 2 The release slot is 22:00 tonight. The aircraft is on the critical path. The avionics modification is complete. The BITE test has passed. The flight-control rigging is in progress. Two mechanics are on it. The spares are available in the store. There is no blocker. The duct activity has a buffer. It is a lower priority. We will transfer the aircraft by 21:30. I will report each milestone. If the rigging slips, escalate to the planner immediately. Please keep accurate handover notes for the night shift.

Why this answer scores well

Content complete. It states the slot (22:00), the mod status (complete, test passed), the rigging plan (in progress, two mechanics), and the spares (available, no blocker).
Future forms used correctly. “We will transfer… by 21:30” and “I will report” are commitments made to the next shift — will is the right choice.
Present simple for the timetable. “The slot is 22:00” — a fixed schedule, so present simple, not will be.
Simplified Technical English. One idea per line, short active sentences, facts not opinion, and the blocker is named (“There is no blocker”). The conditional line uses a clear if for the escalation rule.
Accept variation. “We are transferring the aircraft by 21:30” is also correct if the learner frames it as a fixed arrangement. Reward any answer that hits the content checklist with at least one correct future form.
