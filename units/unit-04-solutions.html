E1Gap-fill — one bank term per gap

#AnswerWhy
1work orderThe document that authorises work — the legal permission to begin. No activity starts without it.
2task cardEach is a single job with its own steps and sign-off box; "its own" signals the countable card.
3IPCPart numbers come from the Illustrated Parts Catalogue, the controlled source — never the old label.
4tech logEvery fault ("fault") is recorded there immediately; the tech log holds defects and completed work.
5safety cordonThe barrier set up "before we lift" — it excludes people from the working area.
6wheel chockSet on "each tyre" to stop the aircraft rolling; you never jack until it is fitted.
7FODA loose item on the floor is foreign object debris — it can damage an engine or tyre.
8torqueThe specified tightness value, read "from the AMM" and recorded before closing.
9lockout"As soon as … up, we fit the … pins" — lockout pins stop a jack lowering. One word.
10access panelThe removable cover you "open … to reach the bracket behind it".

E2Matching — term to meaning

#AnswerWhy
1AMM"The official how-to book" = Aircraft Maintenance Manual — the instructions, not the parts list.
2IPC"Shows parts and part numbers" — that is the Illustrated Parts Catalogue, not the AMM.
3work pack"The full set of paperwork for one job" — the pack contains the work order and every task card.
4sign-offThe certifying signature that says the job is complete and correct.
5defectA fault or damage found on the aircraft — distinct from FOD (loose debris).
6handoverPassing information from one shift to the next — the voicemail in Dialogue 4.
7wheel chock"A block that stops the aircraft rolling" — placed against a tyre.
8base check"A large, planned inspection in the hangar" — the whole scenario of this unit.
9warning noticeA displayed sign that warns people not to enter or operate.
10jack point"The fixed spot where a jack pad must sit" beneath the wing or nose.

#AnswerWhy
1FirstStep one of the plan. Use "First," not "At first" (which means "in the beginning, later changed").
2ThenThe following step after checking the work order — the natural next sequencer.
3handover"The last …" recorded in the tech log — the note from the previous shift.

E4Grammar transformation — correct sequencer form

#AnswerWhy
1FirstStep one takes "First,", not "At first" — a frequent Latvian/Russian carry-over error.
2removingAfter "after", use the -ing form: after removing. There is no gerund in the L1s.
3jackingAfter "before", use the -ing form: before jacking.
4theEnglish needs the article: jack the aircraft. Latvian and Russian have no articles.
5asThe full connector is "as soon as", not "as soon". Three words.
6removeAfter "we", the plain present is we remove — not the -ing form here.
7sign"we" takes the base form: we sign, not "we signs".
8is"Once the card is complete" — the linking verb cannot be dropped.

WModel writing answer — annotated

A tech-log defect entry in Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100): short sentences, one idea each, approved unambiguous words, and sequencers for the order of actions. 79 words.

MODEL — DEFECT ENTRY (Simplified Technical English)

Defect: There is a crack on a bracket behind access panel 191AB. The length is about 12 mm. The defect is not in the work pack.

Action: First, we stopped the task. Then we measured the crack. After that, we recorded the defect in the tech log. The AMM gives no crack limit here. We told the supervisor. We raised a new work order. The panel stays open. Sign-off is held until the repair is done and inspected.

Why this scores well

Short sentences, one idea each. Every sentence gives a single fact or action — the core STE rule. Long compound sentences are broken up ("We told the supervisor. We raised a new work order.").
Sequencers show the order. First … Then … After that … put the actions in the correct, safe order — the grammar focus of the unit, used correctly (not "At first").
Complete content. It states what (a crack), where (behind panel 191AB), the size (about 12 mm), what was done, and why the sign-off is held — the five required points.
Approved, unambiguous words. It reuses the controlled bank terms — tech log, work order, sign-off, AMM — and avoids vague synonyms. "There is a crack" is preferred to a nominalised, ambiguous phrase.
Grammar accuracy. Articles are present (the task, the crack, a new work order) and the present simple is used for procedure — the two error types Latvian and Russian speakers must watch.
