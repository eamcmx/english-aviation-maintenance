2 · First-language interference (Latvian / Russian)

Latvian and Russian share features that push learners toward the same three future-form errors. Knowing why lets you recast quickly and without a grammar lecture.

I do it tomorrow.  /  Tomorrow I call you.
I will do it tomorrow.  /  Tomorrow I will call you.
Why: Neither Latvian nor Russian has a separate “will” auxiliary. Russian marks the future with a perfective verb form (сделаю = “I-will-do”), and Latvian uses a simple future ending (darīšu); to a learner it feels like one word, so they reach for the bare English present. Recast by stressing the promise: “It is a promise, so — I will do it.”
We will to finish the rigging.  /  They will to check it.
We will finish the rigging.  /  They will check it.
Why: In the learner's mind “will” is glossed as “going to / intend to”, both of which take an infinitive with to in the English they already know. They over-generalise and insert to. Fix by drilling the pattern out loud: will + bare verb, no to — “will finish, will check, will protect.”
Tomorrow I will meet the planner at 9:00. (already arranged)
Tomorrow I am meeting the planner at 9:00.
Why: Both first languages use one future form for every kind of future, so the English split between a spontaneous promise (will) and a pre-arranged, timetabled meeting (present continuous) has no equivalent to transfer from. Learners default to will for everything. Recast by asking “Is the time already fixed with the other person?” If yes → present continuous.

3 · Extension activities

  • Escalation chain (fast finishers). Give a pair a slippage of three hours. They must telephone the planner and, in four turns, state the slippage, name the blocker, propose a fix, and agree an escalation deadline — all in target future forms.
  • Genre swap. Learners rewrite Dialogue 1's outcome as a one-line entry each for a work order, a task card sign-off, and a tech log line. Tests register transfer across authentic documents.
  • Devil's-advocate planner. Stronger learner plays a planner who refuses every request once. The partner must produce a second, different justification (a new because clause). Builds the “justify the trade-off” outcome under pressure.
  • Numbers dictation. Read eight mixed times and quantities (18:00, 21:30, 600 flight hours, two mechanics…) in natural speech; learners write the figure. Recycles the listening-for-figures skill the quiz assesses.
  • STE tightening. Project a wordy handover note and have the class cut it to one idea per line in the active voice — the core Simplified Technical English habit.
  • AI partner homework. Learners run one of the body-page conversation-partner prompts and paste three lines the partner corrected, plus the correction. Turns self-study into evidence of noticing.

4 · CEFR B2 speaking rubric — the role-plays

Use for the paired negotiation (Cards 1–4). Score each learner on five dimensions; band 5 = strong B2 (approaching C1), band 3 = solid B2, band 1 = below B2 (B1). Total /25.

Dimension5 — strong B23 — solid B21 — below B2
Range Uses the unit's specialist terms and varied future forms accurately and naturally. Uses the key terms and future forms; some repetition or safe choices. Very limited vocabulary; avoids the target forms.
Accuracy Future forms consistently correct; errors are rare and do not distract. Future forms mostly correct; the odd L1 slip (present-for-future, extra to). Frequent form errors that obscure the commitment.
Fluency Speaks at a natural pace with only occasional hesitation. Keeps going with some pausing to plan. Frequent long pauses; delivery breaks down.
Interaction Initiates, pushes back, concedes and agrees smoothly; genuine negotiation. Responds and agrees; negotiation moves are present but prompted. Mostly waits to be asked; little give-and-take.
Coherence Status is a clear three-part structure; reasons linked with because/so/unless. Status is clear; connectors used but sometimes basic. Ideas listed without linking; hard to follow the plan.
Minimum pass evidence: a three-sentence status (task → progress → blocker), one justification with because, and one correct future-form commitment. A learner who produces all three is at least band 3 on Coherence and Interaction.

5 · Quiz key with per-item rationale

Listening 8Reading 5Use of English 8Vocabulary 4 — 25 items, five question types, no Cloze. Answers below refer to the item names in quiz-rich.xml (category CF/U27-r1).

Listening

L1 The BITE test on the avionics mod failed.
Rationale: the wiring is complete but the BITE test failed, so the task card cannot be signed — that is the blocker. The cable set is in the store (not late), and the Bay 3 job simply has a buffer.
L2 True.
Rationale: the planner says “I am going to reassign two mechanics from Bay 3 onto the avionics modification.” A decided plan in be going to.
L3 2 (numerical)
Rationale: “we are approximately two hours behind” — two hours of slippage.
L4 1800 (numerical)
Rationale: “we will complete the test by 18:00.” 22:00 is the release slot, a distractor.
L5 Rushing a control cable is never safe.
Rationale: the rigging keeps priority on a safety ground, not because of lead time or crew size.
L6 4 (numerical)
Rationale: “the cable set requires four hours to rig and tension.” Two hours is the slippage, a distractor.
L7 False.
Rationale: the spares are already in the store, so the runner is unnecessary and is reassigned to the duct activity.
L8 1815 (numerical)
Rationale: “we are commencing the rigging at 18:15.” 18:00 is the test deadline, a near-miss distractor.

Reading

R1 A mechanic and an inspector.
Rationale: the work-order sign-off line names one B1 mechanic and one inspector. The store only issues spares; the planner is not on the sign-off.
R2 False.
Rationale: the Service Bulletin is “recommended, not mandatory” with a 600-flight-hour window — not a today job.
R3 Within ten days.
Rationale: MEL Category C allows rectification within 10 days; the item is deferred and placarded, so the aircraft may fly.
R4 False.
Rationale: the BITE readout shows PASS with no faults stored — nothing blocks the release.
R5 Every 200 flight hours.
Rationale: the AD sets a repetitive 200-flight-hour interval and is mandatory; it is not a one-off or landing-triggered task.

Use of English

U1 will finish / 'll finish
Rationale: a promise made now = will + base verb.
U2 am going to reassign / 'm going to reassign
Rationale: a plan decided before now = be going to.
U3 are starting / 're starting
Rationale: a fixed arrangement with a clock time = present continuous.
U4 is
Rationale: a timetable you do not control = present simple.
U5 will protect / 'll protect
Rationale: correct the L1 error — drop to after will.
U6 bottleneck
Rationale: the single slow step that limits the operation.
U7 buffer
Rationale: reserve time deliberately built into the plan.
U8 critical path
Rationale: the chain of tasks that sets the earliest finish.

Vocabulary

V1 slot → fixed ready time · manpower → the workers you have · slippage → time fallen behind · milestone → a key checkpoint · handover → the pass to the next team.
Rationale: matching term to plain meaning across the field.
V2 protect the slot · clear a blocker · raise a work order · sign off the task card · hit a milestone.
Rationale: the standard word partners (collocations) from the bank.
V3 protect the slot.
Rationale: tension goes with a cable, embody with a Service Bulletin, placard with a deferred defect.
V4 AOG.
Rationale: aircraft on ground — cannot fly until rectified. The others name time (lead time, turnaround) or a document (work order).
Anti-bias design: in every multiple-choice item the correct option is not the longest and carries no extra hedging; distractors are plausible and length-balanced; there is no “all of the above”. Every item has general feedback, and every option has its own feedback for review.
